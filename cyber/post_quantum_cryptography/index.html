    Preface¶

    The quantum computing era is approaching. While cryptographically-relevant quantum computers (CRQCs) capable of breaking today’s public-key cryptography do not yet exist, their eventual arrival is now widely considered a matter of when, not if. The consequences are severe: every public-key cryptosystem in use today — RSA, Diffie-Hellman, ECDH, ECDSA — would be broken by Shor’s algorithm running on a CRQC.

    Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) is the field developing new cryptographic algorithms that resist both classical and quantum attacks. After an eight-year standardization process, NIST published the first three PQC standards in August 2024 (FIPS 203, 204, 205), and selected a fifth algorithm (HQC) in March 2025. The migration from classical to quantum-safe cryptography is now an organizational imperative.

    This book provides a thorough technical treatment of PQC, covering:

    • Mathematical foundations: Lattice problems, learning with errors, code-based cryptography.

    • Algorithm families: Lattice-based, code-based, hash-based, multivariate, isogeny-based.

    • NIST standards: FIPS 203/204/205 and upcoming algorithms.

    • Implementation: Software libraries (liboqs, PQClean), hardware (NTT ASIC, GPU), embedded systems.

    • Migration strategy: Cryptographic inventory, hybrid deployment, PKI transition.

    • Side-channel attacks: Power analysis, timing attacks, fault injection, and defenses.

    • Applications: TLS, messaging, PKI, code signing, blockchain.

    • National solutions: NIST, NSA, German BSI, French ANSSI, Korean KPQC, Chinese SM, Russian GOST.

    The “Harvest Now, Decrypt Later” threat means action is required today, not after CRQCs arrive.
