    About this library¶

    The Open Book Project is a free reading room for books on computer science, quantum computing, cyber security, science and technology, university courses and the English language. Tap a cover to start reading where you left off; every book can be kept on your device for reading without a connection.

    The collections¶

    Science and Technology¶

    Reference books on computing, security and defence technology. 2 books by Nikhil Kumar Rajput.

    Books on the technology of computing, security and defence: how a program becomes electrons, how hardware is secured, how networks are watched, and how drones and missiles are built. Each is generated from the LaTeX source of its print edition, with the book's own figures, tables, code listings and boxes.

    Quantum Computing¶

    Field guides to quantum computing and quantum machine learning. 3 books by Nikhil Kumar Rajput.

    Plain-English field guides to quantum computing and to quantum machine learning, in the same pattern as the other guides of the library: each topic has an analogy, an example, the steps, a diagram and a table of the concepts, tools and standards to know.

    Computing and Networks¶

    Field guides to how computers, memory, networks and data centres work. 13 books by Nikhil Kumar Rajput.

    Student field guides to the machinery of computing: chip design, computer architecture, memory and storage, operating systems, databases, data centres, computer networks, mobile networks and machine learning. Every topic is explained in plain English with an everyday analogy, a worked example, step-by-step mechanics, a diagram and a table of the concepts, tools, formats and standards to know.

    Cyber Security¶

    Field guides to security tools, cryptography and roots of trust. 5 books by Nikhil Kumar Rajput.

    Field guides to defensive security: the tools and the cryptography that protect systems, and the hardware roots of trust, TPMs, secure boot and public-key infrastructure that make them trustworthy. Each topic follows the same plain-English pattern, with an analogy, an example, the steps, a diagram and a table of what to know.

    Courses¶

    University course books, lesson by lesson. 6 books by Nikhil Kumar Rajput.

    Course material for university papers, written as lessons: each lesson states its learning outcomes, develops the topic with worked examples and ends with a summary and practice problems.

    Language¶

    A book for learning English, in three editions. 3 books by Nikhil Kumar Rajput.

    A book for learners of English, with grammar, speaking, vocabulary and writing, and a short set of exercises, with answers, at the end of every chapter. It is published in three editions with the same chapters: the English edition, the Hinglish edition (Hindi in the Roman script, as the book was first written) and the Hindi edition (in Devanagari).

    Open Textbooks¶

    Freely licensed textbooks from Wikibooks, read in this library's format. 3 books by Jim Hefferon and Wikibooks contributors, Wikibooks contributors.

    Textbooks written in the open by the contributors of Wikibooks and published under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike licence. They are not stored in this library: the list of their chapters is kept here, and each chapter is fetched from Wikibooks when it is opened and shown in this library's reader, with numbered sections, figures, code and mathematics, and the attribution of its authors at the end.

    Science Classics¶

    Mathematics and natural science, from Euclid to Darwin. 7 books by Charles Darwin, Christiaan Huygens, translated by Silvanus P. Thompson, Edwin A. Abbott, Euclid, edited by John Casey, Michael Faraday, Silvanus P. Thompson.

    Classics of mathematics and natural science in the public domain: Euclid's Elements, Faraday's lectures on a candle, Huygens on light, Thompson's calculus, and Darwin and Abbott, which are read from the public repositories of their Standard Ebooks editions when a chapter is opened.

    Literature Classics¶

    English and American fiction, plays and essays. 22 books by Aesop, translated by V. S. Vernon Jones, Arthur Conan Doyle, Charles Dickens, Jane Austen, Lewis Carroll, Mark Twain, O. Henry, Oscar Wilde, Rudyard Kipling, William Shakespeare, William Strunk Jr..

    Novels, plays, stories and essays in the public domain, by Austen, Dickens, Shakespeare, Wilde, Twain, Kipling and others, with Strunk's Elements of Style. Most are Standard Ebooks editions, read from their public repositories when a chapter is opened.

    Philosophy and History Classics¶

    Philosophers, historians and political thinkers. 14 books by Aristotle, translated by F. H. Peters, Edward Gibbon, Epictetus, translated by George Long, Immanuel Kant, translated by J. M. D. Meiklejohn, John Stuart Mill, Laozi, translated by James Legge, Marcus Aurelius, translated by George Long, Niccolò Machiavelli, translated by W. K. Marriott, Plato, translated by Benjamin Jowett, René Descartes, translated by John Veitch, Sun Tzu, translated by Lionel Giles, Thomas More, translated by Ralph Robinson, Thucydides, translated by Richard Crawley.

    Works of philosophy, politics and history in the public domain, from Plato and Thucydides to Gibbon and Mill. Most are Standard Ebooks editions, read from their public repositories when a chapter is opened.

    How it was produced¶

    The web edition of the books that have a print edition is generated from its LaTeX sources. Nothing is retyped: when the sources change, the site is generated again from them. In the conversion:

    • each chapter becomes a page, and each book has a landing page with the preface and the list of chapters;
    • chapter, section, figure, table and appendix numbers are those of the print edition, and cross-references such as "Chapter 3" or "Table 4.2" link to their targets;
    • the boxes of the print edition (verses, key concepts, scholarly notes, safety notes and the others) appear as boxes with their titles; safety notes are always kept;
    • citations are rendered in author-date form and link to the bibliography page of the book, which lists every entry of the print bibliography;
    • diagrams drawn in TikZ are compiled with each book's own settings and shown as vector graphics (SVG);
    • mathematics is typeset with MathJax;
    • print-only material is left out: page numbers and running heads, ornaments, the printed contents lists and, in volume 13 of Bhāratīya Jñāna Paramparā, the printed index. Page numbers of the print edition are therefore not shown; please cite by chapter and section.

    The field guides, the course books and the English book were written as web pages or Markdown. They are converted to the same form: each chapter is one page, its sections are numbered, boxes keep their titles, code is shown with a language, mathematics is typeset with MathJax, diagrams are shown as vector graphics, and the exercises of the English book have their answers folded away until they are opened. They have no print edition, so their numbers are those of the web edition.

    Images¶

    The print editions contain photographs and other raster images. A photograph is reproduced in the web edition only if its licence has been confirmed for this use. Every other photograph is replaced by a grey note, "Photograph not reproduced in the web edition (licence not confirmed)", and its caption is kept so that the text still reads in full. The diagrams drawn in the books' own sources are included in full.

    Reading on this site¶

    The books are published for reading on this site only. A tap on a cover on the front page opens the book in a full-screen reader, at the place where you stopped or, for a book you have not read, at its preface or first chapter. The small i button on a cover opens the page of the book itself: its description, Start reading or Continue, Keep for offline reading and the full contents, with the chapters you have read marked.

    In the reader, turn pages by tapping or clicking the edges of the screen, swiping, or with the arrow keys, Page Up/Page Down and the space bar; at the end of a chapter the next turn continues with the next chapter. A tap in the middle shows the controls, which hide again while you read. The bottom of the screen shows the page in the chapter, how many pages are left in it and how far you are through the book; the slider in the controls, with a mark for each chapter, moves anywhere in the book. On a wide landscape screen two pages are shown side by side.

    • Aa sets the typeface (a serif, a book serif or a sans-serif face), the size of the type, the line spacing, the margins, justified or ragged text, and a white, sepia, dark or black page.
    • The contents panel lists the chapters and sections, with a Go to box; its other tabs list your bookmarks and your highlights and notes. Select a passage to highlight it or add a note; the bookmark button marks the page.
    • The search button searches the text of the book.
    • A tap on a figure shows it full screen, where it can be zoomed; wide tables and code open full screen too. Footnotes, references to figures, tables and sections, and citations open in a small window, from which you can go to them.
    • Keys: arrows and space turn pages, C contents, A settings, B bookmark, / search, + and - text size, ? all the keys, Esc closes.

    Your settings, bookmarks, highlights and notes and your place in each book are kept only in this browser, on this device.

    Reading as an app¶

    The Open Book Project can be installed as a reading app. On an iPad or iPhone, open the site in Safari, tap the Share button and choose Add to Home Screen; the books then open full screen from their own icon, without the browser's address bar. On a laptop, Chrome and Edge show an install button at the right of the address bar (or Install in the browser menu). On Android, Chrome offers Install app or Add to Home screen in its menu, and there is an Android app of its own. The page Get the app has the steps for each device.

    Pages you have opened are remembered for reading without a connection. To keep a whole book, use Keep for offline reading on the page of the book (the i button on its cover): every chapter, the front and back matter and the figures and photographs of the book are then stored in your browser, with a progress bar while this happens, and the book is marked Available offline. Remove from this device frees the space again. When a new edition of the site is published, a small notice offers to reload, and books you have kept are brought up to date the next time you are online. The front page shows the book you are reading under Continue reading, and on each cover you have started, how far you have read; a book kept on the device is marked on its cover. All of this is stored only in your browser.

    Adding a book¶

    Books are added to the library one repository at a time. If you have written a book, in Markdown, LaTeX or HTML, the page Publish your book shows how to start from the template, check the book and send it, and the Book Format describes the format in full.
