    Lesson 16: Review: Putting It All Together¶

    Repaso: todo junto

    You have reached the last lesson. In fifteen lessons you have learnt to pronounce and spell Spanish, to greet people and introduce yourself, to count and tell the time, to use nouns, articles and adjectives, to choose between ser, estar and hay, to talk about your family, your town, your food, your day and your tastes, to shop, to make plans and to tell what happened. This lesson brings it all together. It has no new grammar: instead, it shows how the pieces fit, collects the mistakes English speakers make most often, gives you model texts to imitate, and ends with a long mixed quiz. Use it to find your weak points, and go back to the lesson that covers each one.

    Learning outcomes

    By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

    • hold a simple conversation that moves between present, past and future;
    • choose the right verb form for what you want to say, using the overview of the verb system;
    • avoid the ten most common mistakes of English-speaking learners;
    • write a short text about yourself, your routine, your last holiday and your plans;
    • check your level against the A1 descriptors and decide what to study next.

    16.1 Dialogue: a farewell party¶

    It is the end of Daniel's course in Salamanca. His friends have organised a small party in a café on the main square. Señora Ruiz, his teacher, and Javier, his language exchange partner, are there too. Notice how the conversation moves from the present to the past and the future.

    • Lucía: Daniel, ¿cuándo te vas? — Daniel, when are you leaving?
    • Daniel: Me voy el domingo. Mi vuelo sale a las once de la mañana. — I'm leaving on Sunday. My flight leaves at eleven in the morning.
    • Señora Ruiz: ¿Y qué tal el curso? ¿Estás contento? — And how was the course? Are you pleased?
    • Daniel: Sí, muy contento. Aprendí muchísimo. Cuando llegué en septiembre, solo sabía decir hola y gracias. — Yes, very pleased. I learnt a huge amount. When I arrived in September, I could only say hello and thank you.
    • Javier: ¡Es verdad! El primer día hablamos casi todo el tiempo en inglés. — That's true! On the first day we spoke English almost the whole time.
    • Lucía: ¿Qué es lo que más te gustó de Salamanca? — What did you like most about Salamanca?
    • Daniel: Me encantó la Plaza Mayor por la noche. Y la gente es muy simpática. — I loved the Plaza Mayor at night. And the people are very friendly.
    • Señora Ruiz: ¿Y qué vas a hacer en Inglaterra? — And what are you going to do in England?
    • Daniel: Voy a buscar trabajo y voy a seguir estudiando español. Quiero hacer el examen DELE el año que viene. — I'm going to look for a job and I'm going to keep on studying Spanish. I want to take the DELE exam next year.
    • Lucía: Y en verano tienes que venir a México. Te voy a enseñar Guadalajara. — And in the summer you have to come to Mexico. I'm going to show you Guadalajara.
    • Daniel: ¡Me encantaría! ¿Cuánto cuesta un billete a México? — I'd love to! How much is a ticket to Mexico?
    • Lucía: Un boleto, Daniel, en México decimos boleto. — A "boleto", Daniel, in Mexico we say "boleto".
    • Javier: Bueno, ¡un brindis! Por Daniel y por su español. — Right, a toast! To Daniel and his Spanish.
    • Todos: ¡Salud! — Cheers!

    Find in the dialogue an example of each of these. The answers follow.

    1. A reflexive verb in the present (Lesson 11).
    2. Estar for a feeling (Lesson 5).
    3. Two regular preterites (Lesson 15).
    4. A sentence with ir a + infinitive (Lesson 14).
    5. Gustar or encantar in the past (Lessons 12 and 15).
    6. Tener que + infinitive (Lesson 10).
    Answers
    1. te vas, me voy (irse, to leave). 2. ¿Estás contento? 3. aprendí, llegué, hablamos. 4. Voy a buscar trabajo, Te voy a enseñar Guadalajara. 5. me encantó, te gustó. 6. tienes que venir.

    Two forms are new: sabía (I knew, I could) is the imperfect tense, mentioned in Lesson 15; me encantaría (I would love to) is the conditional. Both are taught at the next level, but you can learn these two as phrases now.

    16.3 Grammar review: the verb system at a glance¶

    Most of what you want to say at this level can be said with five tools. The table shows which tool to pick.

    You want to talk about Use Lesson Example
    what is true now, habits, routines the present 6, 10, 11 Trabajo en un banco. Me levanto a las siete.
    what someone or something is ser 5 Mi hermana es médica. Es alta.
    where something is, how someone feels estar 5 El banco está en la plaza. Estoy cansado.
    that something exists hay 4, 5 Hay un banco en la plaza.
    likes and dislikes gustar, encantar 12 Me gusta el cine. Me encantan los libros.
    obligation tener que + infinitive 10 Tengo que estudiar.
    ability, permission poder + infinitive 10 ¿Puedo pagar con tarjeta?
    wishes querer + infinitive 10 Quiero viajar a Perú.
    plans and predictions ir a + infinitive 14 Voy a viajar a Perú. Va a llover.
    completed past events the preterite 15 El año pasado viajé a Perú.

    Table 16.4. Which form for which meaning.

    The present in four families¶

    Person regular -ar e → ie o → ue reflexive
    yo hablo quiero puedo me levanto
    tú hablas quieres puedes te levantas
    él, ella, usted habla quiere puede se levanta
    nosotros hablamos queremos podemos nos levantamos
    vosotros habláis queréis podéis os levantáis
    ellos, ellas, ustedes hablan quieren pueden se levantan

    Table 16.5. The four kinds of present tense you know. Note that the stem changes leave nosotros and vosotros alone.

    Present, past and future of one verb¶

    The same verb, viajar (to travel), in the three time frames:

    • Normalmente viajo en tren. — I usually travel by train. (present: habit)
    • El mes pasado viajé en avión. — Last month I travelled by plane. (preterite: finished event)
    • El mes que viene voy a viajar en autobús. — Next month I'm going to travel by bus. (ir a: plan)

    Worked example: choosing the form

    How would you say "This weekend I'm going to visit my grandparents, because last weekend I couldn't"?

    1. "This weekend I'm going to visit": a plan, so ir a + infinitive: Este fin de semana voy a visitar a mis abuelos. Note the personal a before people.
    2. "because": porque, one word, no accent.
    3. "last weekend I couldn't": a finished event in the past, so the preterite of poder, which is irregular: el fin de semana pasado no pude.

    Result: Este fin de semana voy a visitar a mis abuelos, porque el fin de semana pasado no pude.

    16.4 Grammar review: nouns, adjectives, pronouns and ten classic mistakes¶

    The noun group¶

    Everything in the noun group agrees with the noun, in gender and number (Lessons 4, 7 and 13):

    Masculine Feminine
    singular este coche rojo es mío esta casa roja es mía
    plural estos coches rojos son míos estas casas rojas son mías

    Table 16.6. Agreement: the demonstrative, the adjective and the possessive all follow the noun. Mío, mía (mine) are the stressed possessives, which you may meet before you study them.

    The pronouns you know¶

    Subject Reflexive With gustar (indirect object) Direct object (it, them, him, her) After a preposition
    yo me me me mí (conmigo)
    tú te te te ti (contigo)
    él, ella, usted se le lo, la él, ella, usted
    nosotros nos nos nos nosotros
    vosotros os os os vosotros
    ellos, ellas, ustedes se les los, las ellos, ellas, ustedes

    Table 16.7. Pronouns. Object pronouns go before a conjugated verb (me lo llevo) or are attached to an infinitive (voy a llevármelo).

    Ten classic mistakes of English speakers¶

    Ten classic mistakes

    1. Age with ser. Not soy veinte años but tengo veinte años (Lessons 3 and 10).
    2. Feelings with ser. Not soy cansado but estoy cansado (Lesson 5).
    3. Location with ser. Not Madrid es en España but Madrid está en España (Lesson 5). But events use ser: La fiesta es en mi casa.
    4. An article with jobs. Not soy un profesor but soy profesor (Lesson 5).
    5. Gustar like "like". Not yo gusto el café but me gusta el café; and me gustan for plural things (Lesson 12).
    6. Forgetting agreement. Not la casa blanco but la casa blanca; not mis amigos es but mis amigos son (Lessons 4 and 7).
    7. Missing the article in general statements. English says "I like coffee"; Spanish says me gusta el café, and "Children like sweets" is a los niños les gustan los dulces (Lessons 4 and 12).
    8. Hot and cold with ser or estar. For a person, tengo calor, tengo frío; for the weather, hace calor, hace frío (Lesson 10).
    9. Translating "to" before an infinitive after ir. Not voy estudiar but voy a estudiar (Lesson 14).
    10. Accents left out. esta and está, hablo and habló, tu and tú are different words (Lessons 1 and 15).

    False friends¶

    Some Spanish words look like English words but mean something else. These are the ones that cause the most embarrassment:

    Spanish means not
    embarazada pregnant embarrassed (avergonzado)
    librería bookshop library (biblioteca)
    éxito success exit (salida)
    constipado having a cold (Spain) constipated
    carpeta folder carpet (alfombra)
    actual current, present-day actual (real)
    sensible sensitive sensible (sensato)
    asistir to attend to assist (ayudar)
    largo long large (grande)
    recordar to remember, to remind to record (grabar)

    16.5 Practice: writing and speaking about yourself¶

    A model text¶

    Read this text by Priya, a learner who has finished a first course. It uses the main tools of the book: the present, ser and estar, gustar, the preterite and ir a. Listen to it, then use it as a model for your own.

    Me llamo Priya y soy india, de Bombay, pero vivo en Leeds, en Inglaterra. Tengo treinta y dos años y soy enfermera. Trabajo en un hospital grande. Normalmente me levanto a las seis y media, porque empiezo a trabajar a las ocho. Mi trabajo es difícil, pero me gusta mucho. Tengo un hermano, Arjun, que vive en Toronto. Es ingeniero y es muy divertido. En mi tiempo libre me encanta cocinar y leer novelas. No me gusta nada el fútbol. Empecé a estudiar español hace un año. El verano pasado fui a Barcelona con unas amigas. Visitamos la Sagrada Familia, comimos tapas y pasamos dos días en la playa. ¡Lo pasamos genial! El año que viene voy a ir a México, porque quiero practicar mi español y conocer la cultura mexicana. Estoy muy contenta con mis progresos.

    My name is Priya and I'm Indian, from Mumbai, but I live in Leeds, in England. I'm thirty-two and I'm a nurse. I work in a large hospital. I usually get up at half past six, because I start work at eight. My job is hard, but I like it a lot. I have a brother, Arjun, who lives in Toronto. He's an engineer and he's great fun. In my free time I love cooking and reading novels. I don't like football at all. I started learning Spanish a year ago. Last summer I went to Barcelona with some friends. We visited the Sagrada Familia, ate tapas and spent two days at the beach. We had a great time! Next year I'm going to go to Mexico, because I want to practise my Spanish and get to know Mexican culture. I'm very pleased with my progress.

    Bombay or Mumbai?

    Spanish often keeps older or Spanish forms of place names: Bombay, Londres (London), Nueva York, Pekín (Beijing). The Spanish academies recommend some of these traditional names, but the new official names are increasingly common too.

    Your own text¶

    Write ten to twelve sentences about yourself, in this order. Use Priya's text as a model, but write about your own life.

    1. Your name, nationality, where you live (me llamo, soy, vivo).
    2. Your age and job or studies (tengo … años, soy, trabajo, estudio).
    3. Your daily routine (me levanto, desayuno, voy).
    4. Your family, with a description (tengo, es, tiene).
    5. Your likes and dislikes (me gusta, me encanta, no me gusta nada).
    6. Your last holiday (fui, visité, comí, lo pasé).
    7. Your plans (voy a, quiero).

    16.6 Culture: where to go from here¶

    Exams and levels. The levels used in this book come from the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR), which describes language ability in six levels, from A1 (beginner) to C2 (mastery). This course covers A1 and the beginning of A2. The official exams of Spanish as a foreign language are the DELE (Diplomas de Español como Lengua Extranjera), awarded by the Instituto Cervantes on behalf of Spain's Ministry of Education, at all six levels. A newer exam, SIELE, is taken on a computer and also promoted by the Instituto Cervantes. A1 or A2 is a good first goal after this book.

    Living Spanish. The best next step is contact with the language as it is really used:

    • Listen to music from different countries, from flamenco and Mexican rancheras to salsa, tango, cumbia and pop. Look up the lyrics and listen again.
    • Watch films and series from Spain, Mexico, Argentina, Colombia and elsewhere, first with Spanish subtitles, then without.
    • Read graded readers, children's books and the news. Many newspapers and broadcasters in Spain and Latin America publish their articles and programmes freely online.
    • Talk. An intercambio (Lessons 6 and 12) gives you real conversation for free. Online, you can find exchange partners anywhere.
    • Travel, if you can, and use every chance to speak: ordering, asking the way, chatting at a market.

    Which Spanish? You may wonder whether to learn the Spanish of Spain or of Latin America. The answer is: the one you will use most, and understand the other. The grammar of this book is shared by all educated speakers; the differences are mainly in a few sounds (Lesson 1), the plural "you" (Lesson 2), the use of the present perfect (Lesson 15) and everyday vocabulary (in the culture notes of every lesson). A Spanish speaker from any country will be glad that you have learnt their language, whichever accent you have.

    Mistakes are normal. Native speakers are generally patient and encouraging with learners, and most will be pleased that you are trying. Do not wait until your Spanish is perfect to use it: you learn by speaking, being misunderstood and trying again.

    16.8 Exercises¶

    Exercise 1

    Complete with ser, estar or hay: Mi oficina ___ en el centro.

    Answer

    está. Mi oficina está en el centro. Location takes estar.

    Exercise 2

    Complete with ser, estar or hay: En mi calle ___ dos supermercados.

    Answer

    hay. En mi calle hay dos supermercados. Existence takes hay.

    Exercise 3

    Give the plural: el lápiz azul.

    Answer

    los lápices azules. los lápices azules. z becomes c before e; azul adds -es.

    Exercise 4

    Translate: "We like the beaches of Mexico."

    Answer

    Nos gustan las playas de México. Nos gustan las playas de México. Plural thing liked, so gustan.

    Exercise 5

    Complete with the present: ¿A qué hora ___ (acostarse) tú?

    Answer

    te acuestas. ¿A qué hora te acuestas? Reflexive and o → ue.

    Exercise 6

    Complete with the preterite: Ayer mis amigos ___ (venir) a cenar.

    Answer

    vinieron. Ayer mis amigos vinieron a cenar.

    Exercise 7

    Say "Next week I'm going to buy a car" in Spanish.

    Answer

    La semana que viene voy a comprar un coche. La semana que viene voy a comprar un coche. In much of Latin America: un carro or un auto.

    Exercise 8

    Give the feminine plural of trabajador.

    Answer

    trabajadoras. trabajadoras

    Exercise 9

    Choose: Hoy hace / tiene / está mucho calor.

    Answer

    hace. Hoy hace mucho calor. Weather takes hacer.

    Exercise 10

    Correct the sentence: Mi hermana es veinticinco años y es muy cansada hoy.

    Answer

    Mi hermana tiene veinticinco años y está muy cansada hoy. Age takes tener; a temporary state takes estar.

    Exercise 11

    Ask a stranger politely, using usted, if they can help you.

    Answer

    ¿Puede ayudarme, por favor? ¿Puede ayudarme, por favor? or ¿Me puede ayudar, por favor?

    Exercise 12

    Write three sentences about the same topic, eating, in the present, the preterite and with ir a: what you usually have for lunch, what you had yesterday, and what you are going to have tomorrow.

    Answer

    A possible answer: Normalmente como un bocadillo. Ayer comí una ensalada. Mañana voy a comer paella.
