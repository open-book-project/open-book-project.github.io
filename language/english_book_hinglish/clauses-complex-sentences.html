    Chapter 20: Chhote se bade vaakya¶

    Clauses — chhote vaakyon se bade vaakya

    Agar aapki English me har vaakya chhota-chhota hai — "I have a friend. He lives in Delhi. He is a doctor." — to galat kuch nahi, par baat bachchon jaisi lagti hai. Clauses ko jodna seekh lo, to wahi baat ek saaf, pakke vaakya me aa jaati hai. Ye chapter wahi jodne ka hunar sikhata hai.

    Ek line me

    A clause is a group of words with its own subject and verb. A sentence can have one clause or many. Sentence structure wala chapter yaad hai? Phrase me subject-verb jodi nahi hoti; clause me hoti hai.

    • I have a friend who lives in Delhi. Do clause: "I have a friend" + "who lives in Delhi".
    • She was tired, so she went to bed early. Do clause, "so" se jude hue.

    20.1 Independent aur Dependent clause¶

    Har clause ek jaisa nahi hota. Kuch akele khade ho sakte hain, kuch nahi. Ye farq samajhna zaroori hai, kyunki adhoore clause ko poora vaakya maan lena likhne me sabse badi galti hai.

    Independent vs dependent

    Independent clause akela poora vaakya hai (I stayed at home.). Dependent clause because, when, if, although, who, which jaise shabd se shuru hota hai aur akela adhoora rehta hai — use independent clause se jodo.

    Dependent clause shuru karne wale Kaam Example
    because, since, as kaaran because he was ill
    when, while, after, before, until samay when the bell rang
    if, unless shart if you call me
    although, though, even though virodh although it was late
    who, which, that, whose, where noun ke baare me who lives next door

    20.2 Chaar tarah ke vaakya¶

    Clauses kitne hain aur kaise jude hain — isse vaakya ke chaar prakaar bante hain. Achhi English me chaaron ka mel hota hai; sirf ek tarah ke vaakya likhne se lekh neeras ho jaata hai.

    Chaar prakaar

    Simple = ek independent clause. Compound = do independent (and, but, so se). Complex = independent + dependent (because, when, who se). Compound-complex = do independent + kam se kam ek dependent.

    20.3 Compound — barabar ke do clause jodna¶

    Jab do baatein barabar zaroori hon, to unhe compound sentence me jodo. Iske liye saat chhote shabd hain jinhe yaad karne ki trick hai — FANBOYS.

    Shabd Kaam Example
    For kaaran (formal) He left early, for he was ill.
    And jodna I cooked, and she cleaned.
    Nor na ye, na wo He didn't call, nor did he write.
    But virodh It was cheap, but it was good.
    Or vikalp Hurry up, or we'll miss the train.
    Yet phir bhi She was tired, yet she kept working.
    So natija It was raining, so we stayed in.

    Compound ka niyam

    Do independent clause jodne ke liye comma + FANBOYS (for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so) lagao. Sirf comma se do poore vaakya nahi judte: I was tired, I slept. ✗ → I was tired, so I slept. ✓

    Comma kab chhod sakte ho

    Dono clause bahut chhote hon to comma zaroori nahi: I cooked and she cleaned.
    Aur agar doosre hisse me subject hi na ho, to wo clause nahi — comma mat lagao: She opened the door and smiled.

    20.4 Complex — kaaran, samay, shart aur virodh¶

    Jab ek baat mukhya ho aur doosri usse kaaran, samay, shart ya virodh batati ho, to complex sentence banta hai. Ye wahi vaakya hain jo aapki English ko sochi-samjhi aur paripakv banate hain.

    • I took an umbrella because it looked cloudy. Mukhya baat: chhaata liya. Kaaran: baadal the.
    • When the guests arrived, we served lunch. Dependent clause pehle aaya, isliye beech me comma.
    • Although the test was hard, she passed. Test mushkil tha, phir bhi wo paas ho gayi. — "but" ki zaroorat nahi.

    Complex ka niyam

    Dependent clause pehle aaye to beech me comma lagao (When it rained, we stayed in.); baad me aaye to comma nahi. Ek jod ke liye ek hi shabd: although ya but — dono nahi.

    Hindi ka 'halaanki… lekin' English me nahi chalta

    • Galat: Although he is rich, but he is not happy.
    • Sahi: Although he is rich, he is not happy. / He is rich, but he is not happy.
    • Kyun: Hindi me "halaanki… lekin" dono lagte hain, English me ek hi. Isi tarah "because… so" bhi saath nahi aate.

    20.5 Relative clauses — who, which, that, whose, where¶

    Relative clause kisi noun ke baare me aur jaankari deta hai, bilkul adjective ki tarah — bas ek shabd ki jagah poora clause. Isse do chhote vaakya ek ho jaate hain: I met a man. He speaks six languages. → I met a man who speaks six languages.

    Relative clause ka niyam

    who — log, which — cheezein, that — dono, whose — kiska, where — jagah. Relative shabd noun ki jagah leta hai, isliye wahi noun ya pronoun dobara mat lagao.

    Shabd Kiske liye Example
    who insaan the girl who won the prize
    which cheez, jaanwar the bus which goes to Agra
    that insaan ya cheez (bina comma) the book that I lost
    whose maalikana — "jiska" the boy whose bike was stolen
    where jagah the village where I was born
    when samay the day when we met

    Object wala relative chhod bhi sakte ho

    Jab who/that clause ka object ho, to use hata sakte ho: the book (that) I lost, the man (who) I met. Bolchaal me ye bahut aam hai. Par subject ho to nahi: the man who called — "who" zaroori hai.

    20.6 Defining vs Non-defining — comma ka khel¶

    Do commas vaakya ka matlab badal sakte hain. Sawaal ye hai: kya clause batata hai ki kaunsa noun, ya sirf ek extra jaankari jodta hai jo hata bhi do to farq nahi padta?

    Defining vs non-defining

    Defining clause batata hai kaunsa noun — bina comma, who/which/that teeno chalte hain. Non-defining sirf extra jaankari deta hai — commas ke saath, aur isme "that" nahi lagta.

    • The Taj Mahal, which is in Agra, attracts millions of visitors. Taj Mahal ek hi hai — "which is in Agra" extra jaankari hai, isliye commas.
    • Students who finish early can leave. Sirf wo students jo jaldi khatam karein — clause zaroori hai, comma nahi.

    20.7 Chhote vaakyon ko jodkar behtar vaakya¶

    Ab sab saath laate hain. Likhte waqt pehle chhote vaakya likh lo, phir dekho kaunse jod sakte ho. Ye teen sawaal poochho: kya ye barabar hain (FANBOYS)? kya ek kaaran/samay hai (because, when)? kya ek noun ke baare me hai (who, which)?

    Pehle (chhote vaakya) Baad me (jode hue)
    I have a cousin. She lives in Canada. She is a nurse. I have a cousin who lives in Canada and works as a nurse.
    The train was late. We missed the meeting. The train was late, so we missed the meeting.
    It was raining. The match continued. Although it was raining, the match continued.
    This is the school. My father studied here. This is the school where my father studied.
    I met a writer. Her books are famous. I met a writer whose books are famous.

    Sab kuch ek vaakya me mat thoso

    Jodne ka matlab ye nahi ki poora paragraph ek vaakya ban jaaye. Ek vaakya me do-teen clause kaafi hain. Lamba ho jaaye to wapas tod do — saaf baat hamesha lambi baat se behtar hai.

    20.8 Wo galtiyan jo hum sach me karte hain¶

    Clauses ki sabse aam Indian-English galtiyan

    • Galat: Although I studied hard, but I failed.
    • Sahi: Although I studied hard, I failed.

    • Kyun: "halaanki… lekin" ka seedha anuvaad. English me ek jod ke liye ek hi shabd — although ya but.

    • Galat: This is the man who I told you about him.

    • Sahi: This is the man (who) I told you about.

    • Kyun: "who" pehle hi "the man" ki jagah le chuka hai. "him" lagane se wahi aadmi do baar aa gaya. Isi tarah: the book which I bought it.

    • Galat: I was very tired, I went to bed early.

    • Sahi: I was very tired, so I went to bed early.

    • Kyun: Do independent clause sirf comma se nahi judte. Beech me FANBOYS lagao, ya full stop.

    • Galat: My father, that is a teacher, loves reading.

    • Sahi: My father, who is a teacher, loves reading.

    • Kyun: Commas wale (non-defining) clause me "that" nahi lagta. Insaan ke liye "who", cheez ke liye "which".

    • Galat: Because the shop was closed.

    • Sahi: We came back because the shop was closed.
    • Kyun: "Because" wala clause dependent hai — akela vaakya nahi ban sakta. Jawab me bolna theek hai, par likhte waqt use mukhya clause se jodo.

    20.9 Practice — Clauses¶

    Exercise 1

    'I called her, but she didn't answer.' — ye kaunsa vaakya hai?

    1. Compound
    2. Simple
    3. Complex
    4. Compound-complex

    Hint: Kitne independent clause hain?

    Jawaab

    Compound

    Do independent clause hain — 'I called her' aur 'she didn't answer' — jo 'but' (FANBOYS) se jude hain. Dependent clause koi nahi, isliye compound.

    Exercise 2

    'When the power went off, we lit candles.' — ye kaunsa vaakya hai?

    1. Complex
    2. Compound
    3. Simple
    4. Compound-complex
    Jawaab

    Complex

    'When the power went off' dependent clause hai, 'we lit candles' independent. Independent + dependent = complex.

    Exercise 3

    This is the girl ___ father is a pilot.

    1. whose
    2. who
    3. which
    4. where

    Hint: 'jiske pita'

    Jawaab

    whose

    Maalikana batana hai — 'jiske pita'. Iske liye 'whose'. 'who father' galat hai.

    Exercise 4

    Kaunsa vaakya sahi hai?

    1. Although it was late, we kept talking.
    2. Although it was late, but we kept talking.
    3. Although it was late, so we kept talking.
    4. It was late, although but we kept talking.
    Jawaab

    Although it was late, we kept talking.

    Ek jod ke liye ek hi shabd. 'Although' lag gaya to 'but' ki zaroorat nahi — Hindi ka 'halaanki… lekin' English me nahi chalta.

    Exercise 5

    Mumbai, ___ is India's financial capital, is very crowded.

    1. which
    2. that
    3. who
    4. where

    Hint: Commas ke beech — non-defining.

    Jawaab

    which

    Commas wale clause me 'that' nahi lagta, aur Mumbai cheez/jagah hai (yahan subject), insaan nahi — isliye 'which'. 'where' tab aata jab clause me apna subject hota: where my uncle lives.

    Exercise 6

    This is the village ___ my grandmother was born.

    Hint: Jagah ki baat.

    Jawaab

    where / in which

    Jagah ke liye 'where' — the village where she was born. 'in which' bhi sahi hai, par thoda formal.

    Exercise 7

    Ek extra shabd hai — us par tap karo.

    This is the laptop which I bought it last week.

    Jawaab

    Galat shabd: it.

    Sahi roop: This is the laptop which I bought last week.

    'which' pehle hi 'the laptop' ki jagah le chuka hai. 'it' lagaane se wahi cheez do baar aa jaati hai.

    Exercise 8

    Galat shabd dhoondo.

    Although the hotel was expensive, but the food was terrible.

    Jawaab

    Galat shabd: but.

    Sahi roop: Although the hotel was expensive, the food was terrible.

    'Although' aur 'but' ek saath nahi aate. Ek hata do — yahan 'but'.

    Exercise 9

    Relative shabd ko uske kaam se jodo.

    AB
    whojiska / maalikana
    whichjagah
    whoseinsaan
    wherecheez
    Jawaab

    who — insaan; which — cheez; whose — jiska / maalikana; where — jagah

    who log ke liye, which cheezon ke liye, whose maalikana ke liye, where jagah ke liye. 'that' defining clause me log aur cheez dono ke liye chalta hai.

    Exercise 10

    Do vaakya ek banao: 'I have a friend. He speaks Japanese.'

    Shabd: friend / Japanese / I / who / speaks / a / have

    Jawaab

    I have a friend who speaks Japanese

    'who' ne 'He' ki jagah le li aur clause seedha 'a friend' ke baad baith gaya. Do chhote vaakya ek saaf vaakya ban gaye.

    Exercise 11

    Complex sentence banao.

    Shabd: inside / stayed / We / raining / because / was / heavily / it

    Jawaab

    We stayed inside because it was raining heavily

    Mukhya baat pehle (we stayed inside), kaaran baad me (because…). Dependent clause baad me aaya, isliye comma ki zaroorat nahi.

    Exercise 12

    Ye vaakya bolo — commas par halka sa ruko.

    My brother, who lives in Pune, is a doctor

    Jawaab

    Bolo: My brother, who lives in Pune, is a doctor

    Bolte waqt commas ka matlab hai chhota sa thehraav. Is thehraav se sunne wala samajh jaata hai ki 'who lives in Pune' sirf extra jaankari hai.

    Aage kya karein

    Apna pichla koi email ya message kholo. Jahan teen chhote vaakya lagaatar hon, unme se do ko who, because ya so se jodo. Har roz ek paragraph — ek mahine me aapki likhai badal jaayegi.
