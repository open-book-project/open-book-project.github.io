    Chapter 30: Everyday conversations¶

    Everyday conversations — ready answers

    There is no grammar in this chapter. It has ready-made lines — sentences you can memorise and use from tomorrow: at the shop, in an auto, at the doctor's, on the phone, in a restaurant, and when asking for directions. Here is the real secret: fluent people do not build every sentence from scratch — they have ready-made pieces that come out at once. Every line has a 🔊. Listen, speak, memorise.

    How to study

    Do not do the whole chapter in one sitting. One day = one situation. Today do only "the shop" — say its five lines 10 times. Tomorrow "the auto". In six days you will have six situations ready, and you will never forget them.

    30.1 At the shop — buying¶

    You want to English line Listen
    Ask if they have it Excuse me, do you have this in blue?
    Ask the price How much is this?
    Say you are only browsing I'm just looking, thanks.
    Ask for a smaller or bigger size Do you have a smaller size?
    Ask to try it on Can I try this on?
    Ask for a lower price Can you do it for a little less?
    Say it is too expensive That's a bit more than I wanted to spend.
    Decide to buy it I'll take it.
    Ask if they accept cards Do you take cards?
    Ask for the bill Could I have the receipt, please?
    Ask about exchanging Can I exchange it if the size is wrong?

    A full mini-dialogue — the shop

    Speak both sides. First your line, then the shopkeeper's line too — this gets your ear ready.

    • You: Excuse me, do you have this shirt in medium? = Is this shirt available in medium?
    • Shopkeeper: Let me check. Yes, here you go. "Here you go" = here, take it. A very common line.
    • You: Thanks. How much is it? = What is the price?
    • Shopkeeper: It's nine hundred and fifty. 950. Note: "nine hundred AND fifty" — there is an "and" in the middle.
    • You: Could you do nine hundred? I'll take two. = Will you take 900 if I buy two? The politest way to bargain.
    • Shopkeeper: All right, nine hundred each. Cash or card? "each" = for one piece. Notice it is not just "nine hundred".
    • You: Card, please. And could I have the receipt? receipt = "ri-SEET", the P is silent.

    30.2 Auto and cab¶

    You want to English line Listen
    Ask if they will go to the station Will you go to the railway station?
    Ask the fare How much will it be?
    Ask for the meter Could you go by the meter, please?
    Say the fare is too high That's too much. Two hundred is fine.
    Ask how long it takes How long will it take?
    Ask the driver to go faster Could you go a bit faster? I'm running late.
    Ask the driver to go slower Could you slow down a little, please?
    Ask the driver to stop You can stop here, thanks.
    Tell the driver where to turn Take a left at the next signal.
    Say you have no small change Sorry, I don't have change.
    Ask if you can pay online Can I pay online?
    Ask for the AC Could you turn on the AC, please?

    A full mini-dialogue — the cab

    • You: Hi, are you going towards the airport? = Are you heading in the direction of the airport?
    • Driver: Yes, hop in. It'll take about forty minutes. "Hop in" = get in. "About forty" = around forty.
    • You: Perfect. My flight is at six, so I have time. = The flight is at six, so there is enough time.
    • Driver: There's some traffic near the bridge, is that okay? = The road is busy near the bridge. Is that fine?
    • You: That's fine. Take whichever route is faster. = Use the quicker road, whichever it is.
    • You (on arrival): You can drop me at gate three. Thanks a lot. = Please leave me at gate three. "Drop me" = let me get out.

    30.3 At the doctor's¶

    A pattern that always works

    "I have [problem] + for [how long]." I have a bad headache. It's been two days. What it is, and since when — these are the only two things a doctor needs.

    Problem English line Listen
    Fever I have a fever. It started last night.
    Headache I've had a headache since yesterday.
    Cough and cold I have a cough and a blocked nose.
    Stomach pain I have a stomach ache, mostly after eating.
    Feeling dizzy I feel dizzy when I stand up.
    Feeling weak I've been feeling weak and tired all week.
    Pain in one place It hurts here, right below the knee.
    Cannot sleep I'm not sleeping well these days.
    Allergic to a medicine I'm allergic to penicillin.
    Already taking a medicine I'm already taking a tablet for blood pressure.
    What the doctor will ask In simple words Listen
    How long have you had this? Since when do you have it?
    Does it hurt when I press here? Do you feel pain when I push on this spot?
    Are you on any medication? Are you taking any medicine now?
    Take this twice a day after meals. Two times a day, after you eat.
    Come back if it doesn't improve in three days. If you are not better in three days, visit again.

    Always ask these two questions

    How many times a day should I take this?
    Before food or after food?

    30.4 Phone call¶

    The phone feels the hardest — you cannot see the face, and the sound cuts in and out. That is why memorising lines helps the most here.

    Purpose English line Listen
    Answering a call Hello, this is Ravi speaking.
    Asking who is calling May I know who's calling?
    Asking for someone Could I speak to Mr Sharma, please?
    Asking someone to wait Hold on a second, please.
    Saying the person is not there He's not at his desk right now.
    Offering to take a message Can I take a message?
    The sound is cutting out Sorry, you're breaking up. Could you say that again?
    The network is bad The line is bad. Let me call you back.
    Wrong number I think you have the wrong number.
    Talking later Can I call you back in ten minutes?
    Ending the call Thanks for your time. Have a good day.

    Three common phone mistakes

    • Wrong: "Hello, myself Ravi."
    • Right: Hello, this is Ravi.

    • Why: "Myself Ravi" is not a sentence in English. On the phone, always use "This is…".

    • Wrong: "Who is this?" (blunt, a little rude)

    • Right: May I know who's calling?

    • Why: "Who is this?" sounds harsh. "May I know…" says the same thing, but politely.

    • Wrong: "I didn't get you." (it sounds like you did not recognise the person)

    • Right: Sorry, I didn't catch that.
    • Why: "Didn't catch that" has a clear meaning: I could not hear or understand what you said.

    30.5 In a restaurant¶

    You want to English line Listen
    Ask for a table for two A table for two, please.
    Ask for the menu Could we see the menu, please?
    Ask what is good here What would you recommend?
    Ask how spicy it is Is this very spicy?
    Ask for less spice Could you make it less spicy, please?
    Place your order I'll have the paneer tikka, please.
    Ask if it is vegetarian Is this vegetarian?
    Ask for water Could we get some water, please?
    The food has not come Excuse me, our order hasn't come yet.
    They brought the wrong dish Sorry, I think this isn't what I ordered.
    Ask for a takeaway box Could you pack the rest, please?
    Ask for the bill Could we have the bill, please?

    A full mini-dialogue — the restaurant

    • Waiter: Good evening. How many of you? = How many people are in your group?
    • You: Three, please. Somewhere away from the AC, if possible. "If possible" = only if it can be done. These two words make any request softer.
    • Waiter: Sure. Are you ready to order? = Do you want to tell me what you would like to eat now?
    • You: Give us a couple of minutes. What would you recommend? "A couple of minutes" = two or three minutes.
    • Waiter: The butter chicken is very popular here. = Many people order it here.
    • You: We'll have that, two rotis and a plain rice. And could you make it less spicy? The pattern for ordering: "We'll have…" + a list. Completely natural.
    • You (later): That was lovely, thanks. Could we have the bill? = The food was very good. "Lovely" is a very natural word for food.

    30.6 Asking for and giving directions¶

    You want to English line Listen
    Start by asking for help Excuse me, could you help me with directions?
    Ask where the metro is How do I get to the metro station?
    Ask how far it is Is it far from here?
    Ask if you can walk Can I walk there, or should I take an auto?
    Say you are lost I think I'm lost. I'm looking for Lake Road.
    Ask them to repeat Sorry, could you say that once more, slowly?
    The answer you will get In simple words Listen
    Go straight for about five minutes. Keep walking forward for five minutes.
    Take the second left. Turn into the second street on the left.
    It's right opposite the bank. It is directly across from the bank.
    It's next to the chemist. It is right beside the medical store.
    You'll see it on your right. It will be on your right-hand side.
    You've come too far — turn around. You went past it — go back.

    30.7 Small phrases that work everywhere¶

    Line When to say it Listen
    Excuse me… Before you speak to a stranger — always.
    Sorry to bother you. When you interrupt someone.
    No worries. "It's nothing" — when someone says sorry.
    That's all right. "It's fine, forget it."
    Thanks a lot — that helps. Thanking someone for their help.
    Appreciate it. A short, natural thank-you.
    Take care. Saying goodbye — to a friend or someone you know.

    Four lines we say, but that sound odd in English

    • Wrong: What is your good name?
    • Right: May I know your name?

    • Why: "Good name" is a word-for-word copy of the Hindi "shubh naam". English does not say this.

    • Wrong: Please do the needful.

    • Right: Could you take care of this, please?

    • Why: "Do the needful" does not tell the other person what to do. Say it clearly.

    • Wrong: I am having two brothers.

    • Right: I have two brothers.

    • Why: "Have" does not take -ing when it means "to own or possess".

    • Wrong: Where you are going?

    • Right: Where are you going?
    • Why: In a question the helping verb comes before the subject: Where are you…

    30.8 Practice — Everyday conversations¶

    Exercise 1

    What is the most natural way to ask the price in a shop?

    1. How much is this?
    2. What is the cost of this?
    3. How much price?
    4. This is how much rupees?
    Answer

    How much is this?

    Short, direct, works everywhere. 'What is the cost' sounds bookish.

    Exercise 2

    How do you give your name when you pick up the phone?

    1. Hello, this is Ravi speaking.
    2. Hello, myself Ravi.
    3. Hello, I am Ravi speaking here.
    4. Hello, Ravi this side.

    Hint: Not with 'Myself'.

    Answer

    Hello, this is Ravi speaking.

    On the phone, always give your name with 'This is…'. 'Myself Ravi' is not a sentence in English.

    Exercise 3

    What is the pattern for telling a doctor your problem?

    1. What it is + since when
    2. Only the name of a medicine
    3. How much it hurts, from 1 to 10
    4. Say your age first
    Answer

    What it is + since when

    'I have a fever. It started last night.' At the start, the doctor needs only these two things.

    Exercise 4

    The sound on the phone keeps cutting out. What do you say?

    1. Sorry, you're breaking up. Could you say that again?
    2. I didn't get you.
    3. Your voice is not coming.
    4. Speak loudly please.
    Answer

    Sorry, you're breaking up. Could you say that again?

    'Breaking up' is the right phrase for a bad phone connection. 'I didn't get you' sounds like you did not recognise the person.

    Exercise 5

    What is the natural pattern for ordering in a restaurant?

    1. I'll have the… / We'll have the…
    2. Give me one…
    3. I want to eat…
    4. Bring me…
    Answer

    I'll have the… / We'll have the…

    'I'll have the paneer tikka, please.' The most common and the most polite way.

    Exercise 6

    What is the right English for asking an auto driver to go by the meter?

    1. Could you go by the meter, please?
    2. Please put meter on.
    3. Meter only please.
    4. Go in meter.
    Answer

    Could you go by the meter, please?

    'by the meter' is the right phrase. 'Could you…, please' makes it a request, not an order.

    Exercise 7

    At the shop: Do you have this ___ a smaller size?

    Hint: Which preposition goes with size or colour?

    Answer

    in

    Use 'in' with size and colour: in blue, in medium, in a smaller size.

    Exercise 8

    Restaurant: Could we have the ___, please? (what you ask for when you want to pay)

    Answer

    bill / cheque / check

    'Bill' in India and the UK, 'check' in the US. Both are fine.

    Exercise 9

    Directions: Take the second ___. (the second street on the left)

    Answer

    left

    'Take the second left' = the second turn on the left. 'Take a left at the signal' is also common.

    Exercise 10

    There is a mistake in this sentence — tap the word.

    I am having two brothers and one sister.

    Answer

    Wrong word: having.

    Corrected: I have two brothers and one sister.

    When 'have' means 'to own or possess', it does not take -ing.

    Exercise 11

    Find the wrong word.

    What is your good name, sir?

    Answer

    Wrong word: good.

    Corrected: May I know your name, sir?

    'Good name' is a word-for-word copy of the Hindi 'shubh naam'. English does not say this.

    Exercise 12

    Match each situation to its ready-made line.

    AB
    Shop — just browsingIt started last night.
    Cab — stop hereCan I call you back in ten minutes?
    Doctor — since whenI'm just looking, thanks.
    Phone — I will talk laterYou can stop here, thanks.
    Answer

    Shop — just browsing — I'm just looking, thanks.; Cab — stop here — You can stop here, thanks.; Doctor — since when — It started last night.; Phone — I will talk later — Can I call you back in ten minutes?

    One ready-made line for each situation. You do not have to think and build it.

    Exercise 13

    Build the shop line.

    Words: please / Could / have / I / receipt / the

    Answer

    Could I have the receipt please

    'Could I have…' is the politest pattern for asking. receipt = ri-SEET, the P is silent.

    Exercise 14

    At the shop. Say the first line.

    Excuse me, do you have this in blue

    Answer

    Say: Excuse me, do you have this in blue

    Start with 'Excuse me'. 'in blue' — always 'in' with a colour.

    Exercise 15

    Bargain politely.

    Could you do it for a little less

    Answer

    Say: Could you do it for a little less

    Better than saying 'Reduce the price' directly. 'Could you' makes the same thing softer.

    Exercise 16

    Your first words as you get into a cab.

    Are you going towards the airport

    Answer

    Say: Are you going towards the airport

    'towards' makes it easy. The voice rises slightly at the end — it is a question.

    Exercise 17

    When you get out of the cab.

    You can drop me at gate three. Thanks a lot

    Answer

    Say: You can drop me at gate three. Thanks a lot

    'drop me at' = leave me here. End with a thank-you — short and natural.

    Exercise 18

    Tell the doctor your problem — what and since when.

    I have a bad headache. It's been two days

    Answer

    Say: I have a bad headache. It's been two days

    Problem + time. These are the two things a doctor needs at the start.

    Exercise 19

    An important question about the medicine.

    How many times a day should I take this

    Answer

    Say: How many times a day should I take this

    How many times a day. Ask 'Before food or after food?' as well.

    Exercise 20

    Pick up the phone — give your name.

    Hello, this is Ravi speaking

    Answer

    Say: Hello, this is Ravi speaking

    'This is' + name. Never 'Myself'.

    Exercise 21

    You did not understand on the phone. Ask politely.

    Sorry, you're breaking up. Could you say that again

    Answer

    Say: Sorry, you're breaking up. Could you say that again

    Use 'breaking up' for a network problem. Then ask them to repeat.

    Exercise 22

    As soon as you sit down in the restaurant.

    A table for two please

    Answer

    Say: A table for two please

    That is enough. You do not need a long sentence.

    Exercise 23

    Ask for the food to be less spicy.

    Could you make it less spicy please

    Answer

    Say: Could you make it less spicy please

    'less spicy' = not so hot. 'Could you' makes every request polite.

    Exercise 24

    Ask for directions.

    Excuse me, how do I get to the metro station

    Answer

    Say: Excuse me, how do I get to the metro station

    'How do I get to…' is the most useful pattern for asking directions — for any place.

    Exercise 25

    You are lost. Ask for help.

    I think I'm lost. I'm looking for Lake Road

    Answer

    Say: I think I'm lost. I'm looking for Lake Road

    Say your situation first, then the name of the place. People help at once.

    What next?

    Today's task: choose one situation — one that will really come up tomorrow — and say its five lines 10 times. Then use the same lines in the real place tomorrow. The first time your heart will beat fast; the second time it will be easier; the third time it will be a habit.
