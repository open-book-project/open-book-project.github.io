    Chapter 17: Subject-verb agreement¶

    Subject-verb agreement — "He go" or "He goes"?

    Agreement means a match — the subject and the verb must match. Hindi has this match too, but in a different way (ladka jaata hai / ladke jaate hain), so you already know the idea. There are only two trouble spots. First, in English the -s goes with the singular (it feels backwards!). Second, sometimes the real subject hides behind the words in between. This chapter clears up both for good.

    In one line

    A singular subject takes a singular verb; a plural subject takes a plural verb. One subject → a singular verb. Many subjects → a plural verb. And remember: an -s on a verb means singular.

    17.1 The basic chart¶

    Subject Present Simple be (present) be (past) have
    I go am was have
    You go are were have
    He / She / It goes is was has
    We go are were have
    They go are were have

    The double trick of -s

    An -s on a noun makes it plural: boy → boys.
    An -s on a verb makes it singular: go → goes.
    So in one sentence the -s goes either on the noun or on the verb — not on both. The boy runs ✓ The boys run ✓ The boys runs ✗

    17.2 Find the real subject — words in between don't count¶

    This is where most mistakes happen. A long phrase comes between the subject and the verb, and your brain takes the last word of that phrase as the subject. The trick: put an imaginary glass over the middle part and forget it.

    None of these in-between words change the verb

    of, with, along with, together with, as well as, including, besides, in addition to, accompanied by
    The minister, along with his officers, has arrived. The subject is "the minister" — one person. The "officers" make no difference.

    17.3 Always singular — remember these¶

    Subject Correct Wrong
    each Each boy has a book. Each boy have…
    every Every student was present. Every student were…
    everyone / everybody Everyone knows it. Everyone know it.
    someone / somebody Somebody is at the door. Somebody are…
    nobody / no one Nobody came. —
    anyone / anybody Does anybody know him? Do anybody know…
    either / neither (on their own) Neither is correct. Neither are correct.
    one of + plural One of my friends is a doctor. One of my friend is…
    the number of The number of cases is rising. The number of cases are…
    each of / every one of Each of the girls sings well. Each of the girls sing…

    'one of my friend' — two mistakes at once

    • Wrong: One of my friend are coming today.
    • Right: One of my friends is coming today.
    • Why: Think: "one of my friends". "Friends" is plural, but the sentence is about one person — so the verb is singular (is). The same rule works for "one of the best players", "one of my sisters" and so on.

    17.4 "A number of" vs "The number of"¶

    Phrase Meaning Verb Example
    a number of several, many plural A number of students are absent.
    the number of the count, the total singular The number of students is falling.
    a lot of / plenty of depends on the noun look at the noun A lot of water is wasted. · A lot of people are waiting.
    50% of / half of depends on the noun look at the noun Half of the cake is gone. · Half of the boys are here.

    17.5 Two subjects joined together — what then?¶

    Joining word Rule Example
    and plural Ravi and Sita are friends.
    and (one single thing) singular Bread and butter is my breakfast.
    or / nor matches the nearer subject Ravi or his brothers are coming.
    either… or matches the nearer subject Either the boys or the teacher is wrong.
    neither… nor matches the nearer subject Neither the teacher nor the boys are wrong.
    not only… but also matches the nearer subject Not only the players but also the coach was blamed.

    A smart tip

    In either/or and neither/nor sentences, if you can, put the plural subject last. Then the verb is plural and the sentence sounds more natural.
    Neither the manager nor the workers were happy.

    17.6 Collective nouns — one or many?¶

    Team, family, class, committee, government, jury, staff — these are groups. When the group acts as one unit, use a singular verb; when the people in the group act separately, use a plural verb.

    • The team is playing well today. The whole team as one unit — singular.
    • The team are arguing among themselves. The members are arguing with each other — separate people, so plural.

    Three words that are always plural

    police, people, cattle — the verb is always plural with these.
    The police have arrested him. ✓ The police has arrested him. ✗
    Many people were waiting. ✓

    17.7 Looks plural, but is singular¶

    Type Words Example
    Names of subjects mathematics, physics, economics, politics, statistics Mathematics is my favourite subject.
    Diseases measles, mumps, diabetes Measles is contagious.
    Reports news The news is good.
    Uncountable information, advice, furniture, luggage, work, money This information is useful.
    Distance, money, time — seen as one unit — Ten kilometres is a long walk. · Five hundred rupees is too much.
    Name of a book / film — "Three Idiots" is a great film.

    And the opposite — looks singular, but is plural

    scissors, trousers, jeans, spectacles, pliers, scales — things with two parts are plural: My spectacles are broken.
    But with "a pair of" the verb becomes singular: A pair of scissors is missing.

    17.8 There is / There are¶

    "There" is not the subject — the subject comes after it. So the verb matches the noun that comes after it.

    • There is a problem with the laptop. One problem — singular.
    • There are three problems in this file. Three problems — plural.

    17.9 The mistakes we really make¶

    Top agreement mistakes

    • Wrong: He don't know my name.
    • Right: He doesn't know my name.

    • Why: With he/she/it we use "does". "Don't" is only for I, you, we, they.

    • Wrong: My parents is very supportive.

    • Right: My parents are very supportive.

    • Why: "Parents" is plural — are. For one person, say "My father is".

    • Wrong: Everyone have their own problems.

    • Right: Everyone has their own problems.

    • Why: "Everyone" looks like it means many people, but grammatically it is singular — has. (Using "their" afterwards is fine; it is normal English today.)

    • Wrong: The police is looking for him.

    • Right: The police are looking for him.

    • Why: "Police" is always plural. For one officer, say "a policeman is".

    • Wrong: Mathematics are difficult for me.

    • Right: Mathematics is difficult for me.

    • Why: Subjects that end in -s (mathematics, physics, economics) are singular.

    • Wrong: One of my cousin works in Dubai.

    • Right: One of my cousins works in Dubai.

    • Why: After "one of" the noun is plural, but the verb is singular. Remember both together.

    • Wrong: The list of items are on the table.

    • Right: The list of items is on the table.

    • Why: The subject is "the list" — one list. "Of items" is a phrase in between; remove it.

    • Wrong: My brother and I is going to Goa.

    • Right: My brother and I are going to Goa.
    • Why: Two subjects joined by "and" = plural. Also mind the order — "My brother and I", not "I and my brother".

    Four steps, every time

    1. Find the verb.
    2. Ask "who or what?" — the answer is the subject.
    3. Remove everything between the subject and the verb.
    4. Now see whether the subject is one or many — and choose the verb to match.

    17.10 Practice — Subject-verb agreement¶

    Exercise 1

    One of my cousins ___ in Canada.

    1. live
    2. lives
    3. are living
    4. have lived

    Hint: How many people is the sentence about?

    Answer

    lives

    'One of my cousins' means 'ONE of my cousins'. The sentence is about one person, so the verb is singular — lives.

    Exercise 2

    The box of chocolates ___ on the fridge.

    1. are
    2. is
    3. were
    4. have

    Hint: What is the real subject — the box or the chocolates?

    Answer

    is

    The subject is 'the box' — one box. 'Of chocolates' is a phrase in between; remove it and 'The box is' is left.

    Exercise 3

    Neither the principal nor the teachers ___ present.

    1. was
    2. were
    3. is
    4. has

    Hint: Which subject is nearest to the verb?

    Answer

    were

    With neither…nor, the verb matches the nearest subject. The nearest is 'the teachers' — plural, so 'were'.

    Exercise 4

    The police ___ already caught the thief.

    1. has
    2. have
    3. is
    4. was

    Hint: Is 'police' singular or plural?

    Answer

    have

    'Police' is always plural in English — have. For one officer we say 'a policeman has'.

    Exercise 5

    Every student ___ (have) a separate roll number.

    Hint: Which form comes after 'every'?

    Answer

    has

    'Every' and 'each' are always singular, however many people come after — so 'has'.

    Exercise 6

    There ___ two pens and a pencil in my bag.

    Hint: What comes after 'There'?

    Answer

    are

    'There' is not the subject — the noun after it is. 'Two pens' is plural, so 'are'.

    Exercise 7

    Mathematics ___ my favourite subject.

    Hint: Ending in -s does not make a word plural.

    Answer

    is

    Mathematics, physics, economics, politics end in -s but are singular, so 'is'.

    Exercise 8

    There is one mistake in this sentence — tap the wrong word.

    My younger brother don't like sweets.

    Answer

    Wrong word: don't.

    Corrected: My younger brother doesn't like sweets.

    The subject 'my younger brother' = he. With he/she/it we use 'doesn't', not 'don't'.

    Exercise 9

    Find the wrong word.

    One of my friend works in a bank.

    Answer

    Wrong word: friend.

    Corrected: One of my friends works in a bank.

    After 'one of' the noun is always plural — friends. The verb is still singular — works.

    Exercise 10

    One word is wrong — catch it.

    The quality of these mangoes are very poor.

    Answer

    Wrong word: are.

    Corrected: The quality of these mangoes is very poor.

    The subject is 'the quality', one thing. Remove 'of these mangoes' and 'The quality is' is left.

    Exercise 11

    Match each subject with the correct verb.

    AB
    Everyoneis
    My parentsare
    The newswere
    My spectaclesknows
    A number of boysknow
    Answer

    Everyone — knows; My parents — know; The news — is; My spectacles — are; A number of boys — were

    Everyone is singular → knows. Parents is plural → know. News is singular → is. Spectacles is plural → are. 'A number of' is plural → were.

    Exercise 12

    Put the words in the correct order.

    Words: this / thirty / number / class / is / of / in / The / students

    Answer

    The number of students in this class is thirty

    'The number of' always takes a singular verb — is. 'A number of students' would take 'are'.

    Exercise 13

    Now say this line aloud — there are three agreements to handle.

    One of my friends has a shop and his brothers work there

    Answer

    Say: One of my friends has a shop and his brothers work there

    'One of my friends' is singular → has. 'His brothers' is plural → work. Two kinds of agreement in one sentence — saying it aloud builds the habit.

    What to do next

    When you write, read your text once with your finger on the verbs only — stop at each verb and ask "who is its subject?". This one-minute check will catch 90% of your agreement mistakes by itself.
