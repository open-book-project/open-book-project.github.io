    Chapter 48: Spelling rules¶

    Spelling rules — memorise less, understand more

    One wrong spelling in an email — recieve, seperate, definately — and the reader immediately thinks the writer was careless. The good news is that English spelling is not as crazy as it looks. A handful of rules handle most words, and we will memorise the leftover exceptions in one list.

    In one line

    Most English spelling follows a few rules about adding endings — double the consonant, drop the e, change y to i. Learn the rules first, then memorise the exceptions. Understand the rules first, then memorise only the exceptions — not the whole dictionary.

    48.1 When does a consonant double?¶

    stopped or stoped? beginning or begining? This question comes up in office emails every day. The answer is hidden in three small questions — follow the chart below once.

    The 1-1-1 rule

    One syllable, one vowel, one final consonant + a suffix that starts with a vowel = the consonant doubles: stop → stopped, big → bigger. In a longer word, only when the stress is on the last syllable: begin → beginning, but visit → visited.

    • We stopped the meeting at five. stop = one syllable, one vowel (o), one consonant (p) → stopped.
    • This is only the beginning. be-GIN — the stress is on the last syllable, so the n doubles: beginning.
    • She visited her parents on Sunday. VI-sit — the stress is on the first syllable, so the t stays single: visited.

    British twist — travel, cancel, label

    In British English, a final l always doubles, wherever the stress is: travelled, cancelled, labelled, modelling. American English keeps it single: traveled, canceled. India follows British English, so in school and at the office write travelled.

    48.2 When does the silent e drop?¶

    The last e of make is not pronounced — and that e causes the most mistakes when you add an ending. The rule is simple: look at the first letter of the suffix.

    The silent-e decision

    The suffix starts with a vowel (-ing, -able, -ed) → drop the e: make → making, use → usable. The suffix starts with a consonant (-ful, -ment, -ly) → keep the e: care → careful, move → movement.

    Word Vowel suffix (e dropped) Consonant suffix (e kept)
    make making —
    hope hoping hopeful
    care caring careful
    move moving movement
    use usable useful
    manage managing management
    achieve achieving achievement

    Exceptions you must memorise

    Words with c / g keep the e so the sound stays soft: noticeable, manageable, courageous.
    The e drops for no obvious reason: truly, argument, ninth, awful.
    Words ending in ee do not drop the e: seeing, agreeing, fleeing.

    48.3 When does y become i?¶

    happyness or happiness? studys or studies? Just look at the letter right before the y — that gives you the answer.

    The y rule

    Consonant + y → i: happy → happiness, study → studied. Vowel + y → y stays: play → played. And before -ing the y never changes: studying, carrying.

    • She studies late and still looks happier than all of us. stud-y → studies (d is a consonant); happ-y → happier.
    • The children enjoyed the play. enjo-y → o is a vowel, so the y stays: enjoyed.

    Four old exceptions

    day → daily, pay → paid, say → said, lay → laid. These have vowel + y, yet the y still became i — just memorise them.

    48.4 i before e — and its exceptions¶

    receive or recieve, believe or beleive — mixing up ie and ei is the most common spelling mistake among Indian writers. The old rhyme still works; you just need to remember all of it.

    ie or ei — a three-line trick

    The "ee" sound → ie: believe, piece, achieve.
    Right after c → ei: receive, ceiling, receipt.
    The "ay" sound → ei: eight, weigh, neighbour.

    When Spelling Examples
    The "ee" sound ie believe, achieve, piece, field, chief, niece, brief
    After c, with the "ee" sound ei receive, receipt, ceiling, deceive, perceive
    The "ay" sound ei eight, weight, neighbour, vein, reign
    Exceptions — learn by heart ei seize, weird, either, neither, protein, foreign, leisure, their
    After c but still ie (the sound is not "ee") ie science, ancient, efficient, society
    • I received the receipt from my neighbour. receive, receipt — ei after c. neighbour — the "ay" sound, so ei.

    48.5 -able or -ible¶

    acceptable or acceptible? Both sound the same, so your ear does not help. But there is a simple test that works for most words.

    The -able / -ible test

    When you remove the suffix and a whole word is left → -able (acceptable, comfortable). When only a broken piece is left → -ible (possible, visible, terrible). This test works for most words.

    Some words break the test

    responsible, flexible, accessible, sensible — whole words are hidden inside them, yet they take -ible. And noticeable, changeable keep the e so that the c/g sound stays soft. Memorise these six separately.

    48.6 British or American — colour or color¶

    There are two spelling systems, and both are correct. But in India, schools, board exams, government work and most newspapers follow British spelling. Trouble starts when software quietly switches to American spelling.

    Pattern British (India) American
    -our / -or colour, favour, behaviour color, favor, behavior
    -re / -er centre, metre, theatre center, meter, theater
    -ise / -ize organise, realise, apologise organize, realize, apologize
    -ll / -l travelled, cancelled, jewellery traveled, canceled, jewelry
    -ogue / -og catalogue, dialogue catalog, dialog
    -ce / -se licence (noun), defence license, defense
    -yse / -yze analyse, paralyse analyze, paralyze
    Different words programme, cheque, tyre, grey program, check, tire, gray

    The real mistake is mixing them

    Writing "color" is not a mistake — the mistake is writing both colour and color in one email. In Word or Google Docs, set the language to English (India) or English (UK). For computers, program is used in both systems, and -ize is not wrong in British English either — just pick one system and keep it throughout the document.

    48.7 The 30 words we misspell most¶

    This list comes from mistakes that really show up in Indian offices, colleges and job applications. Each word has a small trick — if you remember the trick, you will remember the spelling.

    The double-letter game¶

    Correct Common mistake How to remember
    accommodation accomodation Two c's, two m's — the room is big enough to fit both doubles.
    occurrence occurence Two c's, two r's, and -ence at the end.
    recommend reccomend re + commend: one c, two m's.
    necessary neccessary A shirt has one Collar and two Sleeves — one c, two s's.
    committee commitee Everything doubles: mm, tt, ee.
    embarrass embarass So much shame that you need two r's and two s's.
    beginning begining be-GIN + n + ing — the 1-1-1 rule.
    tomorrow tommorow to + morrow: one m, two r's.

    Vowel trouble¶

    Correct Common mistake How to remember
    separate seperate "There is a rat in separate."
    definitely definately "Finite" is hiding inside: de-FINITE-ly.
    privilege priviledge No d, no a: pri-vi-lege.
    grammar grammer It ends in -ar; the word has two a's.
    calendar calender It ends in -ar, just like grammar.
    independent independant It ends in -ent: in-de-pen-dent.
    existence existance exist + ence — all the vowels are e.
    maintenance maintainance It comes from maintain, but the "ai" is lost: main-te-nance.

    ie/ei and hidden letters¶

    Correct Common mistake How to remember
    receive recieve Right after c comes e-i.
    achieve acheive The "ee" sound and no c — so i-e.
    believe beleive "Never believe a lie."
    foreign foriegn It is an exception — ei. Learn it by heart.
    government goverment govern + ment — don't leave out the n.
    environment enviroment environ + ment — the n is here too.
    Wednesday Wensday Say it in your head as you write: Wed-nes-day.

    Special ones for Indian English¶

    Correct Common mistake How to remember
    until untill till has two l's, until has only one.
    across accross a + cross — only one c.
    truly truely The e of true drops (an exception).
    pronunciation pronounciation pronounce has "ou", but pronunciation has only "u".
    argument arguement The e of argue drops.
    colleague collegue col + league — the same spelling as a cricket league.
    guarantee gaurantee g-u-a: u first, then a.

    Five words a week

    Don't try to memorise the whole list in one day. Pick five words each week, write each one five times by hand while saying it aloud, and use each of them at least once in that week's emails. The list is finished in six weeks.

    48.8 The mistakes we really make¶

    These sentences are like real office emails and applications. Each one has a spelling mistake — and behind each mistake is a rule we have already studied above.

    Real spelling mistakes from offices and colleges

    • Wrong: I am writting to inform you about the delay.
    • Right: I am writing to inform you about the delay.

    • Why: The silent e of write drops → writing (one t). written has two t's — that is a different word.

    • Wrong: We will definately recieve the payment by Monday.

    • Right: We will definitely receive the payment by Monday.

    • Why: de-FINITE-ly. And after c it is always e-i: receive.

    • Wrong: Kindly accomodate my request for a seperate room.

    • Right: Please accommodate my request for a separate room.

    • Why: accommodate has two c's and two m's. "A rat" is hiding in separate.

    • Wrong: It was a priviledge to work with such a commited team.

    • Right: It was a privilege to work with such a committed team.

    • Why: privilege has no d. com-MIT — the stress is on the last syllable, so the t doubles: committed.

    • Wrong: I do not want to loose this oppurtunity.

    • Right: I do not want to lose this opportunity.
    • Why: lose = to no longer have, loose = not tight. op-por-tu-ni-ty — it begins with "port", with two p's.

    Spell-check doesn't catch everything

    loose/lose, their/there, its/it's, then/than, affect/effect — these are all real words, so no red line appears. Before sending an email, read it aloud once; the ear catches what the eye misses.

    48.9 Practice — Spelling¶

    Exercise 1

    One spelling in this sentence is wrong — tap that word.

    We will definately send the report today.

    Answer

    Wrong word: definately.

    Corrected: We will definitely send the report today.

    'Finite' is hiding inside: de-FINITE-ly. There is no 'a' anywhere in this word.

    Exercise 2

    Find the word with the wrong spelling.

    I have made a seperate folder for each client.

    Answer

    Wrong word: seperate.

    Corrected: I have made a separate folder for each client.

    'There is a rat in separate' — sep-A-rate. The middle letter is a, not e.

    Exercise 3

    One word is spelled wrongly — find it.

    The hotel could not give accomodation to all the guests.

    Answer

    Wrong word: accomodation.

    Corrected: The hotel could not give accommodation to all the guests.

    accommodation has two c's and two m's. Leaving out one m is the most common mistake.

    Exercise 4

    Tap the wrong word.

    Did you recieve my email about the meeting?

    Answer

    Wrong word: recieve.

    Corrected: Did you receive my email about the meeting?

    Right after c comes e-i: receive, receipt, ceiling.

    Exercise 5

    Which word doubles its final consonant when you add -ing?

    1. begin
    2. visit
    3. open
    4. happen

    Hint: Say each word aloud — where does the stress fall?

    Answer

    begin

    In be-GIN the stress is on the last syllable, so beginning. In VI-sit, O-pen and HAP-pen the stress is on the first syllable — so visiting, opening, happening, with a single consonant.

    Exercise 6

    Which spelling is correct?

    1. comfortible
    2. visible
    3. acceptible
    4. dependible
    Answer

    visible

    'vis' alone is not a word, so -ible. comfort, accept and depend are whole words — they take -able: comfortable, acceptable, dependable.

    Exercise 7

    Which spelling system is used in Indian schools, board exams and government work?

    1. British
    2. American
    3. Both mixed in one document
    4. There is no rule
    Answer

    British

    India uses British spelling — colour, centre, organise, travelled. American is not wrong, but keep to one system in one document.

    Exercise 8

    In American English, 'centre' is written ___.

    Answer

    center

    British -re, American -er: centre/center, metre/meter, theatre/theater.

    Exercise 9

    The silent e drops when the suffix starts with a ___ — as in make + ing = making.

    Hint: Consonant or vowel?

    Answer

    vowel

    If the suffix starts with a vowel (-ing, -able, -ed), the e drops: making, usable. If it starts with a consonant (-ful, -ment), the e stays: careful, movement.

    Exercise 10

    Match each word + suffix to its correct form.

    AB
    happy + nessstopped
    make + ingcareful
    stop + edplayed
    care + fulhappiness
    play + edmaking
    Answer

    happy + ness — happiness; make + ing — making; stop + ed — stopped; care + ful — careful; play + ed — played

    happy: consonant + y, so y → i. make: a vowel suffix, so the e drops. stop: 1-1-1, so it doubles. care: a consonant suffix, so the e stays. play: vowel + y, so the y stays.

    Exercise 11

    Put the words in the right order to make a sentence.

    Words: this / colleagues / to / definitely / recommend / my / would / I / hotel

    Answer

    I would definitely recommend this hotel to my colleagues

    Subject (I) + would + adverb (definitely) + verb (recommend) + object. Watch the spelling too: definitely, recommend (one c, two m's), colleagues.

    Exercise 12

    Now say this line — pronounce each hard word clearly, in parts.

    It is necessary to separate the old receipts

    Answer

    Say: It is necessary to separate the old receipts

    Remember the parts: NE-ces-sa-ry, SEP-a-rate, re-CEIPT (the p is silent). Saying words in parts also fixes the spelling in your mind.

    What to do next

    Make a list called "my spellings" in your phone's notes. Whenever you misspell a word and spell-check catches it, add it to the list. By the end of the month, that list will be your most useful dictionary.
