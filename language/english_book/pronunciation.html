    Chapter 26: Pronunciation¶

    Fix your pronunciation

    You do not need to change your accent — there is nothing wrong with an Indian accent. What needs fixing is the six or seven sounds that come out from the wrong place, because those are the sounds that make people misunderstand some words. This chapter gives clear instructions for each sound: where the lips go, where the tongue goes, and whether it is voiced or just air. Read, and do it right away. Reading and then leaving it is useless.

    The three-step method — the same for every sound

    1. Press 🔊 and listen. 2. Read the instructions and put your mouth in that position — with no voice, just the position. 3. Now make the sound, slowly, five times. Speaking fast is not the goal. Speaking clearly is the goal. Speed comes by itself.

    26.1 V and W — the biggest battle¶

    Hindi has only one "व". English has two completely different sounds, and each one uses a different mouth shape. Fix just this one thing and your English will sound clearer at once — because these sounds come up a hundred times a day.

    V — how to make it

    Put your upper teeth on your lower lip — press lightly, as if you were biting the lip, but very gently. Now make a sound from the throat: vvvvv. The air rubs out between the teeth and the lip. The lips are not round. Put a finger on your throat — you should feel a vibration.

    W — how to make it

    The teeth do not touch anything. Make both lips round — as if you were about to say "oo", or about to blow out a candle. Then start the sound from that round shape and open your lips as you go: oo-wet. W is a gliding sound, not a rubbing sound.

    Minimal pairs — V versus W¶

    Play both, hear the difference, then say them yourself. This is the real test.

    V Listen W Listen Meaning
    vet wet animal doctor / not dry
    vine wine a climbing plant / an alcoholic drink
    vest west undershirt / the direction
    veil whale a face covering / a huge sea animal
    verse worse a line of poetry / more bad
    vary wary to change / careful
    • We were very worried about the weather. Three W sounds and one V in the same line. Teeth on the lip for "very", round lips for the other three.
    • The vet gave the dog water. Teeth for "vet" and "gave"; round lips for "water". Both sounds in one sentence.

    Careful — the opposite mistake happens too

    Many people try so hard to fix V that they use their teeth everywhere — "vater" for "water", "vindow", "ve are". This sounds even stranger than the first mistake. The rule is simple: where the spelling has W, say W. Where it has V, say V.

    26.2 TH — two sounds, one position¶

    For TH the tongue has to come out a little — this feels odd to many people, but it is correct. Hindi "थ" and "ध" are both made behind the teeth; English TH is made between the teeth.

    TH — step by step

    1. Put the thin tip of your tongue just out past your upper front teeth — only 2-3 millimetres, do not let the tongue hang out.
    2. Let the teeth touch the tongue lightly.
    3. Now blow air out: thhh — this is the think TH (air only).
    4. Keep the same position, but now add voice from the throat: dhhh — this is the this TH (vibration).

    A sure test

    Hold your hand in front of your mouth. Say "think" — you should feel a puff of air. If you do not, you are saying "tink" or "sink", not "think".

    TH (air only) Listen What comes out by mistake Listen
    think sink (basin)
    three tree (a plant)
    thin tin (a metal can)
    thank tank (water tank)
    mouth mouse (the animal)
    path pass
    TH (with vibration) Listen Note
    this, that, these, those The four most common words, every day — work on these first.
    the Not "da" — tongue between the teeth, then a light "uh".
    they, them, their "de, dem, der" is the most common Indian pattern. Put your tongue out.
    mother, father, brother, other The TH in the middle of a word is the one most often dropped.
    • I think this is the third thing. Four TH sounds in one line — two with air only (think, third, thing) and two with vibration (this, the).
    • My brother and my mother both thanked them. A drill for the TH in the middle of words. Say it slowly — tongue out on every TH.

    26.3 P and F — Hindi "फ" sits in the middle¶

    Hindi "फ" is made with both lips. English F is made with the teeth and lip. That is why "coffee" sometimes sounds like "copy", and "phone" sometimes sounds like "pone".

    F vs P — the difference in position

    P: close both lips completely, hold the air back, then release it suddenly — pop! You feel a push of air.
    F: upper teeth on the lower lip (exactly like V), but with no voice — just a long, thin stream of air: ffff. The lips do not close, and the air never stops.

    P Listen F Listen Meaning
    pan fan a cooking pan / a ceiling fan
    pin fin a pin / a fish's fin
    copy coffee to copy / the drink
    pool fool a pond or swimming pool / a silly person
    supper suffer the evening meal / to feel pain or trouble
    • Please put the fan near the phone. Both lips for "please" and "put"; teeth and lip for "fan" and "phone". All three in one line.

    26.4 S and SH — how far back the tongue goes¶

    S vs SH

    S: the tongue tip is just behind the upper teeth, the lips are wide (like a slight smile). Thin, sharp air: ssss — like a snake.
    SH: move the tongue back a little (towards the roof of the mouth) and round the lips, pushing them forward. Thick, heavy air: shhh — like asking someone to be quiet.

    S Listen SH Listen Meaning
    sea she the ocean / that woman
    sip ship a small mouthful of a drink / a big boat
    save shave to rescue or keep / to cut off hair
    sort short to arrange in groups / not long
    seat sheet a place to sit / a bed sheet or sheet of paper

    26.5 Z and J — a buzz vs a jerk¶

    /z/ does not exist in Hindi, so we say either "s" or "j". Both are wrong.

    Z vs J — one continuous, one sudden

    Z: exactly the same position as /s/ — tongue behind the teeth — but add the throat vibration. The sound keeps going: zzzzzz, as long as your breath lasts.
    J: press the tongue against the roof of the mouth to stop the air, then release it suddenly — there is a jerk. You cannot stretch the sound. Test: you can stretch "zzzz" for 5 seconds. You cannot stretch "j".

    Z Listen J / S Listen Note
    zoo Jew Z can be stretched, J is a jerk
    buzz bus to hum like a bee / a bus
    prize price a reward / what something costs — used every day
    eyes ice what you see with / frozen water
    rise rice to go up / the grain

    A small rule that helps a lot

    The plural and third-person "s" is very often /z/, not "s": dogs = dogz, jobs = jobz, plays = playz, comes = comz, is = iz, was = woz. If the sound before it has a throat vibration, the "s" becomes /z/ too.

    26.6 "school" — not "iskool"¶

    Hindi words do not start with two consonants, so our tongue adds a small "i" in front to make it easier. The cure is simple, and it works in 5 minutes:

    The three-step cure

    Step 1: Say just ssssssss for 3 seconds — no vowel.
    Step 2: Without breaking the breath, add the rest: sssss-kool.
    Step 3: Slowly make the "sss" shorter: sss-kool → ss-kool → school. The real trick is not to break the breath in the middle. Breath broken = an "i" sneaks in.

    Wrong Right Listen
    iskool school
    istation station
    istudy study
    ispeak / isport speak / sport
    istreet street
    iskin skin
    filim film
    pleeze / pilees please
    • I study at a school near the station. Three "s" clusters — study, school, station. Not a single "i" added.
    • The street is small but the sky is clear. street, small, sky — all three have joined consonants. Do not break the breath.

    26.10 Practice — Pronunciation¶

    Exercise 1

    When you say W, where are your teeth?

    1. They touch nothing — the lips are round
    2. Upper teeth on the lower lip
    3. Tongue between the teeth
    4. Both lips closed

    Hint: What would you see in a mirror?

    Answer

    They touch nothing — the lips are round

    W does not use the teeth. The moment the teeth touch the lip, it turns into V.

    Exercise 2

    When you say 'think' with your hand in front of your mouth, what should you feel?

    1. A puff of air
    2. Nothing
    3. Only a throat vibration
    4. A push from the lips
    Answer

    A puff of air

    For /θ/ the air comes out between the teeth and the tongue. No air = you said 'tink' or 'sink'.

    Exercise 3

    What is the difference between English F and Hindi 'फ'?

    1. F uses teeth on the lip; 'फ' uses both lips
    2. There is no difference
    3. F puts the tongue between the teeth
    4. F has a throat vibration
    Answer

    F uses teeth on the lip; 'फ' uses both lips

    That is why 'coffee' sometimes sounds like 'copy'. F has the same position as V, just with no voice — only air.

    Exercise 4

    What is the easiest test to tell Z and J apart?

    1. You can stretch Z for 5 seconds, but not J
    2. J has a throat vibration, Z does not
    3. Z has round lips
    4. J puts the tongue between the teeth

    Hint: You can stretch one sound but not the other.

    Answer

    You can stretch Z for 5 seconds, but not J

    Z is a continuous buzz; J is a jerk where the air stops first.

    Exercise 5

    What sound is the last 's' of 'dogs' really?

    1. /z/ — dogz
    2. /s/ — dogs
    3. It is silent
    4. /sh/
    Answer

    /z/ — dogz

    The sound before it (g) has a throat vibration, so the 's' becomes /z/ too: jobz, playz, comz.

    Exercise 6

    What is the right way to lose the 'iskool' habit?

    1. Stretch 'sss', then add 'kool' without breaking the breath
    2. Say 'school' faster
    3. Say 'i' first, then drop it
    4. Keep the 'k' silent
    Answer

    Stretch 'sss', then add 'kool' without breaking the breath

    A vowel sneaks in the moment the breath breaks. Join it in one breath: sss-kool, then make it shorter.

    Exercise 7

    One word in this sentence is written with a wrong pronunciation — tap it.

    My brother studies in a iskool near my house.

    Answer

    Wrong word: iskool.

    Corrected: My brother studies in a school near my house.

    school — starts straight with 's'. The extra 'i' in front is a Hindi habit.

    Exercise 8

    Find the word that is written wrongly.

    We are going to see a filim tonight.

    Answer

    Wrong word: filim.

    Corrected: We are going to see a film tonight.

    film — 'l' and 'm' together, with no 'i' in between. Say it slowly: fi-lm.

    Exercise 9

    Match the minimal pairs to their meanings.

    AB
    vetthe number 3
    weta tall plant
    threeanimal doctor
    treenot dry
    Answer

    vet — animal doctor; wet — not dry; three — the number 3; tree — a tall plant

    One sound of difference, and the whole meaning changes. That is why these pairs need practice.

    Exercise 10

    Match each word to how it sounds with its silent letter.

    AB
    knowwok
    thumbri-seet
    walkno
    receipttham
    Answer

    know — no; thumb — tham; walk — wok; receipt — ri-seet

    K, B, L and P — all four are silent. You do not have to say every letter that is written.

    Exercise 11

    V and W together. Look in the mirror — where should you see the teeth?

    We were very worried about the weather

    Answer

    Say: We were very worried about the weather

    On 'very', upper teeth on the lower lip. On 'we, were, worried, weather', round lips and no teeth.

    Exercise 12

    TH drill. Tongue out between the teeth.

    I think this is the third thing

    Answer

    Say: I think this is the third thing

    think, third, thing = air only. this, the = throat vibration. The position is the same for both.

    Exercise 13

    V and F — both use teeth on the lip, but one has voice.

    Very few of my friends have a van

    Answer

    Say: Very few of my friends have a van

    very, have, van have vibration (V). few, friends have air only (F).

    Exercise 14

    The S and SH difference. Move the tongue back and round the lips for SH.

    She sells sheets by the sea

    Answer

    Say: She sells sheets by the sea

    She, sheets have SH (round lips). sells, sea have S (wide lips, thin air).

    Exercise 15

    /z/ drill. Put a finger on your throat while you speak.

    The prize is zero rupees

    Answer

    Say: The prize is zero rupees

    prize, is, zero, rupees — all four have /z/. If you said 'price', the meaning changed.

    Exercise 16

    Joined consonants — do not add any 'i' at all.

    I study at a school near the station

    Answer

    Say: I study at a school near the station

    study, school, station — all start straight with 's'. Join them in one breath.

    Exercise 17

    The final exam — all the sounds together.

    Three of those women work in a very small school

    Answer

    Say: Three of those women work in a very small school

    Three (air TH), those (voiced TH), women (W), very (V), small and school (s- clusters). Say it slowly and say all of it.

    What next?

    One thing to remember: even when your sounds are right, if you say every word with equal force, your English will still sound robotic. The next chapter — Rhythm and stress — is the part that gives you the real "fluent" feeling.
