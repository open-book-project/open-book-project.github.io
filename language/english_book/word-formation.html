    Chapter 36: Word formation¶

    One word, ten words — the magic of prefixes and suffixes

    The cheapest way to grow your vocabulary is not memorising new words — it is spotting the relatives of the words you already know. Do you know happy? Then you also know unhappy, happiness, happily, unhappiness — you just did not realise it. Pieces added at the start (prefixes) change the meaning, and pieces added at the end (suffixes) change the job of the word. After this chapter, every long new word will look like a set of small pieces.

    36.1 One root, a whole family — word family¶

    Learning a word together with its family has two benefits: you get four words for the effort of one, and it becomes easy to pick the right form in a sentence. Mistakes like "He spoke happy" or "Her happy was clear" stop right here.

    • She looks happy. She smiled happily. Her happiness was clear. One root, three jobs: how she looks (adjective), how she smiled (adverb), what we could see (noun).

    Two kinds of pieces

    A prefix (at the start) changes the meaning of a word: happy → unhappy. A suffix (at the end) changes the job of a word: happy (adj) → happiness (noun) → happily (adverb).

    36.2 Prefixes — add at the start, change the meaning¶

    Seven prefixes are so common that hundreds of everyday English words are built from them. Once you remember what they mean, you can guess unknown words too — misread = to read wrongly.

    Prefix Meaning Examples
    un- not / opposite unhappy · unfair · unlock
    dis- not / opposite dislike · disagree · disconnect
    im- / in- not impossible · incorrect · invisible
    re- again / back redo · rewrite · return
    mis- wrongly misunderstand · misspell · mislead
    pre- before preview · prepaid · preheat
    over- too much overeat · overload · overconfident
    • I think you misunderstood me. Let me rephrase that. = You understood me wrongly. I will say it again in different words.
    • Don't overcook the rice, and please preheat the oven. = Do not cook the rice too long, and heat the oven first.

    A prefix never changes the spelling

    A prefix is simply stuck on the front — the spelling of the root stays the same. That is why two identical letters sometimes meet: un + necessary = unnecessary, mis + spell = misspell, dis + satisfied = dissatisfied.

    36.3 Which "not" — un, dis, in, im, il, ir¶

    There are six prefixes that mean "not", and they cause the most confusion — "unpossible", "unregular". The good news: half of them follow a firm rule, and that rule only looks at the first letter of the root.

    The first-letter rule

    p, m, b → im- (impossible) · l → il- (illegal) · r → ir- (irregular). There is no rule for un- / dis- / in- — learn them as pairs with each word. If in doubt, check a dictionary — a word like "unpossible" does not exist.

    36.4 Suffixes that make nouns — -tion, -ment, -ness¶

    Formal English, emails and reports are full of nouns — "We made a decision" instead of "We decided". Three suffixes make most nouns: the name of an action (-tion, -ment) and the name of a quality (-ness).

    Word → noun Suffix Example
    inform → information -tion Thanks for the information.
    educate → education -tion Education changes lives.
    decide → decision -sion It was a hard decision.
    develop → development -ment The city needs development.
    agree → agreement -ment We signed an agreement.
    pay → payment -ment The payment is due today.
    kind → kindness -ness Thank you for your kindness.
    happy → happiness -ness Money can't buy happiness.

    Name of an action, name of a quality

    Verb + -tion / -ment = noun (inform → information, agree → agreement). Adjective + -ness = noun (kind → kindness). A final y becomes i before -ness: happy → happiness. Also, a silent final "e" often drops: educate → education, decide → decision.

    36.5 Adjectives and adverbs — -ful, -less, -able, -ly¶

    These four suffixes are the heart of description. One noun can give two opposite adjectives (-ful and -less), and if you add -ly to them, they can describe a verb.

    Suffix Meaning Examples
    -ful full of careful · useful · hopeful
    -less without careless · useless · hopeless
    -able can be done readable · washable · comfortable
    -ly adjective → adverb quickly · carefully · happily

    One l, two l's

    -ful always has only one l: careful, useful, beautiful — never "carefull". But when you add -ly, two l's appear: careful + ly = carefully. Watch out: friendly, lovely, lonely end in -ly but they are adjectives — "a friendly person".

    36.6 -ize and some old roots¶

    -ize turns a noun or an adjective into an action (a verb) — you see it everywhere in office English. Many long words are also built from old Latin roots; once you recognise one root, a whole bunch of words makes sense.

    Word + -ize = verb Example
    modern modernize We need to modernize the office.
    real realize I didn't realize it was so late.
    summary summarize Can you summarize the report?
    priority prioritize Let's prioritize the urgent tasks.
    apology apologize I apologize for the delay.

    The British spelling -ise (realise, apologise) is also perfectly correct, and both are used in India. Just keep to one style in a document.

    Root Meaning Words
    port carry transport · export · portable
    dict say predict · dictionary · contradict
    spect look carefully inspect · respect · spectator
    vis / vid see visible · vision · video
    scrib / script write describe · script · subscribe

    Long word? Break it up

    Prefix + root + suffix: in-spect-ion = the act of looking inside (a check); pre-dict-able = can be said in advance. Break an unknown word into pieces — half of the meaning will appear by itself.

    36.7 Six families — in one table¶

    These are the families whose forms are most often used wrongly. Read each line aloud from left to right — you will learn four words in one go.

    Verb Noun Adjective Adverb
    decide decision decisive decisively
    succeed success successful successfully
    create creation creative creatively
    care care careful carefully
    differ difference different differently
    attract attraction attractive attractively
    • She succeeded because she worked carefully. Her success was no accident. Three family members, three jobs — the adverb goes with the verb, the noun stands alone.

    The notebook habit

    When you write down a new word, write its whole family next to it — verb, noun, adjective, adverb. A box that stays empty (like a common adverb for "differ") is useful information too.

    36.8 The mistakes we really make¶

    These mistakes happen because we choose the right root but the wrong form — a verb instead of a noun, an adjective instead of an adverb, or the wrong "not" prefix.

    Common word-formation mistakes

    • Wrong: This is unpossible.
    • Right: This is impossible.

    • Why: "possible" starts with p → im-. In the same way unregular → irregular, unlegal → illegal.

    • Wrong: Her behave in the meeting was rude.

    • Right: Her behaviour in the meeting was rude.

    • Why: A noun is needed after "Her ___ was". behave = verb, behaviour (US: behavior) = noun.

    • Wrong: He answered very confident.

    • Right: He answered very confidently.

    • Why: To describe a verb (answered) you need an adverb: -ly. The adjective is fine in "He is confident".

    • Wrong: I am very boring in this class.

    • Right: I am very bored in this class.

    • Why: -ed = how you feel (bored, tired, interested). -ing = what causes the feeling (a boring class). "I am boring" means I am a boring person!

    • Wrong: Can you repeat it again?

    • Right: Can you repeat it? / Can you say it again?
    • Why: re- already means "again" — "repeat again" is "again again". In the same way, "back" is unnecessary in "revert back".

    36.9 Practice — Word formation¶

    Exercise 1

    What is the opposite of 'possible'?

    1. impossible
    2. unpossible
    3. dispossible
    4. nonpossible
    Answer

    impossible

    The root starts with 'p', so it takes im-. Remember: p, m, b → im-.

    Exercise 2

    What is the opposite of 'regular'?

    1. irregular
    2. unregular
    3. disregular
    4. misregular
    Answer

    irregular

    The root starts with 'r' → ir-. In the same way: irrelevant, irresponsible.

    Exercise 3

    Which of these is an adverb?

    1. happily
    2. happiness
    3. unhappy
    4. happy
    Answer

    happily

    -ly turns an adjective into an adverb: happy → happily. happiness is a noun, unhappy is an adjective.

    Exercise 4

    Please drive ___. The road is wet. (care)

    Hint: It describes the verb 'drive' — you need an adverb.

    Answer

    carefully

    care → careful (adjective) → carefully (adverb). Two l's, because careful + ly.

    Exercise 5

    We need to make a ___ by Friday. (decide)

    Hint: A noun comes after 'a'.

    Answer

    decision

    decide → decision. The final 'de' becomes '-sion'. 'make a decision' is a fixed pair.

    Exercise 6

    Thank you for your ___. (kind)

    Hint: A noun comes after 'your' — the name of a quality.

    Answer

    kindness

    Adjective + -ness = noun: kind → kindness, dark → darkness.

    Exercise 7

    One word in this sentence has the wrong form. Tap it.

    Her behave in the meeting was very rude.

    Answer

    Wrong word: behave.

    Corrected: Her behaviour in the meeting was very rude.

    A noun is needed after 'Her ___ was' — behave is a verb, behaviour is a noun.

    Exercise 8

    Find the word with the wrong prefix.

    Finishing this by tonight is unpossible.

    Answer

    Wrong word: unpossible.

    Corrected: Finishing this by tonight is impossible.

    'possible' starts with p → im-possible. The word 'unpossible' does not exist.

    Exercise 9

    Match each prefix with its meaning.

    AB
    re-before
    mis-too much
    pre-again
    over-wrongly
    Answer

    re- — again; mis- — wrongly; pre- — before; over- — too much

    rewrite = write again, misread = read wrongly, preview = see before, overeat = eat too much.

    Exercise 10

    Match each root with its meaning.

    AB
    portlook
    dictwrite
    spectcarry
    scribsay
    Answer

    port — carry; dict — say; spect — look; scrib — write

    transport (carry), predict (say before), inspect (look inside), describe (write to explain).

    Exercise 11

    Put the words in the right order.

    Words: all / confidently / She / questions / the / answered

    Answer

    She answered all the questions confidently

    An adverb (confidently) describes the action of the verb, and it often comes at the end of the sentence.

    Exercise 12

    Say this sentence — pronounce the full suffix of each word clearly.

    It was a careful and creative decision

    Answer

    Say: It was a careful and creative decision

    Say the -ful in 'careful' lightly (kair-fl), and stress the middle of 'decision': di-SI-zhn.

    What to do next

    Pick five long words from today's newspaper or any article and break them into prefix + root + suffix. Then write out the family of each one. Ten minutes, and your vocabulary quietly grows.
