    Chapter 4: Silent letters & liaison¶

    Lettres muettes, liaison, élision

    French is famous for writing letters it does not say. Ils parlent has ten letters but sounds like [eel PARL]. Then, just when you have learned to keep a letter silent, it comes back to life before a vowel: ils ont sounds like [eel-ZÕ]. This chapter gives you the rules, so you can read any French word aloud with confidence.

    masculinefeminineplural

    The big idea

    French hates a gap between two vowels. So words join up: a silent final consonant may wake up (liaison), a spoken one slides onto the next word (enchaînement), or a vowel drops out (élision: l’ami).

    4.1 Most final consonants are silent¶

    At the end of a word, most consonants are not pronounced. In this book a silent letter is greyed and struck through: petit.

    • petit, grand, trop — small, big, too much [puh-TEE, GRÃ, TRO]
    • vous, le nez, beaucoup — you, the nose, a lot [VOO, luh NAY, bo-KOO]
    • Paris, le chocolat, le riz — Paris, chocolate, rice [pa-REE, sho-ko-LA, luh REE]

    Three silent endings you will see everywhere¶

    Ending Where Example
    final -e almost every word la table, la porte
    plural -s / -x nouns, adjectives les livres, les cheveux
    verb -ent ils / elles ils parlent, elles mangent

    A silent final -e still matters: it makes the consonant before it pronounced. Compare petit [puh-TEE] with petite [puh-TEET]. This is how you hear the feminine.

    • un petit garçon / une petite fille — a little boy / a little girl [puh-TEE / puh-TEET]
    • il est grand / elle est grande — he is tall / she is tall [GRÃ / GRÃD]

    -ent: silent only on verbs

    ils parlent = [eel PARL], but in nouns and adverbs -ent is the nasal [ã]: le vent [VÃ], souvent [soo-VÃ], le moment [mo-MÃ].

    Careful

    • Wrong: Ils parlent as “eel par-LENT”.
    • Right: Ils parlent = [eel PARL] — exactly like il parle.
    • Why: The verb ending -ent is never pronounced. Only the context (or a liaison) tells you it is plural.

    4.2 The CaReFuL rule — and its exceptions¶

    Four final consonants are usually pronounced: the consonants in the English word CaReFuL — c, r, f, l.

    CaReFuL

    Final c, r, f, l are usually pronounced: avec, la mer, neuf, le sel. Most other final consonants are silent.

    • avec, le sac, le parc — with, the bag, the park — final c [a-VEK, SAK, PARK]
    • la mer, cher, le soir, finir — the sea, expensive, the evening, to finish — final r [MEHR, SHEHR, SWAR, fee-NEER]
    • le chef, neuf, actif — the boss/chef, nine/new, active — final f [SHEF, NÖF, ak-TEEF]
    • le sel, l’hôtel, avril — salt, the hotel, April — final l [SEL, lo-TEL, a-VREEL]

    The exceptions to learn¶

    Case Silent letter Examples
    -er verbs and most -er/-ier words r parler, le boulanger, premier, le papier
    some words in -c c blanc, le banc, le tabac, le porc
    a few words in -l l gentil, l’outil, le fusil
    plural of œuf, bœuf f (and s) un œuf / des œufs

    Short words in -er often keep the r: la mer, cher, fier (proud), hier (yesterday), l’hiver (winter).

    Other final consonants you do hear¶

    • le bus, le tennis, le fils, le sens — the bus, tennis, the son, the meaning — final s heard [BEWSS, tay-NEESS, FEESS, SÃSS]
    • le sud, l’est, l’ouest, sept — south, east, west, seven — final d/t heard [SEWD, LEST, LWEST, SET]

    The numbers cinq, six, huit, dix have their own rules — see Numbers, dates & time.

    4.3 The e that disappears (e muet)¶

    The plain e [uh] is called e muet or e caduc (“falling e”) because in normal speech it often drops out. You still write it — but you may skip it when you speak.

    • samedi, acheter, maintenant — Saturday, to buy, now [sam-DEE, ash-TAY, mɛ̃t-NÃ]
    • la fenêtre, un petit peu — the window, a little bit [laf-NETR, ɛ̃ ptee PÖ]
    • Je ne sais pas. — I don't know. — in fast speech: [zhuhn seh PA], even [shay PA]

    The three-consonant rule

    Drop the e muet only if it leaves two consonants together. If dropping it would join three, keep it: vendredi [vã-druh-DEE], une fenêtre [ewn fuh-NETR].

    • vendredi, mercredi — Friday, Wednesday — the e stays (d-r-d, k-r-d) [vã-druh-DEE, mehr-kruh-DEE]

    As a beginner, you can always pronounce the e softly — you will be understood. But expect to hear it dropped: that is why spoken French sounds faster than the page.

    4.4 Liaison: when a silent letter wakes up¶

    A liaison happens when a word ends in a silent consonant and the next word begins with a vowel or a silent h. The silent consonant is pronounced at the start of the next word. In this book it is marked with ‿.

    • les livres / les‿amis — the books / the friends — s is silent before l, but says [z] before a [lay LEEVR / lay-za-MEE]
    • vous parlez / vous‿avez — you speak / you have [voo par-LAY / voo-za-VAY]
    Letter Liaison sound Example
    s, x, z [z] deux‿ans, chez‿elle
    t, d [t] petit‿ami, quand‿il pleut
    n [n] un‿ami, mon‿enfant, en‿Inde
    f [v] neuf‿heures

    The pair that changes meaning: ils sont / ils ont¶

    Liaison is not just style. Sometimes it is the only thing that separates two verbs:

    • Ils sont en Inde. — They are in India. — [s] [eel sõ-tã-NƐ̃D]
    • Ils‿ont un chat. — They have a cat. — [z] [eel-zõ-tɛ̃ SHA]
    • deux sœurs / deux‿heures — two sisters / two hours [dö SÖR / dö-ZÖR]

    Careful

    • Wrong: Ils ont said [eel SÕ].
    • Right: Ils‿ont = [eel-ZÕ].
    • Why: With [s] you are saying ils sont (they are). The liaison [z] means “they have”.

    4.5 Obligatory liaison: always link¶

    Liaison only happens inside a tight group of words that belong together. In these groups it is obligatory — leaving it out sounds wrong to any French speaker.

    Group Examples
    Article / number / my, this… + noun les‿enfants, un‿homme, mes‿amis, ces‿arbres, trois‿euros
    Adjective + noun un petit‿ami, les grands‿hôtels, de bons‿amis
    Pronoun + verb nous‿avons, vous‿êtes, ils‿aiment, on‿a
    Verb + pronoun (questions) Vont-ils ? Est-elle là ? Prend-il le bus ?
    Short preposition / adverb en‿Inde, chez‿elle, dans‿un café, très‿important
    Fixed expressions c’est‿à-dire, tout‿à fait, de temps‿en temps, les États‿Unis, Comment‿allez-vous ?
    • Nous‿avons deux‿enfants. — We have two children. [noo-za-võ dö-zã-FÃ]
    • Vous‿êtes en‿Inde ? — Are you in India? [voo-zet ã-NƐ̃D]
    • C’est un petit‿hôtel très‿agréable. — It's a very pleasant little hotel. [seh-tɛ̃ puh-tee-to-TEL treh-za-gray-ABL]
    • Mon‿ami habite chez‿elle. — My friend lives at her place. [mõ-na-MEE a-beet shay-ZEL]

    The question “-t-”

    When the verb ends in a vowel, French adds a -t- just to make the liaison: a-t-il ? [a-TEEL], parle-t-elle ?, y a-t-il ? More in Asking questions.

    4.6 Forbidden and optional liaison¶

    Forbidden: never link here¶

    Where Example
    After et (its t is never said) un garçon et | une fille
    After a singular noun un étudiant | anglais
    After a person’s or place name Paris | est beau. Louis | arrive.
    Before an h aspiré les | héros, des | haricots, en | haut
    Before onze, oui, huit les | onze joueurs, mais | oui, les | huit jours

    Careful

    • Wrong: les héros as [lay-zay-RO].
    • Right: les | héros = [lay ay-RO].
    • Why: h aspiré blocks the liaison. [lay-zay-RO] sounds like les zéros — the zeros!

    Careful

    • Wrong: et elle as [ay-TEL].
    • Right: et | elle = [ay EL].
    • Why: The t of et (and) is silent always, even before a vowel.

    Optional: your choice¶

    Between looser words — after a verb, after a plural noun, after pas or a long adverb — the liaison is optional. In everyday conversation people make few of them; in the news, speeches and poetry they make many. Both are correct.

    • Je suis allé. / Je suis‿allé. — I went. — [zhuh swee a-LAY] (everyday) or [zhuh swee-za-LAY] (careful)
    • Pas encore. / Pas‿encore. — Not yet. [pa ã-KOR] / [pa-zã-KOR]
    • C’est‿un ami. — He's a friend. — after c’est the liaison is technically optional but almost always made [seh-tɛ̃-na-MEE]

    Beginner strategy

    Make all the obligatory liaisons, avoid the forbidden ones, and skip the optional ones (except after c’est). You will sound natural and correct.

    4.7 Enchaînement: sliding a spoken consonant forward¶

    Enchaînement (“chaining”) is liaison’s cousin. Here the final consonant is already pronounced, and it simply slides onto the next vowel, so the syllable boundary moves. French speech flows as one long chain of syllables.

    • il a / elle est — he has / she is [ee-LA / eh-LEH]
    • avec elle, une amie — with her, a (female) friend [a-veh-KEL, ew-na-MEE]
    • Pierre arrive avec quatre amis. — Pierre is arriving with four friends. [pyeh-ra-reev a-vek ka-tra-MEE]
    • Il y a une école. — There is a school. [ee-lya ew-nay-KOL]
    Liaison Enchaînement
    Final consonant alone silent: les [lay] spoken: elle [el]
    Before a vowel wakes up: les‿amis slides over: elle‿est
    Can it be skipped? depends (section 5–6) never — always chain

    Do not put a stop or a little “h” between words, as English speakers do in “the | apple”. Even two vowels flow straight into each other: il a eu [ee-la-EW], “he has had”.

    4.8 Élision: l’, j’, qu’ and friends¶

    In élision, a short word loses its final vowel before another vowel or a silent h, and an apostrophe takes its place. Unlike optional liaison, élision is always written and always required.

    Word Becomes Example
    le, la l’ l’ami, l’école, l’homme
    je j’ j’aime, j’habite
    me, te, se m’, t’, s’ je m’appelle, tu t’appelles, il s’appelle
    ne n’ je n’aime pas
    de d’ d’accord, beaucoup d’amis
    que qu’ qu’est-ce que…, parce qu’il
    ce (+ être) c’ c’est, c’était
    si — only before il/ils s’ s’il vous plaît, s’ils viennent
    • J’habite à l’hôtel, c’est l’idéal ! — I'm living at the hotel — it's ideal! [zha-BEET a lo-TEL, seh lee-day-AL]
    • Je n’aime pas l’eau froide. — I don't like cold water. [zhuh nem pa lo FRWAD]
    • Qu’est-ce qu’il dit ? — What is he saying? [kes-kil DEE]

    When élision does NOT happen¶

    • si elle vient — if she comes — si changes only before il/ils
    • le héros, la honte, le onze novembre — the hero, shame, the eleventh of November — h aspiré and onze block élision
    • tu es, tu as — you are, you have — tu is not elided in writing (t’es, t’as = casual speech only)

    Élision mistakes beginners make

    • Wrong: Je aime le cricket.
    • Right: J’aime le cricket.

    • Why: je → j’ before a vowel: always.

    • Wrong: Je vais à la école.

    • Right: Je vais à l’école.

    • Why: la → l’ before a vowel, even though école is feminine. The gender is hidden — learn it as une école.

    • Wrong: Si il pleut, je reste.

    • Right: S’il pleut, je reste.

    • Why: si + il = s’il. But si + elle stays si elle.

    • Wrong: L’héros du film

    • Right: Le héros du film
    • Why: héros has an h aspiré: no élision, no liaison.

    À retenir

    • French hates a gap between two vowels. So words join up: a silent final consonant may wake up (liaison), a spoken one slides onto the next word (enchaînement), or a vowel drops out (élision: l’ami).
    • Final c, r, f, l are usually pronounced: avec, la mer, neuf, le sel. Most other final consonants are silent.
    • Drop the e muet only if it leaves two consonants together. If dropping it would join three, keep it: vendredi [vã-druh-DEE], une fenêtre [ewn fuh-NETR].
    • When the verb ends in a vowel, French adds a -t- just to make the liaison: a-t-il ? [a-TEEL], parle-t-elle ?, y a-t-il ? More in Asking questions.
    • Make all the obligatory liaisons, avoid the forbidden ones, and skip the optional ones (except after c’est). You will sound natural and correct.
    • Read any French sentence aloud now, marking liaisons with a pencil before you speak. Then go on to Hello, please, thank you — your first real conversations.

    4.9 Practice — Silent letters & liaison¶

    Exercise 1

    In 'petit', which letter is silent?

    1. p
    2. e
    3. i
    4. t
    Answer

    t

    Final t is silent: [puh-TEE]. In petite, the e makes the t heard.

    Exercise 2

    The CaReFuL rule: which final letters are usually pronounced?

    1. c, r, f, l
    2. s, t, d, x
    3. p, n, m, z
    4. all of them
    Answer

    c, r, f, l

    avec, la mer, neuf, le sel — but watch the -er verbs: parler.

    Exercise 3

    Which word ends with a pronounced consonant?

    1. grand
    2. vous
    3. avec
    4. trop
    Answer

    avec

    Final c is pronounced (CaReFuL): [a-VEK]. grand, vous, trop end silently.

    Exercise 4

    How do you say 'ils parlent'?

    1. [eel par-LENT]
    2. [eel PARL]
    3. [eel par-LÃ]
    4. [eel-zar-PARL]
    Answer

    [eel PARL]

    Verb ending -ent is silent: ils parlent sounds like il parle.

    Exercise 5

    'Ils ont' (they have) is pronounced…

    1. [eel SÕ]
    2. [eel-ZÕ]
    3. [eel-TÕ]
    4. [eel ONT]
    Answer

    [eel-ZÕ]

    Liaison: s → [z]. [eel SÕ] would be ils sont, 'they are'.

    Exercise 6

    Match each phrase to its liaison sound.

    AB
    les amis[v]
    un grand homme[n]
    neuf ans[z]
    un ami[t]
    Answer

    les amis — [z]; un grand homme — [t]; neuf ans — [v]; un ami — [n]

    s → z, d → t, f → v (neuf only), n stays n.

    Exercise 7

    Where is liaison obligatory?

    1. les enfants
    2. et elle
    3. un étudiant anglais
    4. les héros
    Answer

    les enfants

    Article + noun = always link. The others are forbidden liaisons.

    Exercise 8

    Why is there no liaison in 'les héros'?

    1. héros is singular
    2. héros starts with an h aspiré
    3. les never links
    4. it is a name
    Answer

    héros starts with an h aspiré

    h aspiré blocks liaison and élision: les | héros, le héros.

    Exercise 9

    Tap the word that should be elided.

    Je aime le chocolat.

    Answer

    Wrong word: Je.

    Corrected: J'aime le chocolat.

    je → j' before a vowel.

    Exercise 10

    Tap the wrong word.

    Elle est dans la école.

    Answer

    Wrong word: la.

    Corrected: Elle est dans l'école.

    la → l' before a vowel: l'école.

    Exercise 11

    Je ne sais pas ___ elle vient. (if)

    Hint: élision or not?

    Answer

    si

    si only becomes s' before il/ils. Before elle it stays si.

    Exercise 12

    ___ homme (the man)

    1. le
    2. la
    3. l'
    4. les
    Answer

    l'

    homme has a silent h (h muet), so le → l'.

    Exercise 13

    Which has a [z] sound?

    1. deux sœurs
    2. deux heures
    3. deux livres
    4. deux chats
    Answer

    deux heures

    deux‿heures [dö-ZÖR]: liaison before a vowel sound.

    Exercise 14

    In 'elle est', the l slides onto 'est'. This is called…

    1. élision
    2. enchaînement
    3. liaison
    4. h aspiré
    Answer

    enchaînement

    The l is already pronounced; it just chains onto the next vowel: [eh-LEH].

    Exercise 15

    Do you keep the e in 'vendredi'? Write yes or no: ___

    Hint: three-consonant rule

    Answer

    yes / oui

    Dropping it would join d-r-d: three consonants, so keep it: [vã-druh-DEE].

    Exercise 16

    Put the words in order: 'They have a friend.'

    Words: ont / un / ami / Ils

    Answer

    Ils ont un ami

    Two liaisons: ils‿ont‿un‿ami [eel-zõ-tɛ̃-na-MEE].

    Exercise 17

    Say it aloud — link everything.

    Ils ont un ami

    Answer

    Say: Ils ont un ami

    [eel-zõ-tɛ̃-na-MEE]: s → z, t of ont wakes up, n links.

    Exercise 18

    Say it aloud.

    Vous êtes en Inde

    Answer

    Say: Vous êtes en Inde

    [voo-zet ã-NƐ̃D]: vous‿êtes and en‿Inde are obligatory.

    Exercise 19

    Say it aloud.

    C'est un petit hôtel

    Answer

    Say: C'est un petit hôtel

    [seh-tɛ̃ puh-tee-to-TEL]: c'est‿un and petit‿hôtel.
