    Chapter 48: Registers & slang¶

    Registres, argot et verlan

    French has several words for the same thing, and each one tells people who you think you are talking to. A car can be un véhicule, une voiture, une bagnole or une caisse. In this chapter you climb the ladder of registers, learn how verlan flips words inside out, decode text-message French — and, just as important, learn when to keep all of it out of your mouth and your emails.

    In one line

    A register (un registre de langue) is a level of formality: soutenu (formal) → courant (standard) → familier (casual) → argotique / populaire (slang).

    • Je n’ai plus d’argent. — I have no more money. (courant — safe everywhere)
    • J’ai plus de fric. — I’m out of cash. (familier — friends, family)
    • J’ai plus une thune. — I’m totally broke. (argot — very casual slang)

    48.1 The register ladder¶

    Think of registers as the steps of a ladder. The middle step, courant, is standard French: correct, neutral, safe with anyone. Go up and you get soutenu, the French of formal letters, speeches and literature. Go down and you get familier, the relaxed French of friends, and below it argotique (slang) or populaire, the French of in-groups, teenagers and the street.

    Hindi speakers already know this idea: aap / tum / tu or bhojan / khaana carry different levels of respect. French does the same with whole vocabularies.

    Register Where you meet it Typical features
    Soutenu Formal letters, speeches, literature, the news at its most careful Rare words, full negation, inversion: Puis-je… ?
    Courant Work, shops, teachers, strangers, most books Standard words, vous with strangers
    Familier Friends, family, relaxed colleagues, social media Short words, tu, dropped ne
    Argotique / populaire Youth, songs, films, the street, closed groups Verlan, borrowed words, words that change fast

    Learn up, understand down

    Actively use courant (and some soutenu for writing). Learn to understand familier and argot — but use them only with people who use them with you.

    Grammar changes with register too — dropped ne, on instead of nous, chais pas — but those belong to the chapter Real spoken French. Here we focus on words. For tu vs vous basics, see Hello, please, thank you.

    48.2 Familier: the words of everyday friends¶

    Familier words are not "bad French". French people of every age use them at home, with friends and often at work over coffee. Many are just shorter or punchier versions of standard words. Here are the ones you will hear constantly.

    Courant Familier Argot / note
    un homme un mec, un type un keum (verlan)
    une femme une nana une meuf (verlan)
    un enfant un gosse, un môme, un gamin — (see the Québec warning below)
    un ami un copain, un pote un poto
    la police les flics les keufs (verlan)
    manger bouffer (la bouffe = food)
    travailler bosser (le boulot, le taf = the job)
    se dépêcher se grouiller, se magner —
    regarder mater can sound like "ogling" people
    comprendre piger capter
    avoir peur flipper, avoir la trouille —
    fatigué crevé, naze —
    ennuyeux chiant (slightly vulgar) relou = annoying (verlan)
    un livre un bouquin —
    les vêtements les fringues —
    les chaussures les godasses, les pompes —
    très vachement, super grave, trop (youth)
    • Mon pote s’est acheté une nouvelle bagnole. — My mate bought himself a new car.
    • On bouffe où ce soir ? — Where are we eating tonight?
    • Grouille-toi, le film commence ! — Hurry up, the film is starting!
    • Je bosse tout le week-end, c’est chiant. — I’m working all weekend, it’s a pain. (chiant is mildly vulgar)
    • T’as pigé ce qu’il a dit ? — Did you get what he said?
    • J’ai flippé pendant tout le film. — I was scared during the whole film.
    • Les gosses sont crevés, ils dorment déjà. — The kids are exhausted, they’re already asleep.
    • Il est sympa, ce mec. — He’s nice, that guy.

    Québec trap: gosses

    In France les gosses = the kids. In Québec, gosses is a vulgar word for testicles. With Canadians, just say les enfants or les jeunes.

    'Familier' is not the same as 'vulgaire'

    Dictionaries mark words fam. (casual) or vulg. (vulgar). Bouquin is harmless; chiant, se casser (to clear off) or osef are rude. French also has strong swear words that you will hear in films; recognise them, but do not try them out — a learner’s swear word always sounds louder than intended.

    48.3 Verlan: French turned inside out¶

    Verlan is a slang game: you swap the syllables of a word. The name itself is verlan: l’envers ("the reverse") → vers-l’en → verlan. It grew in the Paris suburbs (les banlieues) in the 1970s–90s and spread through rap, films and teenagers to the whole country. Some verlan words are now so common that your French teacher will understand them.

    How verlan is built

    Cut the word into syllables and reverse them. One-syllable words first get an extra "eu" sound (femme → fa-meu → meu-fa → meuf).

    Verlan From Meaning
    une meuf femme a woman, a girl; ma meuf = my girlfriend
    un keum mec a guy; mon keum = my boyfriend
    relou lourd (heavy) annoying, a pain
    ouf fou crazy, amazing
    chelou louche weird, dodgy
    vénère énervé angry, wound up
    céfran français French (often = "white French", so it can sound labelling)
    une teuf fête a party
    zarbi bizarre strange
    laisse béton laisse tomber forget it (made famous by a 1977 song by Renaud)
    un ripou pourri (rotten) a corrupt cop / official
    les keufs flics the cops
    un beur → un rebeu arabe a person of North African origin (sensitive — see below)
    • Il est trop relou, il m’appelle toutes les cinq minutes. — He’s so annoying, he calls me every five minutes.
    • C’est chelou, cette histoire. — That story is weird.
    • Ma mère est vénère parce que je suis rentré tard. — My mum is furious because I came home late.
    • On fait une teuf samedi, tu viens ? — We’re having a party on Saturday, are you coming?
    • Laisse béton, c’est pas grave. — Forget it, it doesn’t matter.

    Re-verlan and sensitive words¶

    When a verlan word becomes too mainstream, young speakers sometimes flip it again: arabe → beur → rebeu, femme → meuf → feumeu. Slang always runs away from the mainstream.

    Words about origin: listen, don’t use

    Beur, rebeu and céfran label people by origin. People inside a community may use them about themselves, but from a foreigner they can sound reductive or offensive. Say une personne d’origine maghrébine or simply the person’s name.

    48.4 Slang you will hear every day¶

    These expressions are everywhere among people under 40 — in cafés, on YouTube, in series. Most are familier rather than strictly argot, so they are useful to recognise early.

    Expression Meaning Standard equivalent
    C’est ouf ! It’s crazy / amazing! C’est incroyable !
    grave totally, really vraiment, tout à fait
    kiffer to love, to really like adorer, aimer beaucoup
    trop + adjective so…, really… très…
    Ça craint. That sucks / that’s dodgy. C’est embêtant / inquiétant.
    C’est nul. It’s rubbish. C’est très mauvais.
    Je suis crevé(e). I’m exhausted. Je suis très fatigué(e).
    avoir la dalle to be starving avoir très faim
    • Ce concert, c’était ouf ! — That concert was amazing!
    • — Il est sympa, non ? — Grave ! — — He’s nice, isn’t he? — Totally!
    • Je suis grave d’accord avec toi. — I totally agree with you.
    • Je kiffe cette série. — I love this series.
    • Il est trop mignon, ton chien ! — Your dog is so cute!
    • Le dernier métro est parti ? Ça craint. — The last metro has gone? That sucks.
    • Ce film est nul, on s’en va ? — This film is rubbish, shall we leave?
    • J’ai la dalle, on va manger ? — I’m starving, shall we go and eat?

    'trop' has two meanings

    Standard trop = too (much): C’est trop cher = it’s too expensive. Familier trop = very: C’est trop bien = it’s really great. Context decides.

    Many fixed idioms (avoir la flemme, poser un lapin) are also familier; you will find them in Idioms & expressions.

    48.5 Texting French: SMS abbreviations¶

    French text messages (les textos, les SMS) squeeze words into a few letters. You need to read them; you write them only to friends. Most abbreviations come from the spoken, familier form, so jsp is je sais pas, not je ne sais pas.

    Abbr. Full form Meaning
    slt salut hi
    cc coucou hey (very friendly)
    bjr bonjour hello
    mdr mort de rire LOL ("dead from laughing")
    ptdr pété de rire ROFL (even stronger)
    stp / svp s’il te plaît / s’il vous plaît please (tu / vous)
    bcp beaucoup a lot
    jsp je sais pas I dunno
    dsl désolé(e) sorry
    tkt t’inquiète (pas) don’t worry
    pk / pq pourquoi why
    jtm je t’aime I love you
    auj aujourd’hui today
    rdv rendez-vous appointment, meeting
    mtn maintenant now
    ct c’était it was
    osef on s’en fout who cares (vulgar)
    a+ à plus (tard) see you later
    • cc, tu fais quoi auj ? rdv au café à 18h ? — Hey, what are you doing today? Meet at the café at 6 pm?
    • dsl jsp, je bosse mtn. tkt, je te rappelle. — Sorry, I don’t know, I’m working now. Don’t worry, I’ll call you back.
    • ct trop bien hier mdr — Yesterday was so great lol.

    For how to write complete, well-formed messages and emails, see Messages, letters & emails.

    48.6 Going up: soutenu words¶

    The other direction matters too, especially for B2–C1 writing. Swapping a courant word for a soutenu one makes a letter or an essay sound mature. Use these in writing and formal speech, not when ordering a croissant.

    Courant Soutenu Meaning
    vouloir souhaiter, désirer to wish, to want
    aller (à) se rendre (à) to go (to)
    avoir peur craindre, redouter to fear
    comprendre saisir, appréhender to grasp
    se dépêcher se hâter to hasten
    ennuyeux fastidieux tedious
    un livre un ouvrage a book, a work
    une maison une demeure a residence
    la police les forces de l’ordre the police forces
    malade souffrant(e) unwell
    désolé navré very sorry
    • Je souhaiterais prendre rendez-vous avec vous. — I would like to make an appointment with you.
    • Je me rendrai à Lyon la semaine prochaine. — I shall be going to Lyon next week.
    • Je suis navré de ce contretemps. — I am very sorry for this delay / mishap.
    • Les forces de l’ordre sont intervenues rapidement. — The police intervened quickly.

    Formal grammar (the passé simple, the ne explétif, inversion in literature) is covered in Literary & formal French.

    48.7 When NOT to use slang¶

    A learner who uses slang in the wrong place does not sound cool — they sound as if they do not know the rules. French society is quite attentive to register, especially in writing. Keep slang, verlan and text abbreviations out of these situations:

    • Job interviews and professional meetings — courant, with vous.
    • Emails to teachers, universities, the administration (la préfecture, la CAF, your bank): full words, a formal opening and closing.
    • Older people and strangers: vous and courant until they invite you to relax.
    • DELF / DALF writing and speaking: examiners grade "adequacy to the situation". Slang in a formal letter costs you marks. (See DELF, DALF & your level.)

    The safe default

    Unsure? Use courant + vous. Nobody is offended by standard French; many people are put off by slang from a stranger.

    Register mistakes learners make

    • Wrong: Madame, je vous écris pour avoir un boulot dans votre boîte.
    • Right: Madame, je vous écris afin d’obtenir un poste dans votre entreprise.

    • Why: Boulot and boîte (company) are familier. A cover letter needs courant or soutenu: un poste, un emploi, une entreprise.

    • Wrong: (to a stranger in the street) Excuse-moi, tu peux m’aider ?

    • Right: Excusez-moi, vous pourriez m’aider, s’il vous plaît ?

    • Why: With an adult stranger, use vous. Tu is for friends, family, children — or when someone invites you: On peut se tutoyer ?

    • Wrong: Monsieur le Directeur, votre offre est grave intéressante.

    • Right: Monsieur le Directeur, votre offre est très intéressante.

    • Why: Mixing registers is jarring: a formal opening followed by youth slang sounds comic. Keep one register through the whole text.

    • Wrong: Bjr, jsp si je peux venir auj, dsl.

    • Right: Bonjour Madame, je ne sais pas si je pourrai venir aujourd’hui. Je vous prie de m’excuser.

    • Why: SMS abbreviations never go in an email to a teacher or an office. Write every word, keep the ne, and add a polite formula.

    • Wrong: (in an interview) J’ai kiffé mon dernier stage, c’était ouf.

    • Right: J’ai beaucoup apprécié mon dernier stage, c’était très enrichissant.

    • Why: Kiffer and ouf are youth slang. In an interview, choose precise standard words: apprécier, enrichissant, passionnant.

    • Wrong: (to a shop assistant) Salut, je voudrais un pain.

    • Right: Bonjour, je voudrais une baguette, s’il vous plaît.
    • Why: Salut is for people you know. Shops, offices and strangers get Bonjour (Madame / Monsieur).

    48.8 In real life: one message, four registers¶

    You are ill and cannot come to a meeting. Watch how the same message changes depending on who receives it.

    • Soutenu — Je suis navré, mais je ne pourrai pas me rendre à notre rendez-vous : je suis souffrant. — I am very sorry, but I shall not be able to attend our meeting: I am unwell. (to a director, a client)
    • Courant — Désolé, je ne peux pas venir au rendez-vous, je suis malade. — Sorry, I can’t come to the meeting, I’m ill. (to a colleague, a teacher)
    • Familier — Dsl, je peux pas venir, je suis malade comme un chien. — Sorry, can’t come, I’m sick as a dog. (to a friend)
    • Argotique — Wesh, je suis HS, je viens pas, tkt. — Hey, I’m out of action, not coming, don’t worry. (to a close friend; HS = hors service)

    A short dialogue among friends¶

    • — Salut ! T’étais où hier ? La teuf, c’était ouf. — — Hi! Where were you yesterday? The party was amazing.
    • — J’ai bossé jusqu’à minuit, j’étais crevé. — — I worked until midnight, I was exhausted.
    • — Ça craint ! Ton chef est trop relou. — — That sucks! Your boss is such a pain.
    • — Grave. Bon, j’ai la dalle : on va bouffer ? — — Totally. Right, I’m starving: shall we go and eat?

    Match the other person

    Let the French speaker set the level. If they say tu and use slang, you can relax a step; if they stay with vous, stay there too.

    À retenir

    • A register (un registre de langue) is a level of formality: soutenu (formal) → courant (standard) → familier (casual) → argotique / populaire (slang).
    • Actively use courant (and some soutenu for writing). Learn to understand familier and argot — but use them only with people who use them with you.
    • Cut the word into syllables and reverse them. One-syllable words first get an extra "eu" sound (femme → fa-meu → meu-fa → meuf).
    • Standard trop = too (much): C’est trop cher = it’s too expensive. Familier trop = very: C’est trop bien = it’s really great. Context decides.
    • Unsure? Use courant + vous. Nobody is offended by standard French; many people are put off by slang from a stranger.
    • Let the French speaker set the level. If they say tu and use slang, you can relax a step; if they stay with vous, stay there too.

    48.9 Practice — Registers & slang¶

    Exercise 1

    Which word is the familier way to say 'une voiture'?

    1. un véhicule
    2. une automobile
    3. une bagnole
    4. un emploi

    Hint: Friends, not a formal letter.

    Answer

    une bagnole

    véhicule/automobile are formal, bagnole is familier, caisse is slang.

    Exercise 2

    'relou' is the verlan form of…

    1. lourd
    2. roulé
    3. louer
    4. relevé

    Hint: Swap the two syllables back.

    Answer

    lourd

    lourd (heavy) → reversed sounds → relou = annoying, a pain.

    Exercise 3

    You email a university office. Which opening is right?

    1. Salut,
    2. Coucou,
    3. Madame, Monsieur,
    4. Wesh,
    Answer

    Madame, Monsieur,

    An administration needs a formal opening; salut, coucou and wesh are for friends.

    Exercise 4

    Match the verlan word to the standard word.

    AB
    meuffête
    keumlouche
    teufénervé
    cheloufemme
    vénèremec
    Answer

    meuf — femme; keum — mec; teuf — fête; chelou — louche; vénère — énervé

    Verlan reverses syllables: fê-te → teuf, lou-che → chelou.

    Exercise 5

    Match the text abbreviation to its full form.

    AB
    mdrje sais pas
    tktdésolé
    jsprendez-vous
    dslmort de rire
    rdvt'inquiète
    Answer

    mdr — mort de rire; tkt — t'inquiète; jsp — je sais pas; dsl — désolé; rdv — rendez-vous

    SMS forms come from spoken French: jsp = je sais pas.

    Exercise 6

    J'ai trop faim, j'ai la ___ ! (I'm starving)

    Hint: avoir la … = to be starving

    Answer

    dalle

    avoir la dalle = avoir très faim (familier).

    Exercise 7

    Mon ___ m'a aidé à déménager. (my mate)

    Hint: familier for ami, starts with p

    Answer

    pote / copain

    un pote / un copain = a friend (familier).

    Exercise 8

    Formal letter: Je ___ obtenir un rendez-vous. (I would like)

    Hint: conditional of souhaiter, je

    Answer

    souhaiterais / voudrais

    je souhaiterais is the polite, formal 'I would like'.

    Exercise 9

    ___-toi, le train part dans deux minutes ! (Hurry up)

    Hint: familier: se grouiller

    Answer

    Grouille / Dépêche / Magne

    Grouille-toi ! = Dépêche-toi ! (familier).

    Exercise 10

    Tap the word that is too casual for a cover letter.

    Madame, je vous écris pour obtenir un boulot dans votre entreprise.

    Answer

    Wrong word: boulot.

    Corrected: Madame, je vous écris pour obtenir un poste dans votre entreprise.

    boulot is familier; use un poste or un emploi.

    Exercise 11

    Tap the slang word in this formal sentence.

    Monsieur le Directeur, votre proposition est grave intéressante.

    Answer

    Wrong word: grave.

    Corrected: Monsieur le Directeur, votre proposition est très intéressante.

    grave = 'totally' is youth slang; use très.

    Exercise 12

    What does 'Ça craint' mean?

    1. That's great
    2. That sucks
    3. I'm scared
    4. That's expensive
    Answer

    That sucks

    Ça craint = it's bad / dodgy (familier).

    Exercise 13

    Which register is 'les forces de l'ordre'?

    1. soutenu
    2. courant
    3. familier
    4. argotique
    Answer

    soutenu

    police (courant) → flics (familier) → keufs (argot); forces de l'ordre is formal.

    Exercise 14

    In a familier sentence, 'C'est trop bien' means…

    1. It's too good to be true
    2. It's really great
    3. It's not good enough
    4. It's quite good
    Answer

    It's really great

    Familier trop + adjective = very.

    Exercise 15

    Put the words in order (to a stranger).

    Words: s'il / vous / plaît / m'aider / Pourriez-vous

    Answer

    Pourriez-vous m'aider s'il vous plaît

    With strangers: vous + polite conditional pourriez.

    Exercise 16

    Put the words in order (to a friend).

    Words: ce / Je / suis / trop / crevé / soir

    Answer

    Je suis trop crevé ce soir

    Familier: trop crevé = really exhausted.

    Exercise 17

    Say it aloud — casual, to a friend.

    C'est ouf, je kiffe trop cette chanson

    Answer

    Say: C'est ouf, je kiffe trop cette chanson

    ouf (verlan of fou) and kiffer are youth slang.

    Exercise 18

    Now say it formally.

    Je souhaiterais prendre rendez-vous avec vous

    Answer

    Say: Je souhaiterais prendre rendez-vous avec vous

    souhaiter in the conditional is the formal choice.
