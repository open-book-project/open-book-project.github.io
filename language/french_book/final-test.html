    Chapter 60: Final test¶

    L’examen final

    Forty questions, one for (almost) every chapter, in the order of the book: sounds first, then the foundations, the big grammar machine, speaking, vocabulary, writing and culture. It is not an official exam — it is a map of your gaps. Every wrong answer points to exactly one or two chapters to reopen.

    How to use this test

    Do it in one sitting, without looking back at the chapters. Write down the number of every question you miss — then use the table in section 6 to see which chapter to reopen.

    60.1 How the test works¶

    The test mixes all six question types you have met in the book: multiple choice, fill the gap, spot the mistake, put the words in order, match the pairs, and say it aloud.

    • Time: about 45 minutes. Do not stop to look things up.
    • Paper: keep a notebook open and write the numbers of the questions you miss.
    • Voice: read every French sentence in your head with its sounds — liaison, nasals, silent letters.
    • Retake: do it again in a week. The second score is the one that counts.

    Read the hint only after you try

    Answer first, then check the hint. A guess you correct teaches you more than an answer you copy.

    60.2 What the test covers¶

    The questions follow the seven Parts of the book. Part 3 (Construire) has the most, because it is where most marks are lost in real exams: pronouns, past tenses, the subjunctive.

    The weighting

    More than half the test (22 of 40) is grammar from Parts 2 and 3 — the skeleton of every sentence you will ever say.

    60.3 Warm-up — say these aloud first¶

    Ten sentences from across the book. Listen, repeat each one twice, then start the test. If any of them feels strange, note the chapter in brackets.

    • Les‿enfants sont‿au jardin. — The children are in the garden. (Silent letters & liaison)
    • Il a quatre-vingt-dix-sept ans. — He is ninety-seven. (Numbers, dates & time · Être & avoir)
    • Je n’ai pas de voiture, mais j’ai un vélo. — I don’t have a car, but I have a bike. (Articles · Saying no)
    • Tu en veux ? — Oui, j’en veux deux. — Do you want some? — Yes, I want two. (Y, en & pronoun order)
    • Quand j’étais petit, j’habitais à Chennai. — When I was little, I lived in Chennai. (Imparfait vs passé composé)
    • Il faut que tu viennes avec nous. — You have to come with us. (The subjunctive)
    • Si j’avais su, je serais venu plus tôt. — If I had known, I would have come earlier. (Advanced compound tenses)
    • Bah, du coup, on y va ou quoi ? — Well, so, are we going or what? (Real spoken French)
    • J’ai la flemme de sortir, il pleut des cordes. — I can’t be bothered to go out, it’s pouring. (Idioms & expressions)
    • Je vous prie d’agréer, Madame, l’expression de mes salutations distinguées. — Yours faithfully. (Messages, letters & emails)
    • Néanmoins, cette démarche suscite de nombreuses questions. — Nevertheless, this approach raises many questions. (Word building & advanced words)
    • À Pondichéry, on voit encore des noms de rue en français. — In Pondicherry you still see street names in French. (Culture & the francophone world)

    60.4 Four traps to avoid on the way¶

    These are not in the test — but they are the slips that most often make a learner change a right answer into a wrong one. Read them once, then start.

    Classic traps

    • Wrong: Je suis fini.
    • Right: J’ai fini.

    • Why: Je suis fini means "I am done for". Finishing something takes avoir.

    • Wrong: J’attends pour le bus.

    • Right: J’attends le bus.

    • Why: attendre, chercher, écouter, regarder take a direct object — no "for", "at" or "to".

    • Wrong: Si j’aurais su…

    • Right: Si j’avais su…

    • Why: Never the conditional right after si (= if). Si + imparfait / plus-que-parfait.

    • Wrong: Il pleut des chats et des chiens.

    • Right: Il pleut des cordes.
    • Why: Idioms do not travel word for word. French rain falls in "ropes".

    60.5 Your score — what it means¶

    Count one point per question. The bands below are a rough guide to which level you are building on — only an official exam gives you a certified level (see DELF, DALF & your level).

    Score What it suggests What to do next
    36–40 A solid B2 foundation Work on C1 material: Literary & formal French, Arguing & presenting, Word building & advanced words; try DELF B2 / DALF C1 sample papers.
    28–35 Around B1, heading to B2 Reopen only the chapters of the questions you missed (section 6), then retake the test in a week.
    18–27 Around A2, heading to B1 Redo Part 3 Construire in order, with each chapter’s quiz twice.
    0–17 A1–A2 No problem — start again from How to use this book and go through Part 2 Les bases slowly, out loud.

    Speaking counts double in real life

    If you missed the three spoken questions (Q2, Q4, Q20), give them extra time: reopen Pronunciation clinic before anything else.

    60.7 After the test — a one-week repair plan¶

    Do not reread the whole book. Reopen only what the table told you, and use the chapter quizzes, which are short enough to do every day.

    Day What to do
    1 List the chapters from section 6. Read the first one and do its quiz.
    2–4 One or two chapters a day; redo yesterday’s quiz before starting a new one.
    5 Say every say example of your weakest chapter aloud — twice.
    6 Write five sentences using what you repaired; check them against the chapters.
    7 Retake this final test. Compare the two scores.

    À retenir

    • Do it in one sitting, without looking back at the chapters. Write down the number of every question you miss — then use the table in section 6 to see which chapter to reopen.
    • Answer first, then check the hint. A guess you correct teaches you more than an answer you copy.
    • More than half the test (22 of 40) is grammar from Parts 2 and 3 — the skeleton of every sentence you will ever say.
    • If you missed the three spoken questions (Q2, Q4, Q20), give them extra time: reopen Pronunciation clinic before anything else.
    • Write your score and the date in your notebook. Fix the chapters from section 6 this week, then retake the test — and when you score 36 or more twice, book your next exam (see DELF, DALF & your level). Bravo et bonne…

    60.8 Final test — the whole book¶

    Exercise 1

    Q1 · Match each accent to its sound.

    A B
    é s, as in garçon
    è oh, as in hôtel
    ç ay, as in café
    ô eh, as in mère
    Answer

    é — ay, as in café; è — eh, as in mère; ç — s, as in garçon; ô — oh, as in hôtel

    é is closed [e], è is open [ɛ], ç is always [s], ô is a closed [o]. See Alphabet & accents.

    Exercise 2

    Q2 · Say it aloud: u and ou in one line.

    Toute la rue a vu le loup

    Answer

    toute, loup = ou [u]; rue, vu = u [y]: say ee with rounded lips. See The sounds of French.

    Exercise 3

    Q3 · How do you pronounce « les amis »?

    • lay-ah-MEE
    • lay-zah-MEE
    • less-ah-MEE
    • lay-sah-MEE

    Hint: liaison

    Answer

    lay-zah-MEE

    The silent s of les is pronounced z before a vowel: les‿amis. See Silent letters & liaison.

    Exercise 4

    Q4 · At the bakery — say it politely.

    Bonjour Madame, je voudrais une baguette, s'il vous plaît

    Answer

    Bonjour first, then je voudrais (not je veux), then s'il vous plaît. See Hello, please, thank you.

    Exercise 5

    Q5 · 99 = quatre-vingt-___

    Hint: 4 × 20 + 19

    Answer

    dix-neuf / dix neuf

    quatre-vingt-dix-neuf = 80 + 19. See Numbers, dates & time.

    Exercise 6

    Q6 · C'est ___ problème difficile.

    • un
    • une
    • la
    • des

    Hint: Words in -ème from Greek

    Answer

    un

    le problème, le système, le thème are masculine despite the final -e. See Nouns, gender & plurals.

    Exercise 7

    Q7 · Le matin, je mange ___ pain. (some bread)

    Answer

    du

    An unspecified amount of a masculine noun: du pain. See Articles.

    Exercise 8

    Q8 · Tap the wrong word.

    Mon frère est vingt-cinq ans.

    Answer

    Wrong word: est.

    Corrected: Mon frère a vingt-cinq ans.

    French has its age: avoir 25 ans. See Être & avoir.

    Exercise 9

    Q9 · Nous ___ (manger) au restaurant ce soir.

    Hint: Keep the g soft

    Answer

    mangeons

    -ger verbs keep an e before -ons: nous mangeons. See Present tense: -er verbs.

    Exercise 10

    Q10 · Ils ___ au cinéma ce soir. (aller)

    • vont
    • allent
    • vas
    • allons
    Answer

    vont

    aller: je vais, tu vas, il va, nous allons, vous allez, ils vont. See Present tense: the rest.

    Exercise 11

    Q11 · Put the words in order: I never eat meat.

    Words: mange / viande / Je / de / jamais / ne

    Answer

    Je ne mange jamais de viande

    ne + verb + jamais, and de (not de la) after a negation. See Saying no.

    Exercise 12

    Q12 · ___ apprends-tu le français ? — Parce que je veux vivre à Montréal.

    • Pourquoi
    • Comment
    • Quand
    • Où
    Answer

    Pourquoi

    The answer parce que (because) matches pourquoi (why). See Asking questions.

    Exercise 13

    Q13 · Cette pizza est ___ que l'autre.

    • meilleure
    • plus bonne
    • meilleur
    • mieux

    Hint: bon → ?

    Answer

    meilleure

    bon becomes meilleur, and agrees with pizza (feminine): meilleure. mieux is the adverb. See Adjectives and Comparing.

    Exercise 14

    Q14 · Tap the wrong word.

    Ma amie Sophie arrive demain.

    Answer

    Wrong word: Ma.

    Corrected: Mon amie Sophie arrive demain.

    Before a vowel sound, ma becomes mon, even for a feminine noun: mon amie. See My, your, this, that.

    Exercise 15

    Q15 · Tap the wrong word.

    Cet été, je vais en Japon.

    Answer

    Wrong word: en.

    Corrected: Cet été, je vais au Japon.

    Masculine countries take au (au Japon, au Canada); feminine ones take en (en France, en Inde). See Prepositions & places.

    Exercise 16

    Q16 · Je donne le livre à Paul → replace both with pronouns.

    Words: le / lui / donne / Je

    Answer

    Je le lui donne

    Order: le / la / les before lui / leur. See Object pronouns and Y, en & pronoun order.

    Exercise 17

    Q17 · Ce matin, elle s'est ___ (lever) à six heures.

    Hint: être + agreement

    Answer

    levée / levee

    Reflexive verbs take être, and the participle agrees: elle s'est levée. See Reflexive verbs and The past: passé composé.

    Exercise 18

    Q18 · Je ___ la télé quand le téléphone a sonné.

    • regardais
    • ai regardé
    • regarde
    • regarderai
    Answer

    regardais

    The action in progress (the scene) is in the imparfait; the interruption (the event) in the passé composé. See Imparfait vs passé composé.

    Exercise 19

    Q19 · Demain, nous ___ (partir) à huit heures. (futur simple)

    Answer

    partirons

    Futur simple = infinitive + -ons: partirons. See The future.

    Exercise 20

    Q20 · Tell a friend not to worry.

    Ne t'inquiète pas, tout va bien

    Answer

    Negative imperative of a reflexive verb: the pronoun stays in front — ne t'inquiète pas. See Giving instructions.

    Exercise 21

    Q21 · C'est le livre ___ je t'ai parlé.

    • dont
    • que
    • qui
    • où

    Hint: parler de…

    Answer

    dont

    parler de quelque chose → dont replaces de + noun. See Relative pronouns.

    Exercise 22

    Q22 · Si j'avais le temps, je ___ (voyager) plus souvent.

    Answer

    voyagerais

    si + imparfait → conditionnel présent: je voyagerais. See Conditional & si clauses.

    Exercise 23

    Q23 · Bien qu'il ___ fatigué, il continue à travailler.

    • soit
    • est
    • sera
    • était
    Answer

    soit

    bien que always takes the subjunctive: qu'il soit. See The subjunctive and Linking words.

    Exercise 24

    Q24 · Quand je suis arrivé, le film ___ déjà commencé.

    • avait
    • a
    • aura
    • aurait
    Answer

    avait

    An action before another past action: plus-que-parfait, il avait commencé. See Advanced compound tenses.

    Exercise 25

    Q25 · Il a dit : « Je viendrai demain. » → Il a dit qu'il ___ le lendemain.

    Hint: future in the past

    Answer

    viendrait

    After a past reporting verb, the future becomes the conditional: viendrait; demain becomes le lendemain. See Passive & reported speech.

    Exercise 26

    Q26 · Il s'est blessé ___ du vélo. (while riding)

    • en faisant
    • en fait
    • pour faire
    • faisant en
    Answer

    en faisant

    Gérondif = en + present participle: en faisant = while doing. See Gérondif & participles.

    Exercise 27

    Q27 · Match the passé simple (novels) to the passé composé (speech).

    A B
    il fut ils ont fait
    elle alla nous avons eu
    ils firent il a été
    nous eûmes elle est allée
    Answer

    il fut — il a été; elle alla — elle est allée; ils firent — ils ont fait; nous eûmes — nous avons eu

    You must recognise the passé simple to read novels. See Literary & formal French.

    Exercise 28

    Q28 · Emergency numbers in France.

    A B
    15 pompiers (fire brigade)
    17 European emergency number
    18 SAMU (medical emergency)
    112 police
    Answer

    15 — SAMU (medical emergency); 17 — police; 18 — pompiers (fire brigade); 112 — European emergency number

    15 doctor, 17 police, 18 fire, 112 anywhere in Europe. See Travel & emergencies.

    Exercise 29

    Q29 · À mon ___, c'est une excellente idée. (opinion)

    Answer

    avis

    À mon avis = in my opinion (never « dans mon opinion »). See Conversation & opinions.

    Exercise 30

    Q30 · Match spoken or slang French to standard French.

    A B
    chais pas travail
    bagnole femme
    boulot je ne sais pas
    meuf voiture
    Answer

    chais pas — je ne sais pas; bagnole — voiture; boulot — travail; meuf — femme

    chais pas is fast speech; bagnole and boulot are familier; meuf is verlan for femme. See Real spoken French and Registers & slang.

    Exercise 31

    Q31 · On the phone, « Ne quittez pas » means…

    • Hold the line
    • Don't leave the building
    • Don't quit your job
    • Stay seated
    Answer

    Hold the line

    Ne quittez pas = please hold. See Work, phone & interviews.

    Exercise 32

    Q32 · Match the French to the English.

    A B
    le genou coat
    la cuisine summer
    le manteau knee
    l'été kitchen
    Answer

    le genou — knee; la cuisine — kitchen; le manteau — coat; l'été — summer

    Body, home, clothes and seasons. See Core vocabulary by theme.

    Exercise 33

    Q33 · How do you say « I miss you »?

    • Tu me manques
    • Je te manque
    • Je manque toi
    • Je te rate
    Answer

    Tu me manques

    With manquer, the person who is missed is the subject: tu me manques = you are missing to me. See The 100 essential verbs.

    Exercise 34

    Q34 · Tap the word that is a false friend here.

    Je vais à la librairie pour emprunter des livres.

    Answer

    Wrong word: librairie.

    Corrected: Je vais à la bibliothèque pour emprunter des livres.

    une librairie is a bookshop; a library is une bibliothèque. See Cognates & false friends.

    Exercise 35

    Q35 · « Il m'a posé un lapin » means…

    • He stood me up
    • He gave me a rabbit
    • He played a trick on me
    • He made me laugh
    Answer

    He stood me up

    poser un lapin à quelqu'un = to not turn up to a date. See Idioms & expressions.

    Exercise 36

    Q36 · changer (to change) → le ___ (a change)

    Hint: a common noun suffix

    Answer

    changement

    verb stem + -ment makes many masculine nouns: changement, développement. See Word building & advanced words.

    Exercise 37

    Q37 · Tap the spelling mistake.

    Hier, elle est allée a Lyon.

    Answer

    Wrong word: a.

    Corrected: Hier, elle est allée à Lyon.

    Can you say « avait » instead? No → it is the preposition à. See Spelling & punctuation.

    Exercise 38

    Q38 · The best ending for a formal letter:

    • Veuillez agréer, Madame, Monsieur, l'expression de mes salutations distinguées.
    • Gros bisous et à bientôt !
    • Merci, à plus !
    • Salut et bonne journée !
    Answer

    Veuillez agréer, Madame, Monsieur, l'expression de mes salutations distinguées.

    The formule de politesse repeats the opening (Madame, Monsieur). See Messages, letters & emails.

    Exercise 39

    Q39 · Match each part of a French essay to its job.

    A B
    l'amorce tells the reader the parts
    la problématique widens the topic at the very end
    l'annonce du plan catches the reader's attention
    l'ouverture the question the essay answers
    Answer

    l'amorce — catches the reader's attention; la problématique — the question the essay answers; l'annonce du plan — tells the reader the parts; l'ouverture — widens the topic at the very end

    Introduction: amorce, problématique, annonce du plan; conclusion: bilan, ouverture. See Essays & structured writing.

    Exercise 40

    Q40 · In France 70 is soixante-dix. In Belgium and Switzerland people say…

    • septante
    • soixante-dix
    • huitante
    • septante-dix
    Answer

    septante

    Belgium and Switzerland: septante (70) and nonante (90); some Swiss cantons also say huitante (80). See Culture & the francophone world.

    Next step

    Write your score and the date in your notebook. Fix the chapters from section 6 this week, then retake the test — and when you score 36 or more twice, book your next exam (see DELF, DALF & your level). Bravo et bonne continuation !
