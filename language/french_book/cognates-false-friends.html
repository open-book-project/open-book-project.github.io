    Chapter 45: Cognates & false friends¶

    Mots transparents et faux amis

    If you speak English, you already know thousands of French words. After 1066 the Norman kings of England spoke French for 300 years, and English took in about a third of its vocabulary from them. This chapter shows you how to recognise these mots transparents on sight — and how to dodge the faux amis, the “false friends” that look English but mean something else.

    masculinefeminineplural

    The big idea

    Most French words that look English are English in meaning — but a small, famous group are traps. Learn the patterns, then learn the traps by heart.

    • C’est une question très importante. — It’s a very important question.
    • Le président a pris une décision difficile. — The president made a difficult decision.
    • La situation est absolument catastrophique. — The situation is absolutely catastrophic.

    45.1 Why French gives you free words¶

    English and French share words in two ways. Some came from Norman French after 1066 (beef, castle, justice, court). Many more came later from Latin, which both languages used for science, law and religion (nation, education, possible). The result: a sentence on politics, science or business can be 50% transparent to you.

    The trick is to read French with your eyes first. Your ear will not recognise restaurant pronounced [res-to-RÃ], but your eye will. So read a lot — news headlines are perfect.

    • Le gouvernement présente un nouveau programme économique. — The government presents a new economic programme.
    • Les étudiants protestent contre la réforme de l’université. — The students are protesting against the university reform.

    Read first, listen second

    Cognates are easy to see but hard to hear: French stresses the last syllable and drops final consonants. Read the text, then listen to it.

    45.2 Suffix rules: endings that translate themselves¶

    Many French endings match an English ending one-to-one. Learn the pairs below and you can build — or guess — hundreds of words. (Prefixes and the wider system of word-building are in Word building & advanced words.)

    French English More examples
    -tion, -sion -tion, -sion information, télévision, attention, solution
    -ité -ty université, qualité, réalité, électricité
    -ique -ic / -ical logique, typique, politique, pratique
    -eux / -euse -ous nerveux, curieux, sérieux, généreux, nombreux
    -ment (adverb) -ly rapidement, normalement, finalement, simplement
    -isme / -iste -ism / -ist optimisme, journaliste, réaliste, féminisme
    -ible / -able -ible / -able possible, visible, confortable, acceptable
    -if / -ive -ive actif, créatif, positif → active, creative
    -ie -y biologie, géographie, économie, philosophie
    -er (verb) -ate décorer, participer, éduquer, imiter
    -iser / -ifier -ise / -ify organiser, simplifier, identifier
    • La révolution a transformé la nation. — The revolution transformed the nation.
    • À l’université, j’ai découvert la liberté. — At university, I discovered freedom (liberty).
    • C’est une solution logique et pratique. — It’s a logical, practical solution.
    • Ce chemin est dangereux, et un peu mystérieux. — This path is dangerous, and a bit mysterious.
    • Elle est nerveuse avant l’examen. — She is nervous before the exam. (feminine: -euse)
    • Il a répondu rapidement et poliment. — He answered quickly and politely.
    • Mon frère est optimiste ; ma sœur est réaliste. — My brother is an optimist; my sister is a realist.

    Endings tell you the gender too

    Nouns in -tion, -sion, -ité, -ie are almost always feminine; nouns in -isme, -ment are masculine. See Nouns, gender & plurals.

    -ment has two jobs

    On an adjective it makes an adverb: lente → lentement (slowly). On a verb it makes a masculine noun: gouverner → le gouvernement. And one -ment adverb is a famous trap: actuellement means “currently”, not “actually” (section 6).

    45.3 Spelling clues: decode the disguised words¶

    Some words are cognates in disguise: the sounds changed a little over the centuries. These five clues unlock them. They are guessing tools, not laws — but they work surprisingly often.

    Clue French English
    é- at the start = s- école, étudiant, épice, étrange, état, éponge, épouse school, student, spice, strange, state, sponge, spouse
    ê, ô, î, â = a lost s forêt, hôpital, île, fête, bête, côte, intérêt, honnête, château forest, hospital, isle, feast, beast, coast, interest, honest, castle
    past participle -é = -ed organisé, préparé, compliqué, intéressé organised, prepared, complicated, interested
    g(u)- = w- guerre, garder, guêpe, gaufre, Guillaume war, ward (guard), wasp, wafer, William
    ch- = c- chat, château, chameau, charpentier cat, castle, camel, carpenter
    -re = -er ordre, membre, nombre, tigre, octobre order, member, number, tiger, October
    • L’étudiant va à l’école. — The student goes to school.
    • Il y a une fête dans la forêt. — There’s a feast (party) in the forest.
    • L’hôpital est près de la côte. — The hospital is near the coast.
    • Ce projet est bien organisé et bien préparé. — This project is well organised and well prepared.
    • Une guêpe est entrée dans la cuisine. — A wasp came into the kitchen.
    • Le chat dort dans le château. — The cat is sleeping in the castle.

    The circumflex trick

    When you see ê, ô, î, â, mentally add an s after the vowel: forêt → forest, hôte → host, pâte → paste.

    45.4 Same word, new sound, new gender¶

    A cognate is free for meaning, but never for sound, gender or spelling. Three things change.

    Sound: stress the end, drop the last consonant¶

    • un restaurant — a restaurant [res-to-RÃ]
    • une nation — a nation — -tion sounds “syõ” [na-SYÕ]
    • une question — a question — but after s, -tion sounds “tyõ” [kes-TYÕ]
    • un hôtel — a hotel — the h is silent [ɛ̃-no-TEL]
    • important — important [ɛ̃-por-TÃ]

    Gender: the ending can lie¶

    English has no gender, so cognates arrive “naked”. Learn each one with its article. These surprise learners because they end in -e but are masculine — or end in -age but are feminine.

    • le problème, le système, le programme, le groupe — masculine, although they end in -e
    • le musée, le lycée, le modèle — the museum, the high school, the model — masculine
    • une image, une page, une plage, une cage — feminine, although most -age words (le voyage, le message) are masculine

    Spelling: almost the same is still wrong¶

    English French What changes
    address adresse one d, -esse
    apartment appartement pp, extra e
    example exemple e, not a
    language langage / langue no u; a language = une langue
    literature littérature tt, é
    responsible responsable -able, not -ible
    marriage mariage one r
    character caractère no h, è

    45.5 True, partial and false friends¶

    Not every look-alike is a trap. Think of a traffic light. Green words mean the same — use them. Amber words share one meaning but their main meaning is different — slow down. Red words are false friends — stop.

    The amber zone: partial friends¶

    French Main meaning Shared with English?
    la chance luck (avoir de la chance = be lucky) also “likelihood”; an opportunity = une occasion
    la figure face also a figure in maths or a diagram
    l’histoire (f.) story also history
    réaliser make, carry out, fulfil (a dream, a film) “realise” = se rendre compte; réaliser is used for it in speech, but is criticised
    ignorer not know, be unaware of also ignore someone
    supporter put up with, stand also support a team; support a person or cause = soutenir
    l’expérience (f.) experience also a scientific experiment
    la place seat; square; room (space) a place = un endroit, un lieu
    l’envie (f.) wish, craving (avoir envie de = feel like) also envy, but jealousy = la jalousie
    contrôler check, inspect control (a skill, emotions) = maîtriser
    terrible terrible — but pas terrible = not great; in slang terrible ! = awesome yes, with a twist
    • J’ai eu de la chance : le train était en retard. — I was lucky: the train was late.
    • Elle a réalisé son rêve : elle a ouvert un restaurant. — She fulfilled her dream: she opened a restaurant.
    • Je me suis rendu compte de mon erreur. — I realised my mistake. (no agreement on rendu here)
    • J’ignore pourquoi il est parti. — I don’t know why he left.
    • Je ne supporte pas le bruit. — I can’t stand noise.
    • Excusez-moi, cette place est libre ? — Excuse me, is this seat free?

    45.6 The false friends you must know¶

    Here are the red-light words, grouped by type. Read each row as: this French word means X; if you want the English look-alike, say Y. Learn ten a day.

    Things and places¶

    French Real meaning English trap → in French
    la librairie bookshop library → la bibliothèque
    le coin corner coin → une pièce (de monnaie)
    le pain bread pain → la douleur
    le four oven four → quatre
    le car coach (long-distance bus) car → une voiture
    la cave cellar cave → une grotte
    le store (window) blind store → un magasin
    la location renting, rental location → un endroit, un emplacement

    le crayon pencil crayon → un crayon de cire
    le préservatif condom preservative → un conservateur
    l’agenda diary, planner agenda (meeting) → l’ordre du jour

    Work, news and abstract words¶

    French Real meaning English trap → in French

    l’habit outfit; (les habits) clothes habit → une habitude
    le hasard chance (par hasard = by chance) hazard → un danger, un risque
    la réunion meeting reunion → des retrouvailles

    Adjectives and adverbs¶

    French Real meaning English trap → in French
    actuel / actuellement current / currently, now actual(ly) → réel / en fait
    éventuel / éventuellement possible / possibly, if need be eventually → finalement, à la fin
    sensible sensitive sensible → raisonnable, sensé
    sympathique (sympa) nice, friendly sympathetic → compatissant, compréhensif
    formidable great, terrific formidable → redoutable
    large wide large → grand
    rude harsh, tough (a winter, a job) rude → impoli, grossier
    excité hyper, worked up; often sexually aroused excited → j’ai hâte, enthousiaste
    gentil kind, nice gentle → doux
    propre clean; own (ma propre voiture) proper → convenable, correct
    sale dirty sale → les soldes

    Verbs¶

    French Real meaning English trap → in French
    assister à attend (be present at) assist (help) → aider (assister quelqu’un exists, but is formal)
    attendre wait (for) attend → assister à, aller à
    blesser injure, hurt bless → bénir
    achever finish, complete achieve → atteindre, accomplir
    demander ask (for) demand → exiger
    prétendre claim pretend → faire semblant
    rester stay, remain rest → se reposer
    passer un examen take, sit an exam pass an exam → réussir un examen
    • Actuellement, je travaille à Lyon. — At the moment, I work in Lyon.
    • J’ai acheté un roman à la librairie. — I bought a novel at the bookshop.
    • Ma fille est très sensible : elle pleure au cinéma. — My daughter is very sensitive: she cries at the cinema.
    • Éventuellement, je peux venir samedi. — If need be, I can come on Saturday.
    • Nous avons assisté à un concert magnifique. — We attended a wonderful concert.
    • Je t’attends devant la gare. — I’ll wait for you outside the station. (no “for”: attendre quelqu’un)
    • Il s’est blessé au genou. — He hurt his knee.
    • La boulangerie est au coin de la rue. — The bakery is on the corner of the street.
    • Location de vélos : dix euros la journée. — Bike rental: ten euros a day.
    • Il prétend qu’il parle six langues. — He claims he speaks six languages.

    The top five traps

    actuellement = now · librairie = bookshop · sensible = sensitive · éventuellement = possibly · attendre = wait. Actually = en fait.

    45.7 The traps in real sentences¶

    These are the mistakes French people hear every day from English speakers. Some are funny, some are embarrassing — the last one is both.

    Careful

    • Wrong: Actuellement, je suis indien, pas pakistanais.
    • Right: En fait, je suis indien, pas pakistanais.
    • Why: To correct someone (“actually…”), say en fait. Actuellement only means “at the moment”.

    Careful

    • Wrong: Je vais à la librairie pour emprunter un livre.
    • Right: Je vais à la bibliothèque pour emprunter un livre.
    • Why: You borrow at a bibliothèque (library); you buy at a librairie (bookshop).

    Careful

    • Wrong: J’ai attendu la réunion hier.
    • Right: J’ai assisté à la réunion hier.
    • Why: Attendre = wait for. To attend an event = assister à (or simply aller à).

    Careful

    • Wrong: Il est très sensible, il prend toujours de bonnes décisions.
    • Right: Il est très raisonnable, il prend toujours de bonnes décisions.
    • Why: Sensible = sensitive. Sensible = raisonnable or sensé.

    Careful

    • Wrong: Je suis fatigué, je vais rester un peu.
    • Right: Je suis fatigué, je vais me reposer un peu.
    • Why: Rester = stay. To rest = se reposer.

    Careful

    • Wrong: J’ai passé mon examen, je suis trop content !
    • Right: J’ai réussi mon examen, je suis trop content !
    • Why: Passer un examen only means you sat it. Passing = réussir.

    Careful

    • Wrong: Je suis très excité pour mes vacances.
    • Right: J’ai vraiment hâte d’être en vacances.
    • Why: Excité suggests hyper or sexually aroused. “I can’t wait” = j’ai hâte.

    Careful

    • Wrong: Ce yaourt ne contient pas de préservatifs.
    • Right: Ce yaourt ne contient pas de conservateurs.
    • Why: A préservatif is a condom. Food preservatives are conservateurs.

    In real life: a conversation full of traps¶

    • — Tu fais quoi actuellement ? — — What are you doing these days?
    • — Je travaille dans une librairie, et le soir je donne des cours. — — I work in a bookshop, and in the evening I give lessons.
    • — Et ton patron, il est sympa ? — — And your boss, is he nice?
    • — Très gentil, mais un peu sensible. Éventuellement, je pourrais changer de travail. — — Very kind, but a bit touchy. I could possibly change jobs, if need be.

    When to trust a cognate

    Trust long, Latin-looking words (-tion, -ité, -ique). Distrust short everyday words (coin, pain, car, four, sale) — those are where the traps live.

    À retenir

    • Most French words that look English are English in meaning — but a small, famous group are traps. Learn the patterns, then learn the traps by heart.
    • Cognates are easy to see but hard to hear: French stresses the last syllable and drops final consonants. Read the text, then listen to it.
    • Nouns in -tion, -sion, -ité, -ie are almost always feminine; nouns in -isme, -ment are masculine. See Nouns, gender & plurals.
    • When you see ê, ô, î, â, mentally add an s after the vowel: forêt → forest, hôte → host, pâte → paste.
    • actuellement = now · librairie = bookshop · sensible = sensitive · éventuellement = possibly · attendre = wait. Actually = en fait.
    • Trust long, Latin-looking words (-tion, -ité, -ique). Distrust short everyday words (coin, pain, car, four, sale) — those are where the traps live.

    45.8 Practice — cognates & false friends¶

    Exercise 1

    Je vais à la ___ pour emprunter un livre. (borrow a book)

    1. librairie
    2. bibliothèque
    3. libraire
    4. boutique

    Hint: Where do you borrow, not buy?

    Answer

    bibliothèque

    You borrow at a bibliothèque (library). A librairie is a bookshop.

    Exercise 2

    What does 'actuellement' mean?

    1. actually
    2. currently, at the moment
    3. eventually
    4. accurately
    Answer

    currently, at the moment

    Actuellement = now. 'Actually' is en fait.

    Exercise 3

    'Mon fils est très sensible.' means…

    1. My son is very sensible.
    2. My son is very sensitive.
    3. My son is very sensual.
    4. My son is very reasonable.
    Answer

    My son is very sensitive.

    Sensible = sensitive. Sensible in English = raisonnable.

    Exercise 4

    Use the é- = s- clue. What does 'une épice' mean?

    1. an epic
    2. a spice
    3. a speech
    4. a space
    Answer

    a spice

    é- at the start often stands for English s-: épice = spice, école = school.

    Exercise 5

    ___ problème est difficile.

    1. Le
    2. La

    Hint: A masculine noun ending in -e

    Answer

    Le

    Problème, système, programme are masculine despite the final -e.

    Exercise 6

    The -ty ending becomes -ité. University = ___

    Answer

    université / l'université

    -ty → -ité, and -ité nouns are feminine: l'université.

    Exercise 7

    The circumflex marks a lost s. Forest = ___

    Answer

    forêt / la forêt / foret

    forêt = forest; hôpital = hospital; fête = feast.

    Exercise 8

    Je suis fatigué, je vais me ___ (rest) un peu.

    Answer

    reposer

    To rest = se reposer. Rester means to stay.

    Exercise 9

    Elle est très nerv___ avant les examens. (nervous)

    Hint: feminine of -eux

    Answer

    euse / nerveuse

    -ous → -eux (m.) / -euse (f.): nerveux, nerveuse.

    Exercise 10

    Tap the false friend.

    Hier, j'ai attendu la réunion de dix heures.

    Answer

    Wrong word: attendu.

    Corrected: Hier, j'ai assisté à la réunion de dix heures.

    To attend = assister à. Attendre = to wait for.

    Exercise 11

    Tap the false friend.

    Ce gâteau ne contient aucun préservatif.

    Answer

    Wrong word: préservatif.

    Corrected: Ce gâteau ne contient aucun conservateur.

    Un préservatif is a condom; a preservative is un conservateur.

    Exercise 12

    Tap the word that does not mean what the speaker wants (he wants 'actually').

    Actuellement je ne suis pas d'accord avec toi.

    Answer

    Wrong word: Actuellement.

    Corrected: En fait je ne suis pas d'accord avec toi.

    Actually = en fait. Actuellement = at the moment.

    Exercise 13

    Match each false friend to its real meaning.

    AB
    la librairiebread
    le coinoven
    le paincellar
    le fourbookshop
    la cavecorner
    Answer

    la librairie — bookshop; le coin — corner; le pain — bread; le four — oven; la cave — cellar

    Short everyday words are where most false friends hide.

    Exercise 14

    Match the verbs to their real meaning.

    AB
    resterto wait for
    blesserto claim
    attendreto finish
    prétendreto stay
    acheverto hurt
    Answer

    rester — to stay; blesser — to hurt; attendre — to wait for; prétendre — to claim; achever — to finish

    None of these mean rest, bless, attend, pretend or achieve.

    Exercise 15

    Put the words in order: 'Actually, I am Indian.'

    Words: je / suis / indien / fait / En

    Answer

    En fait je suis indien

    En fait corrects or adds a surprising fact.

    Exercise 16

    Put the words in order: 'He claims he is ill.'

    Words: est / Il / qu'il / prétend / malade

    Answer

    Il prétend qu'il est malade

    Prétendre = to claim; to pretend = faire semblant.

    Exercise 17

    Say it aloud.

    J'ai acheté un roman à la librairie

    Answer

    Say: J'ai acheté un roman à la librairie

    Librairie = bookshop, roman = novel.

    Exercise 18

    Say it aloud.

    Actuellement je travaille dans un restaurant

    Answer

    Say: Actuellement je travaille dans un restaurant

    Restaurant ends in a nasal: [res-to-RÃ], no t.
