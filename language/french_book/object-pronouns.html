    Chapter 18: Object pronouns¶

    les pronoms COD et COI

    “Do you see Marc?” — “Yes, I see him.” Nobody repeats the name; we use a small word like him, her, it or them. French does the same, but with two surprises: the little word goes before the verb, and you must choose between a direct and an indirect pronoun. This chapter makes both easy.

    masculinefeminineplural

    In one line

    An object pronoun replaces a noun, and in French it goes before the verb: Je vois Marc → Je le vois.

    • Tu vois Marc ? — Oui, je le vois. — Do you see Marc? — Yes, I see him. [zhuh luh VWA]
    • Tu aimes la pizza ? — Oui, je l’aime. — Do you like pizza? — Yes, I like it. [zhuh LɛM]
    • Tu parles à ta mère ? — Oui, je lui parle. — Are you talking to your mother? — Yes, I’m talking to her. [zhuh lwee PARL]

    18.1 Direct or indirect? The one question to ask¶

    An object is the person or thing that receives the action. French splits objects into two kinds, and each kind has its own pronouns.

    Kind How to spot it Example
    COD
    complément d’objet direct    		 Comes straight after the verb, no preposition Je regarde le film.
    COI
    complément d’objet indirect    		 Comes after à + a person Je parle à Julie.

    So the test is simple: look at the French verb. Is there an à between the verb and the person? Then it is indirect. No preposition at all? Then it is direct.

    Think French, not English

    Decide direct or indirect from the French verb, never from English: écouter is direct (“listen to” is not), téléphoner à is indirect (“phone” is not).

    18.2 The direct pronouns: le, la, l’, les¶

    A direct pronoun can replace a person or a thing. It agrees with the noun it replaces: masculine, feminine or plural.

    Pronoun Replaces Example
    le [luh] him / it (masc.) Je prends le bus → je le prends.
    la [la] her / it (fem.) Il appelle sa sœur → il l’appelle.
    l’ le or la before a vowel or mute h J’aime ce livre → je l’aime.
    les [lay] them (people or things) Je fais les courses → je les fais.
    • Où est ma clé ? Je ne la trouve pas. — Where is my key? I can’t find it. [zhuh nuh la troov PA]
    • Tu connais mon frère ? — Oui, je le connais bien. — Do you know my brother? — Yes, I know him well.
    • Les enfants ? Je les emmène à l’école. — The children? I take them to school. [zhuh lay‿zãm-MɛN]
    • Ce café, je le bois sans sucre. — This coffee, I drink it without sugar.
    • Il l’habite depuis dix ans, cette maison. — He has lived in it for ten years, this house.

    Liaison with les

    Before a vowel, les links with a z sound: je les‿aime [zhuh lay-ZɛM]. Without it, French ears hear “je lé ème” — strange and hard to follow.

    18.3 The indirect pronouns: lui, leur — and the four “both” pronouns¶

    Indirect pronouns replace à + a person (or an animal). There are only two third-person forms, and they do not show gender.

    Pronoun Replaces Example
    lui [lwee] à + him or her Je parle à Paul / à Léa → je lui parle.
    leur [luhr] à + them J’écris à mes parents → je leur écris.
    • Tu as un message pour Léa ? Je lui téléphone ce soir. — Do you have a message for Léa? I’m phoning her tonight.
    • Mes grands-parents ? Je leur rends visite le dimanche. — My grandparents? I visit them on Sundays. [zhuh luhr rã vee-ZEET]
    • Le prof nous donne trop de devoirs. — The teacher gives us too much homework.

    The full set¶

    Person Direct (COD) Indirect (COI)
    je me (m’) me (m’)
    tu te (t’) te (t’)
    il / elle le / la (l’) lui
    nous nous nous
    vous vous vous
    ils / elles les leur
    • Tu m’entends ? — Can you hear me? (direct) [tew mã-TÃ]
    • Tu me donnes ton numéro ? — Will you give me your number? (indirect: donner à)
    • Je t’aime. — I love you. [zhuh TɛM]
    • Je vous écoute. — I’m listening (to you). — what a shop assistant says when it’s your turn [zhuh voo‿zay-KOOT]

    lui and leur

    lui = to him or to her; leur = to them. Only people, and never an -s on the pronoun leur.

    Careful

    • Wrong: Je leurs parle souvent.
    • Right: Je leur parle souvent.
    • Why: The pronoun leur (“to them”) never takes -s. Only the possessive does: leurs enfants (their children).

    18.4 The verbs that trick English speakers¶

    Here is the real difficulty. Many verbs are direct in French but need a preposition in English — and the other way round. Learn each verb with its construction.

    Direct in French (no à!) — but not in English¶

    French English With a pronoun
    écouter qqn listen to sb Je l’écoute.
    regarder qqn look at sb Il la regarde.
    attendre qqn wait for sb On les attend.
    chercher qqn look for sb Je le cherche.
    payer qqch pay for sth Je le paie.
    aider qqn help sb Je t’aide.

    Indirect in French (with à) — but direct in English¶

    French English With a pronoun
    téléphoner à qqn phone sb Je lui téléphone.
    répondre à qqn answer sb Elle leur répond.
    parler à qqn talk to sb Tu lui parles ?
    ressembler à qqn look like sb Il lui ressemble.
    obéir à qqn obey sb Ils ne leur obéissent pas.
    plaire à qqn please sb (be liked by) Ça me plaît.
    sourire à qqn smile at sb Le bébé nous sourit.

    Giving and telling: something to someone¶

    Verbs of giving and communicating take both: a direct thing and an indirect person — donner, envoyer, dire, demander, montrer, prêter, écrire, expliquer + quelque chose à quelqu’un.

    • Je lui envoie un message. — I’m sending him/her a message.
    • Tu leur dis la vérité ? — Are you telling them the truth?
    • Elle m’explique la règle. — She explains the rule to me.
    • Je lui demande l’heure. — I ask him/her the time. (demander qqch à qqn)

    Two pronouns together (je le lui donne) follow a fixed order — see Y, en & pronoun order.

    Translating the English preposition

    • Wrong: Je lui écoute.
    • Right: Je l’écoute.

    • Why: Écouter is direct: écouter quelqu’un. The “to” of “listen to” does not exist in French.

    • Wrong: Je la téléphone demain.

    • Right: Je lui téléphone demain.

    • Why: Téléphoner à quelqu’un is indirect, so the pronoun is lui, even for a woman.

    • Wrong: J’attends pour le bus.

    • Right: J’attends le bus. → Je l’attends.
    • Why: Attendre already means “wait for”. Adding pour is a direct copy of English.

    One exception group: penser à, s’intéresser à¶

    A few verbs with à refuse lui/leur for people. They keep à + a stressed pronoun (section 7): penser à, tenir à, faire attention à, s’intéresser à, s’habituer à.

    • Je pense à elle. — I’m thinking of her. (not *je lui pense)
    • Fais attention à lui, il est fragile. — Be careful with him, he is fragile.

    And for things after à, French uses y: Tu penses à ton examen ? — Oui, j’y pense. More in Y, en & pronoun order.

    18.5 Where the pronoun goes¶

    English puts the pronoun after the verb (“I see him”). French puts it directly in front of the verb it belongs to. Think of the pronoun as glued to the front of that verb.

    Negative: ne + pronoun + verb + pas¶

    • Je ne le connais pas. — I don’t know him. [zhuhn luh ko-nɛ PA]
    • Elle ne leur répond jamais. — She never answers them.

    Questions¶

    • Tu la vois ? / Est-ce que tu la vois ? — Do you see her? (everyday)
    • La voyez-vous ? — Do you see her? (formal inversion: pronoun + verb-subject)

    Verb + infinitive: before the infinitive¶

    With aller, vouloir, pouvoir, devoir, aimer… + infinitive, the pronoun belongs to the infinitive, because that is the verb whose object it is.

    • Je vais l’appeler. — I’m going to call him/her.
    • Tu peux m’aider ? — Can you help me? [tew puh may-DAY]
    • Je ne veux pas leur parler. — I don’t want to talk to them.

    Careful

    • Wrong: Je le vais acheter.
    • Right: Je vais l’acheter.
    • Why: You buy it, you don’t “go it”. The pronoun goes in front of acheter. (The old order je le vais acheter survives only in classic literature.)

    Position

    Pronoun before the verb it belongs to: before the conjugated verb (je le vois), before an infinitive (je vais le voir), before the auxiliary (je l’ai vu).

    One exception: in a positive command the pronoun goes after the verb — Regarde-le ! Aide-moi ! See Giving instructions.

    18.6 In the past: je l’ai vu (a first look at agreement)¶

    In the passé composé there are two verb pieces: the auxiliary (ai, as, a…) and the past participle (vu, mangé, pris…). The pronoun goes in front of the auxiliary.

    • Le film ? Je l’ai vu hier. — The film? I saw it yesterday. [zhuh lay vew YɛR]
    • Ton père ? Je ne lui ai pas encore parlé. — Your father? I haven’t talked to him yet.

    The surprise: the participle can agree¶

    When a direct pronoun (la, les, me, te, nous, vous) stands before avoir, the past participle agrees with it — like an adjective. Usually you only see it in writing; with some verbs (pris → prise, fait → faite, mis → mise) you also hear it.

    • Ta sœur ? Je l’ai vue ce matin. — Your sister? I saw her this morning. (vue: feminine)
    • Les clés ? Je les ai prises. — The keys? I took them. (prises — you hear the z) [zhuh lay‿zay PREEZ]
    • Ma mère ? Je lui ai téléphoné. — My mother? I phoned her. (lui is indirect → no agreement)

    Agreement in one sentence

    After avoir, the participle agrees only with a direct object placed before it — never with lui or leur. Full rules in The past: passé composé.

    18.7 Stressed pronouns: moi, toi, lui, elle, eux¶

    French has a third set of pronouns, the stressed (or “disjunctive”) pronouns. They stand on their own, outside the verb block, and they carry emphasis.

    Subject Stressed Example
    je moi [mwa] C’est moi.
    tu toi [twa] Et toi ?
    il / elle lui / elle avec lui / chez elle
    nous / vous nous / vous Nous, on reste.
    ils / elles eux [uh] / elles sans eux / pour elles

    Note that lui has two jobs: the indirect “to him/her” (before the verb), and the stressed “him” (after a preposition).

    When to use them¶

    • Tu viens avec moi ? — Are you coming with me? — after a preposition (avec, pour, chez, sans…)
    • Moi, je préfère le thé. — Me, I prefer tea. — for emphasis or contrast
    • J’ai faim. — Moi aussi ! / Pas moi. — I’m hungry. — Me too! / Not me. — short answers with no verb
    • Qui a fait ça ? — C’est lui ! — Who did that? — It was him! — after c’est
    • Ils sont plus grands que nous. — They are taller than us. — after que in comparisons
    • On mange chez eux ce soir. — We’re eating at their place tonight. [shay ZUH]

    Hindi helps here

    “Main to chai peeunga” — that stressed “main to” is exactly French Moi, je bois du thé. Emphasis goes in a separate word, the normal pronoun stays.

    Careful

    • Wrong: Il vient avec je. / C’est il.
    • Right: Il vient avec moi. / C’est lui.
    • Why: Subject pronouns (je, tu, il) can only stand right before a verb. Anywhere else, use the stressed form.

    Careful

    • Wrong: Moi suis indien.
    • Right: Moi, je suis indien.
    • Why: Moi adds emphasis but cannot replace je as the subject. Keep both.

    18.8 In real life: at the office¶

    Spot every object pronoun, then say which noun it replaces.

    • — Tu as vu le rapport de Sophie ? — — Have you seen Sophie’s report?
    • — Oui, je l’ai lu ce matin. Je lui ai envoyé mes remarques. — — Yes, I read it this morning. I sent her my comments.
    • — Et les clients ? Tu leur as répondu ? — — And the clients? Did you answer them?
    • — Pas encore. Je vais les appeler après le déjeuner. — — Not yet. I’m going to call them after lunch.
    • — Parfait. Si tu as besoin d’aide, je suis là. Compte sur moi ! — — Perfect. If you need help, I’m here. Count on me!

    l’ = le rapport (direct) · lui = à Sophie (envoyer qqch à qqn) · leur = aux clients (répondre à) · les = les clients (appeler qqn, direct) · moi = after the preposition sur.

    Same people, different pronoun

    The clients are leur with répondre à but les with appeler. The pronoun depends on the verb, not on the person.

    À retenir

    • An object pronoun replaces a noun, and in French it goes before the verb: Je vois Marc → Je le vois.
    • Decide direct or indirect from the French verb, never from English: écouter is direct (“listen to” is not), téléphoner à is indirect (“phone” is not).
    • lui = to him or to her; leur = to them. Only people, and never an -s on the pronoun leur.
    • Pronoun before the verb it belongs to: before the conjugated verb (je le vois), before an infinitive (je vais le voir), before the auxiliary (je l’ai vu).
    • After avoir, the participle agrees only with a direct object placed before it — never with lui or leur. Full rules in The past: passé composé.
    • “Main to chai peeunga” — that stressed “main to” is exactly French Moi, je bois du thé. Emphasis goes in a separate word, the normal pronoun stays.

    18.9 Practice — Object pronouns¶

    Exercise 1

    Tu regardes la télé ? — Oui, je ___ regarde.

    1. la
    2. lui
    3. le
    4. leur

    Hint: regarder is direct; la télé is feminine

    Answer

    la

    regarder + direct object, feminine → la.

    Exercise 2

    Tu téléphones à Marc ? — Oui, je ___ téléphone.

    1. le
    2. lui
    3. l'
    4. leur

    Hint: téléphoner à…

    Answer

    lui

    téléphoner à quelqu'un is indirect → lui.

    Exercise 3

    Tu attends tes amis ? — Oui, je ___ attends.

    1. leur
    2. les
    3. lui
    4. y

    Hint: attendre = wait for, but is it direct?

    Answer

    les

    attendre is direct in French: attendre quelqu'un → les.

    Exercise 4

    Tu écris à tes parents ? — Oui, je ___ écris.

    1. les
    2. leurs
    3. leur
    4. lui
    Answer

    leur

    écrire à + plural people → leur (no -s).

    Exercise 5

    Which sentence is correct?

    1. Je vais le voir.
    2. Je le vais voir.
    3. Je vais voir le.
    4. Je vais lui voir.
    Answer

    Je vais le voir.

    With verb + infinitive, the pronoun goes before the infinitive.

    Exercise 6

    Tu écoutes la radio ? — Oui, je ___ écoute.

    Hint: before a vowel

    Answer

    l' / l’

    écouter is direct; la before a vowel → l'.

    Exercise 7

    Tu viens avec ___ ? (with me)

    Hint: after a preposition

    Answer

    moi

    After a preposition use the stressed pronoun moi.

    Exercise 8

    Ta sœur ? Je l'ai ___ (voir) hier.

    Hint: la before avoir → agreement

    Answer

    vue

    The direct pronoun l' (= ta sœur, feminine) comes before avoir, so vu takes -e: vue.

    Exercise 9

    Tu parles à Julie ? — Non, je ne ___ parle pas.

    Answer

    lui

    parler à + one person → lui, inside ne … pas.

    Exercise 10

    Tap the wrong word.

    Je lui écoute tous les jours.

    Answer

    Wrong word: lui.

    Corrected: Je l'écoute tous les jours.

    écouter is direct: écouter quelqu'un → le / la / l'.

    Exercise 11

    Tap the wrong word.

    Elle la répond toujours poliment.

    Answer

    Wrong word: la.

    Corrected: Elle lui répond toujours poliment.

    répondre à quelqu'un is indirect → lui.

    Exercise 12

    Tap the wrong word.

    C'est il qui a gagné.

    Answer

    Wrong word: il.

    Corrected: C'est lui qui a gagné.

    After c'est use the stressed pronoun lui.

    Exercise 13

    Put the words in order: I don't know them.

    Words: Je / ne / pas / les / connais

    Answer

    Je ne les connais pas

    ne + pronoun + verb + pas.

    Exercise 14

    Put the words in order: Can you help me?

    Words: peux / Tu / m'aider

    Answer

    Tu peux m'aider

    The pronoun goes before the infinitive aider.

    Exercise 15

    Match the verb to its construction.

    AB
    écouterà + moi, lui, elle…
    téléphonerdirect: le / la / les
    penser à (a person)direct: le / la / les
    attendreindirect: lui / leur
    Answer

    écouter — direct: le / la / les; téléphoner — indirect: lui / leur; penser à (a person) — à + moi, lui, elle…; attendre — direct: le / la / les

    Learn each verb with its construction — French, not English, decides.

    Exercise 16

    Say it aloud.

    Je lui téléphone ce soir

    Answer

    Say: Je lui téléphone ce soir

    lui = to him or to her; téléphoner à is indirect.

    Exercise 17

    Say it aloud — make the liaison in les‿amis.

    Je les aime beaucoup

    Answer

    Say: Je les aime beaucoup

    les + vowel → z liaison: lay-ZɛM.
