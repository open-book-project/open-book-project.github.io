    Chapter 5: Hello, please, thank you¶

    Les premiers mots

    With about forty words you can greet people, be polite, say who you are and survive when you do not understand. In France these little words matter a lot: a missing bonjour can feel rude. This chapter gives you the words, the right moment for each one, and the big choice every speaker makes: tu or vous?

    Rule number one

    Always start with Bonjour — in a shop, a bus, an office, before any question. Add Monsieur or Madame for a stranger.

    • Bonjour, Monsieur. — Hello, sir. [bõ-zhoor muh-SYÖ]
    • Bonjour, Madame. — Hello, madam. [bõ-zhoor ma-DAM]

    5.1 Saying hello at every time of day¶

    French has no separate “good morning” and “good afternoon”. Bonjour (literally “good day”) covers the whole day. In the evening it becomes bonsoir.

    French When
    Bonjour [bõ-ZHOOR] Hello / good morning / good afternoon — polite, all day
    Bonsoir [bõ-SWAR] Good evening — arriving after about 6 pm
    Salut [sa-LEW] Hi / bye — friends, family, people your age
    Coucou [koo-KOO] Hiya — very casual, close friends, children, text messages
    Bonne nuit [bon NWEE] Good night — going to bed only

    Careful

    • Wrong: (Arriving at a dinner at 8 pm) Bonne nuit !
    • Right: Bonsoir !
    • Why: Like English “good night”, bonne nuit means “sleep well”. To greet in the evening, say bonsoir.

    Careful

    • Wrong: (To a bank manager) Salut !
    • Right: Bonjour, Madame.
    • Why: Salut is informal, like “hi” — fine with friends, too casual for strangers or at work.

    Monsieur, Madame — and the bise¶

    Use Monsieur [muh-SYÖ] and Madame [ma-DAM] alone, without a surname: Bonjour, Madame. Use Madame for any adult woman: Mademoiselle is now old-fashioned and has been dropped from official forms. Friends and family greet with la bise, light cheek kisses (two in Paris, more in some regions); at work and with strangers, a handshake or just a smile and a bonjour.

    5.2 Saying goodbye¶

    • Au revoir ! — Goodbye! — always correct [oh ruh-VWAR]
    • À bientôt ! — See you soon! [a byɛ̃-TOH]
    • À demain ! — See you tomorrow! [a duh-MƐ̃]
    • À tout à l’heure ! — See you later (today)! [a too-ta LÖR]
    • À plus tard ! / À plus ! — See you later! / Later! (casual — in texts: A+) [a plew TAR / a PLEWSS]
    • À la prochaine ! — Until next time! [a la pro-SHEN]

    Wishing a good day¶

    When you leave a shop or end a call, add a wish. The answer is always Merci, vous aussi ! (Thanks, you too!) or, with a friend, Toi aussi !

    • Bonne journée ! — Merci, vous aussi ! — Have a nice day! — Thanks, you too! [bon zhoor-NAY … voo-zoh-SEE]
    • Bonne soirée ! Bon week-end ! — Have a nice evening! Have a good weekend! [bon swa-RAY, bõ wee-KEND]
    • Bon appétit ! Bon voyage ! — Enjoy your meal! Have a good trip! [bo-na-pay-TEE, bõ vwa-YAZH]

    5.3 Please, thank you, you’re welcome¶

    French English
    s’il vous plaît please (polite / plural)
    s’il te plaît please (to a friend)
    merci / merci beaucoup thank you / thank you very much
    merci bien / mille mercis thanks a lot / a thousand thanks
    de rien you’re welcome (everyday)
    je vous en prie / je t’en prie you’re welcome (polite / to a friend)
    avec plaisir with pleasure
    pas de quoi no problem (casual)
    • Un thé, s’il vous plaît. — Voilà. — Merci beaucoup ! — Je vous en prie. — A tea, please. — Here you are. — Thank you very much! — You're welcome.
    • Tu me passes le sel, s’il te plaît ? — Tiens. — Merci ! — De rien. — Can you pass me the salt, please? — Here. — Thanks! — No problem.

    merci = no, thanks

    When someone offers you something, Merci alone (often with a small hand gesture) can mean “no, thank you”. To accept, say Oui, merci or Avec plaisir.

    Careful

    • Wrong: Merci très beaucoup.
    • Right: Merci beaucoup. / Merci mille fois.
    • Why: beaucoup already means “very much”. Never put très before it.

    In Belgium, s’il vous plaît is also used to mean “here you are” when handing something over. In France, you say voilà or tenez (tiens to a friend).

    5.4 Excuse me, sorry, pardon¶

    French Use it to…
    Excusez-moi (Excuse-moi with tu) get attention before a question; apologise for a small thing
    Pardon ! bump into someone; squeeze past
    Pardon ? “Sorry, what did you say?”
    Je suis désolé / désolée say you are really sorry (regret)
    • Excusez-moi, Madame, où est la gare ? — Excuse me, madam, where is the station? [ex-kew-zay-MWA … oo eh la GAR]
    • Oh, pardon ! — Oh, sorry! (bumping into someone) [par-DÕ]
    • Je suis désolée, je suis en retard. — I'm sorry, I'm late. (a woman speaking: désolée) [day-zo-LAY … ã ruh-TAR]

    Désolé agrees with the speaker: a man writes désolé, a woman désolée. Both sound exactly the same.

    5.5 Tu or vous?¶

    French has two words for “you”. Tu is informal and singular. Vous is polite (for one person) and the normal plural (for several people, polite or not). If you speak Hindi, you already know the idea:

    Hindi French With whom
    tum tu friends, family, children, classmates
    aap vous strangers, elders, shops, work, officials
    tum log / aap log vous any group of two or more people

    Careful: Hindi tu (very intimate, or rude) is stronger than French tu. French tu is closer to tum.

    tu vs vous

    vous = one stranger (polite) or any group. tu = one friend, relative or child. Not sure? vous.

    The same question, two ways¶

    With tu With vous
    Comment tu t’appelles ? Comment vous appelez-vous ?
    Ça va ? / Tu vas bien ? Comment allez-vous ?
    Tu es d’où ? Vous êtes d’où ?
    Et toi ? Et vous ?
    s’il te plaît s’il vous plaît

    Careful

    • Wrong: (To a shop assistant) Tu as du lait ?
    • Right: Vous avez du lait ?
    • Why: Using tu with an adult stranger sounds rude or childish. The verb changes too: tu as → vous avez.

    5.6 Introducing yourself¶

    Your name¶

    • Je m’appelle Priya. — My name is Priya. (literally: I call myself Priya) [zhuh ma-PEL pree-YA]
    • Moi, c’est Arjun. — I'm Arjun. (relaxed, very common) [MWA, seh ar-JOON]
    • Enchanté ! / Enchantée ! — Nice to meet you! (a man writes enchanté, a woman enchantée — same sound) [ã-shã-TAY]

    Careful

    • Wrong: Je m’appelle est Priya. / Mon nom est Priya.
    • Right: Je m’appelle Priya.
    • Why: je m’appelle is already a full verb: “I call myself”. Mon nom est… is grammatical but stiff; nobody says it.

    Nationality¶

    Nationality adjectives change for gender (blue = said by or about a man, pink = a woman). They take no capital letter after je suis.

    Masculine Feminine
    indien [ɛ̃-DYƐ̃] indienne [ɛ̃-DYEN]
    français [frã-SEH] française [frã-SEZ]
    anglais [ã-GLEH] anglaise [ã-GLEZ]
    américain [a-may-ree-KƐ̃] américaine [a-may-ree-KEN]
    canadien [ka-na-DYƐ̃] canadienne [ka-na-DYEN]
    pakistanais pakistanaise
    népalais népalaise
    britannique britannique
    • Je suis indien. — I am Indian. (a man) [zhuh swee ɛ̃-DYƐ̃]
    • Je suis indienne. — I am Indian. (a woman) — the nasal disappears, the n is heard [zhuh swee ɛ̃-DYEN]

    Careful

    • Wrong: (Priya speaking) Je suis indien.
    • Right: Je suis indienne.
    • Why: The adjective agrees with the speaker. A woman is indienne, française, canadienne.

    Careful

    • Wrong: Je suis Indien. Je parle Hindi.
    • Right: Je suis indien. Je parle hindi.
    • Why: French writes nationality adjectives and languages in small letters. Only the noun for a person takes a capital: les Indiens (Indian people).

    Where you live, what you do, what you speak¶

    • J’habite à Pune, en Inde. — I live in Pune, in India. — à + city, en + most countries [zha-BEET a … ã-NƐ̃D]
    • Je viens de Chennai. — I come from Chennai. [zhuh vyɛ̃ duh …]
    • Je suis étudiant. / Je suis étudiante. — I am a student. (man / woman) [ay-tew-DYÃ / ay-tew-DYÃT]
    • Je suis ingénieur. Je suis médecin. — I am an engineer. I am a doctor. [ɛ̃-zhay-NYÖR, mayd-SƐ̃]
    • Je parle hindi, anglais et un peu français. — I speak Hindi, English and a little French. [zhuh parl een-DEE, ã-GLEH ay ɛ̃ pö frã-SEH]
    • J’ai vingt-cinq ans. — I am 25 (literally: I have 25 years). [zhay vɛ̃t-sɛ̃-KÃ]

    Careful

    • Wrong: Je suis un ingénieur.
    • Right: Je suis ingénieur.
    • Why: After je suis, professions take no article. (But: C’est un ingénieur — with c’est, the article comes back.)

    Careful

    • Wrong: J’habite en Mumbai.
    • Right: J’habite à Mumbai.
    • Why: Cities take à; most countries take en (en Inde, en France). More in Prepositions & places.

    5.7 Ça va? — How are you?¶

    Ça va ? (literally “it goes?”) is the everyday “how are you?”, and Ça va. is also the answer: “I’m fine.” Only the melody changes.

    Answer Meaning
    Très bien, merci ! Very well, thanks!
    Ça va bien. I'm good.
    Pas mal. Not bad.
    Comme ci, comme ça. So-so.
    Bof… Meh… (casual)
    Pas terrible. Not great.

    Always bounce it back

    After your answer, return the question: Et toi ? (friend) or Et vous ? (polite). Leaving it out feels abrupt.

    Dialogue 1 — two students in Lyon (tu)¶

    • Léa : Salut ! Moi, c’est Léa. Et toi ? — Hi! I'm Léa. And you?
    • Rohan : Salut, Léa ! Je m’appelle Rohan. — Hi, Léa! My name is Rohan.
    • Léa : Tu es d’où ? — Where are you from? [tew eh DOO]
    • Rohan : Je suis indien, je viens de Pune. Et toi ? — I'm Indian, I come from Pune. And you?
    • Léa : Moi, je suis française. J’habite ici, à Lyon. — I'm French. I live here, in Lyon.
    • Rohan : Enchanté ! — Léa : Enchantée ! — Nice to meet you! — Nice to meet you!
    • Léa : Bon, j’ai cours. À plus ! — Right, I have class. See you!
    • Rohan : Salut, à demain ! — Bye, see you tomorrow!

    Dialogue 2 — at a hotel reception (vous)¶

    • Réceptionniste : Bonsoir, Monsieur. — Good evening, sir.
    • M. Mehta : Bonsoir, Madame. J’ai une réservation au nom de Mehta. — Good evening, madam. I have a reservation in the name of Mehta.
    • Réceptionniste : Vous pouvez épeler, s’il vous plaît ? — Can you spell it, please?
    • M. Mehta : Oui : M, E, H, T, A. — Yes: M-E-H-T-A.
    • Réceptionniste : Merci. Voici votre clé. Bonne soirée ! — Thank you. Here is your key. Have a nice evening!
    • M. Mehta : Merci beaucoup. Vous aussi ! — Thank you very much. You too!

    5.8 Survival phrases¶

    These sentences rescue any conversation. Learn them by heart this week — they are worth more than a hundred nouns.

    • Je ne comprends pas. — I don't understand. [zhuhn kõ-prã PA]
    • Vous pouvez répéter, s’il vous plaît ? — Can you repeat, please? [voo poo-vay ray-pay-TAY]
    • Plus lentement, s’il vous plaît. — More slowly, please. [plew lãt-MÃ]
    • Vous parlez anglais ? — Do you speak English? [voo par-lay ã-GLEH]
    • Je ne parle pas bien français. Je suis débutant. — I don't speak French well. I'm a beginner. (woman: débutante) [… day-bew-TÃ]
    • Comment dit-on « thank you » en français ? — How do you say “thank you” in French? [ko-mã dee-TÕ … ã frã-SEH]
    • Qu’est-ce que ça veut dire ? — What does that mean? [kes-kuh sa vö DEER]
    • Je ne sais pas. — I don't know. [zhuhn seh PA]
    • Où sont les toilettes, s’il vous plaît ? — Where are the toilets, please? [oo sõ lay twa-LET]
    • Je voudrais un verre d’eau. — I would like a glass of water. [zhuh voo-DREH ɛ̃ vehr DOH]
    • Oui. Non. D’accord. Peut-être. — Yes. No. OK. Maybe. [WEE, NÕ, da-KOR, pö-TETR]

    Polite magic

    Bonjour + excusez-moi + your question + s’il vous plaît + merci: this frame makes even broken French sound polite.

    À retenir

    • Always start with Bonjour — in a shop, a bus, an office, before any question. Add Monsieur or Madame for a stranger.
    • When someone offers you something, Merci alone (often with a small hand gesture) can mean “no, thank you”. To accept, say Oui, merci or Avec plaisir.
    • vous = one stranger (polite) or any group. tu = one friend, relative or child. Not sure? vous.
    • After your answer, return the question: Et toi ? (friend) or Et vous ? (polite). Leaving it out feels abrupt.
    • Bonjour + excusez-moi + your question + s’il vous plaît + merci: this frame makes even broken French sound polite.
    • Introduce yourself aloud in four sentences: name, nationality, city, languages. Then go on to Numbers, dates & time — you will need them for your age, phone number and prices.

    5.9 Practice — First words¶

    Exercise 1

    It's 9 pm. You walk into a restaurant. You say…

    • Bonjour
    • Bonsoir
    • Bonne nuit
    • Bonne journée
    Answer

    Bonsoir

    Evening greeting = bonsoir. Bonne nuit is only for going to bed.

    Exercise 2

    When do you say 'Bonne nuit'?

    • Arriving in the evening
    • Going to bed
    • Leaving a shop at noon
    • Meeting a friend
    Answer

    Going to bed

    Bonne nuit = sleep well. It is never a greeting.

    Exercise 3

    'Please', to a stranger:

    • s'il te plaît
    • s'il vous plaît
    • de rien
    • pardon
    Answer

    s'il vous plaît

    vous = polite; s'il te plaît is for friends.

    Exercise 4

    Someone says 'Merci !'. You answer…

    • Merci
    • De rien
    • Pardon
    • Salut
    Answer

    De rien

    de rien / je vous en prie = you're welcome.

    Exercise 5

    You bump into someone in the metro. You say…

    • Pardon !
    • Bonsoir !
    • De rien !
    • Enchanté !
    Answer

    Pardon !

    Pardon for small bumps; excusez-moi to get attention; désolé for real regret.

    Exercise 6

    Tu or vous with a shop assistant you don't know?

    • tu
    • vous
    Answer

    vous

    Strangers, shops, work, elders: vous.

    Exercise 7

    Hindi 'tum' is closest to French…

    • tu
    • vous
    • il
    • on
    Answer

    tu

    tum ≈ tu (informal), aap ≈ vous (polite).

    Exercise 8

    Je m'___ Priya. (My name is Priya.)

    Hint: from s'appeler

    Answer

    appelle

    Je m'appelle = I call myself. Two p, two l.

    Exercise 9

    (Priya speaking) Je suis ___ . (Indian)

    Hint: a woman

    Answer

    indienne

    Feminine: indien → indienne. The nasal disappears: [ɛ̃-DYEN].

    Exercise 10

    Pick the extra word.

    Je suis un ingénieur.

    Answer

    Wrong word: un.

    Corrected: Je suis ingénieur.

    No article for a profession after je suis.

    Exercise 11

    Pick the wrong word.

    J'habite en Mumbai.

    Answer

    Wrong word: en.

    Corrected: J'habite à Mumbai.

    à + city; en + most countries (en Inde).

    Exercise 12

    Pick the wrong word.

    Merci très beaucoup !

    Answer

    Wrong word: très.

    Corrected: Merci beaucoup !

    beaucoup already means 'very much'.

    Exercise 13

    Match the goodbyes.

    A B
    Au revoir See you tomorrow
    À bientôt Have a nice day
    À demain Goodbye
    Bonne journée See you soon
    Answer

    Au revoir — Goodbye; À bientôt — See you soon; À demain — See you tomorrow; Bonne journée — Have a nice day

    À + a time = see you then.

    Exercise 14

    Match the survival phrases.

    A B
    Je ne comprends pas More slowly
    Vous pouvez répéter ? I don't know
    Plus lentement I don't understand
    Je ne sais pas Can you repeat?
    Answer

    Je ne comprends pas — I don't understand; Vous pouvez répéter ? — Can you repeat?; Plus lentement — More slowly; Je ne sais pas — I don't know

    Learn these by heart — they rescue any conversation.

    Exercise 15

    'Ça va ?' — the best informal answer:

    • Ça va, et toi ?
    • Je m'appelle Léa.
    • Bonne nuit.
    • De rien.
    Answer

    Ça va, et toi ?

    Answer, then bounce the question back: et toi ? / et vous ?

    Exercise 16

    Who writes 'Enchantée' with -ée?

    • a man
    • a woman
    • a group
    • nobody
    Answer

    a woman

    Enchanté (man), enchantée (woman) — same sound.

    Exercise 17

    Put the words in order: 'What is your name?' (polite)

    Words: appelez-vous / vous / Comment

    Answer

    Comment vous appelez-vous

    Comment + vous + appelez-vous: the polite inverted question.

    Exercise 18

    Put the words in order: 'I don't understand.'

    Words: ne / pas / comprends / Je

    Answer

    Je ne comprends pas

    ne + verb + pas: the negation sandwich.

    Exercise 19

    Say it aloud.

    Vous pouvez répéter s'il vous plaît

    Answer

    voo poo-VAY ray-pay-TAY seel voo PLEH — the most useful sentence in this chapter.

    Exercise 20

    Say it aloud (change the name if you like).

    Je m'appelle Arjun et je suis indien

    Answer

    zhuh ma-PEL … ay zhuh swee ɛ̃-DYƐ̃ (a woman: indienne).
