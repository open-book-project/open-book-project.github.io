    Chapter 6: Numbers, dates & time¶

    Les nombres, la date, l’heure

    Numbers are everywhere: prices, phone numbers, your age, the train time. French numbers are regular up to 69 — then they start counting in twenties: 80 is quatre-vingts, “four twenties”. Good news for Hindi speakers: you already memorised a hundred different number words as a child. French needs far fewer.

    masculinefeminine

    The whole system in one line

    Learn 0–16 by heart, then build: tens + units up to 69, and 60 + 10…19 and 4 × 20 + 0…19 for 70–99.

    6.1 From 0 to 20¶

    No. French Sounds like
    0 zéro [zay-RO]
    1 un / une [Ɛ̃] / [EWN]
    2 deux [DÖ]
    3 trois [TRWA]
    4 quatre [KATR]
    5 cinq [SƐ̃K]
    6 six [SEESS]
    7 sept [SET]
    8 huit [WEET]
    9 neuf [NÖF]
    10 dix [DEESS]
    11 onze [ÕZ]
    12 douze [DOOZ]
    13 treize [TREZ]
    14 quatorze [ka-TORZ]
    15 quinze [KƐ̃Z]
    16 seize [SEZ]
    17 dix-sept [dee-SET]
    18 dix-huit [dee-ZWEET]
    19 dix-neuf [deez-NÖF]
    20 vingt [VƐ̃]

    From 17 on, French simply adds: dix-sept = 10-7. The number one agrees with the noun: un livre, une pomme, vingt et une pages.

    Numbers that change before a noun¶

    Several numbers drop or change their final consonant depending on the next word — the rules of Silent letters & liaison in action.

    Number Alone + consonant + vowel
    six [SEESS] six livres [see] six‿ans [see-ZÃ]
    dix [DEESS] dix livres [dee] dix‿ans [dee-ZÃ]
    huit [WEET] huit livres [wee] huit‿ans [wee-TÃ]
    cinq [SƐ̃K] cinq livres [sɛ̃] or [sɛ̃k] cinq‿ans [sɛ̃-KÃ]
    neuf [NÖF] neuf livres [nöf] neuf‿ans [nö-VÃ]
    • J’ai six ans. J’ai six livres. — I am six. I have six books. [see-ZÃ / see LEEVR]
    • Il a dix-huit ans. Elle a neuf ans. — He is 18. She is 9. [dee-zwee-TÃ / nö-VÃ]

    6.2 From 21 to 69¶

    Learn the tens, then join them to the units with a hyphen. For 1, use et (and) instead: vingt et un.

    Tens French Examples
    20 vingt [VƐ̃] 21 vingt et un · 22 vingt-deux [vɛ̃t-DÖ]
    30 trente [TRÃT] 31 trente et un · 35 trente-cinq
    40 quarante [ka-RÃT] 41 quarante et un · 48 quarante-huit
    50 cinquante [sɛ̃-KÃT] 51 cinquante et un · 56 cinquante-six
    60 soixante [swa-SÃT] 61 soixante et un · 69 soixante-neuf

    Two sound traps

    In soixante the x sounds [s]: [swa-SÃT], never “soks-ant”. And the t of vingt is heard from 21 to 29: vingt-trois [vɛ̃t-TRWA].

    Careful

    • Wrong: vingt-un, trente-un
    • Right: vingt et un, trente et un
    • Why: 21, 31, 41, 51, 61 (and 71) use et. All other combinations use a hyphen. (Since the 1990 spelling reform you may also write vingt-et-un.)

    6.3 From 70 to 99: counting in twenties¶

    Here France does something unusual. There is no single word for 70, 80 or 90. Instead, French builds them from 60 and from 20.

    No. French Built as
    70 soixante-dix 60 + 10
    71 soixante et onze 60 + 11
    72 soixante-douze 60 + 12
    79 soixante-dix-neuf 60 + 19
    80 quatre-vingts 4 × 20
    81 quatre-vingt-un 4 × 20 + 1 (no et!)
    85 quatre-vingt-cinq 4 × 20 + 5
    90 quatre-vingt-dix 4 × 20 + 10
    91 quatre-vingt-onze 4 × 20 + 11
    99 quatre-vingt-dix-neuf 4 × 20 + 19

    The s of quatre-vingts

    quatre-vingts (80) takes an s; as soon as a number follows, the s goes: quatre-vingt-un, quatre-vingt-dix. The t of vingt is silent in 81–99: [ka-truh-vɛ̃-Ɛ̃].

    Careful

    • Wrong: quatre-vingt et un
    • Right: quatre-vingt-un
    • Why: The et appears in 21, 31, 41, 51, 61 and 71 only — never in 81 or 91.

    Belgium and Switzerland¶

    French speakers in Belgium and Switzerland use simpler words: septante (70) and nonante (90). For 80, Belgium says quatre-vingts like France; the Swiss cantons of Vaud, Valais and Fribourg say huitante. Québec uses the France system. Everyone understands both — but in France, use soixante-dix.

    • septante-cinq, nonante-neuf — 75, 99 (Belgium, Switzerland) [sep-tãt-SƐ̃K, no-nãt-NÖF]

    6.4 Hundreds, thousands, millions¶

    Number French
    100 cent [SÃ] (not “un cent”)
    101 cent un
    200 deux cents
    250 deux cent cinquante
    1 000 mille [MEEL] (not “un mille”)
    3 000 trois mille
    1 000 000 un million
    1 000 000 000 un milliard

    cent, mille, million

    cents takes s only when multiplied and last (deux cents, but deux cent trois). mille never takes s. million, milliard are nouns: deux millions d’habitants.

    • L’Inde a plus d’un milliard d’habitants. — India has more than a billion inhabitants. [… plew dɛ̃ mee-LYAR da-bee-TÃ]
    • Paris a environ deux millions d’habitants. — Paris has about two million inhabitants.
    • Ce téléphone coûte trois cent quatre-vingt-dix-neuf euros. — This phone costs €399.

    Lakh and crore in French¶

    Indian Figure French
    1 lakh 100 000 cent mille
    10 lakh 1 000 000 un million
    1 crore 10 000 000 dix millions
    100 crore 1 000 000 000 un milliard

    Writing numbers the French way

    French groups thousands with a space and uses a comma for decimals: 1 500 000 (not 15,00,000 or 1,500,000) and 2,5 = deux virgule cinq.

    Years and phone numbers¶

    • en 2026 — en deux mille vingt-six — in 2026
    • L’Inde est indépendante depuis 1947. — India has been independent since 1947. — mille neuf cent quarante-sept
    • en 1999 — mille neuf cent quatre-vingt-dix-neuf — in 1999 (you may also hear dix-neuf cent…, “nineteen hundred…”)
    • 06 12 34 56 78 — Phone numbers are read in pairs: zéro six, douze, trente-quatre, cinquante-six, soixante-dix-huit.

    6.5 Ordinals: premier, deuxième, troisième…¶

    To say “first, second, third”, add -ième to the number (drop a final e). Only “first” is irregular.

    Ordinal Written Note
    premier / première 1er / 1re irregular, agrees
    deuxième (second·e) 2e second = formal, often “of two”
    troisième 3e
    quatrième 4e quatre − e
    cinquième 5e add u
    neuvième 9e f → v
    vingt et unième 21e no “premier” in compounds
    centième 100e
    • J’habite au premier étage. — I live on the first floor (one floor up). The ground floor is le rez-de-chaussée — as in India. [pruh-myeh-ray-TAZH]
    • C’est la première fois. — It's the first time.
    • Louis XIV, au XXIe siècle — Louis the Fourteenth (kings use plain numbers: Louis quatorze), in the 21st century

    Careful

    • Wrong: le 2ème étage
    • Right: le 2e étage
    • Why: The standard abbreviation is just e: 2e, 3e. “2ème” is common but not correct in careful writing.

    6.6 Days, months and dates¶

    The days of the week¶

    • lundi, mardi, mercredi, jeudi — Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday [lɛ̃-DEE, mar-DEE, mehr-kruh-DEE, zhö-DEE]
    • vendredi, samedi, dimanche — Friday, Saturday, Sunday [vã-druh-DEE, sam-DEE, dee-MÃSH]

    lundi vs le lundi

    Lundi, je travaille = this Monday. Le lundi, je travaille = on Mondays (every Monday). Days and months are masculine and take no capital.

    The months and seasons¶

    • janvier, février, mars, avril, mai, juin — January to June — the s of mars is heard [zhã-VYAY, fay-vree-AY, MARSS, a-VREEL, MEH, ZHWƐ̃]
    • juillet, août, septembre, octobre, novembre, décembre — July to December — août is [OO] or [OOT] [zhwee-YEH, OO(T), sep-TÃBR, ok-TOBR, no-VÃBR, day-SÃBR]
    • au printemps, en été, en automne, en hiver — in spring, in summer, in autumn, in winter [oh prɛ̃-TÃ, ã-nay-TAY, ã-no-TON, ã-nee-VEHR]
    • en janvier / au mois de janvier — in January

    Saying the date¶

    The date formula

    le + number + month: le 14 juillet. Use the ordinal only for the 1st: le 1er mai (le premier mai).

    • Le 14 juillet, c’est la fête nationale française. — The 14th of July is the French national day.
    • Le 15 août, c’est la fête de l’Indépendance en Inde. — The 15th of August is Independence Day in India.
    • le 1er janvier, le 2 octobre, le 26 janvier — 1st January, 2nd October, 26th January
    • — On est le combien ? — On est le 17 octobre. — — What's the date? — It's the 17th of October.
    • Aujourd’hui, nous sommes samedi 17 octobre 2026. — Today is Saturday, 17 October 2026. — day, number, month, year
    • Mon anniversaire, c’est le 3 avril. Je suis née en 1999. — My birthday is on 3 April. I was born in 1999. (a woman: née; a man: né)

    Careful

    • Wrong: le deuxième mai · Mai 2
    • Right: le deux mai
    • Why: Dates use normal numbers (except 1er), the day comes first, and the month has no capital. Written: 02/05 = 2 May — day first, as in India, not the American way.

    6.7 Telling the time¶

    • Quelle heure est-il ? — What time is it? [kel ö-reh-TEEL]
    • Il est une heure. Il est deux heures. — It is one o'clock. It is two o'clock. — always say heure(s) [ee-leh-tew-NÖR, ee-leh dö-ZÖR]
    • Il est midi. Il est minuit. — It is noon. It is midnight. [mee-DEE, mee-NWEE]
    Time Everyday (12 h)
    3:05 trois heures cinq
    3:15 trois heures et quart
    3:30 trois heures et demie
    3:40 quatre heures moins vingt
    3:45 quatre heures moins le quart
    12:30 midi et demi

    Demie has an e after heure (feminine), but not after midi and minuit (masculine): une heure et demie, midi et demi. To make the 12-hour clock clear, add du matin (am), de l’après-midi, du soir.

    The 24-hour clock¶

    Timetables, cinemas, appointments and the news use the 24-hour clock. Then you just read the numbers — no quart, no moins. It is written 14 h 30 (or 14h30).

    • Le train part à 18 h 45. — The train leaves at 6:45 pm. — dix-huit heures quarante-cinq
    • Le film commence à 21 h. — The film starts at 9 pm. — vingt et une heures (feminine: une heure)
    • — À quelle heure ? — À huit heures du soir. — — At what time? — At eight in the evening.

    Careful

    • Wrong: Il est cinq. / C’est 5 heures.
    • Right: Il est cinq heures.
    • Why: Always Il est… + heure(s). Leaving out heures sounds like an unfinished number.

    Careful

    • Wrong: quatorze heures et quart
    • Right: quatorze heures quinze / deux heures et quart
    • Why: Do not mix systems: quart and demie belong to the 12-hour clock.

    6.8 Prices and money¶

    France uses the euro (€), divided into 100 centimes. Prices are written with a comma and the € sign after: 3,50 €. You say the euros, then just the cents.

    • 3,50 € — trois euros cinquante — €3.50 [trwa-zö-RO sɛ̃-KÃT]
    • 0,80 € — quatre-vingts centimes — 80 cents
    • — C’est combien ? — Ça fait douze euros quatre-vingt-dix. — — How much is it? — That comes to €12.90.
    • Ça coûte combien, ce sac ? — How much does this bag cost? [sa koot kõ-BYƐ̃]
    • En Inde, ça coûte cinq cents roupies. — In India, it costs 500 rupees. — la roupie

    Liaison with euros¶

    Euro starts with a vowel, so numbers link to it. This is where the liaison rules pay off every day:

    Price Sounds like
    un‿euro [ɛ̃-nö-RO]
    deux‿euros, trois‿euros [dö-zö-RO], [trwa-zö-RO]
    six‿euros, dix‿euros [see-zö-RO], [dee-zö-RO]
    huit‿euros, vingt‿euros [wee-tö-RO], [vɛ̃-tö-RO]
    cent‿euros [sã-tö-RO]
    neuf euros [nö-fö-RO] — f stays f!

    neuf: v only with ans and heures

    neuf‿ans [nö-VÃ] and neuf‿heures [nö-VÖR] use [v]; everything else keeps [f]: neuf euros, neuf amis.

    In real life — at the bakery¶

    • — Bonjour ! Deux croissants et une baguette, s’il vous plaît. — — Hello! Two croissants and a baguette, please.
    • — Voilà. Ça fait quatre euros trente. — — Here you are. That's €4.30.
    • — Tenez. Merci, bonne journée ! — — Here you go. Thank you, have a nice day!

    À retenir

    • Learn 0–16 by heart, then build: tens + units up to 69, and 60 + 10…19 and 4 × 20 + 0…19 for 70–99.
    • quatre-vingts (80) takes an s; as soon as a number follows, the s goes: quatre-vingt-un, quatre-vingt-dix. The t of vingt is silent in 81–99: [ka-truh-vɛ̃-Ɛ̃].
    • cents takes s only when multiplied and last (deux cents, but deux cent trois). mille never takes s. million, milliard are nouns: deux millions d’habitants.
    • Lundi, je travaille = this Monday. Le lundi, je travaille = on Mondays (every Monday). Days and months are masculine and take no capital.
    • le + number + month: le 14 juillet. Use the ordinal only for the 1st: le 1er mai (le premier mai).
    • neuf‿ans [nö-VÃ] and neuf‿heures [nö-VÖR] use [v]; everything else keeps [f]: neuf euros, neuf amis.

    6.9 Practice — Numbers, dates & time¶

    Exercise 1

    70 in French (France):

    1. septante
    2. soixante-dix
    3. sept-dix
    4. soixante et dix
    Answer

    soixante-dix

    70 = 60 + 10 = soixante-dix. Septante is Belgian/Swiss.

    Exercise 2

    80 in French (France):

    1. huitante
    2. octante
    3. quatre-vingts
    4. huit-dix
    Answer

    quatre-vingts

    80 = 4 × 20 = quatre-vingts, with an s.

    Exercise 3

    90 in French (France):

    1. nonante
    2. quatre-vingt-dix
    3. neuf-dix
    4. quatre-vingts-dix
    Answer

    quatre-vingt-dix

    90 = 4 × 20 + 10. The s of vingts goes when a number follows.

    Exercise 4

    21 = vingt ___ un

    Hint: a little word, not a hyphen

    Answer

    et

    21, 31, 41, 51, 61, 71 use et: vingt et un.

    Exercise 5

    Where do people say 'septante' and 'nonante'?

    1. Paris
    2. Québec
    3. Belgium and Switzerland
    4. Nowhere
    Answer

    Belgium and Switzerland

    Belgian and Swiss French: septante (70), nonante (90).

    Exercise 6

    1 lakh (100 000) in French is…

    1. un million
    2. cent mille
    3. dix mille
    4. un milliard
    Answer

    cent mille

    100 000 = cent mille. 1 crore = dix millions.

    Exercise 7

    Which is correct for 203?

    1. deux cents trois
    2. deux cent trois
    3. deux centre trois
    4. deux cents et trois
    Answer

    deux cent trois

    cents loses its s when another number follows.

    Exercise 8

    Il est trois heures et ___ . (3:30)

    Hint: half

    Answer

    demie

    et demie after heure (feminine); midi et demi without e.

    Exercise 9

    2:45 in everyday French:

    1. deux heures moins le quart
    2. trois heures moins le quart
    3. deux heures et quart
    4. trois heures et demie
    Answer

    trois heures moins le quart

    Before the hour: next hour + moins: three minus a quarter.

    Exercise 10

    'Le 14 juillet' is…

    1. Christmas
    2. the French national day
    3. India's Independence Day
    4. New Year
    Answer

    the French national day

    Le 14 juillet = la fête nationale. India's is le 15 août.

    Exercise 11

    Tap the wrong word.

    Aujourd'hui, c'est le deuxième mai.

    Answer

    Wrong word: deuxième.

    Corrected: Aujourd'hui, c'est le deux mai.

    Dates use normal numbers, except le premier (1er).

    Exercise 12

    Tap the word with a spelling mistake.

    Mon anniversaire est en Janvier.

    Answer

    Wrong word: Janvier.

    Corrected: Mon anniversaire est en janvier.

    Months and days take no capital letter in French.

    Exercise 13

    Match the days.

    AB
    lundiSaturday
    mercrediSunday
    samediMonday
    dimancheWednesday
    Answer

    lundi — Monday; mercredi — Wednesday; samedi — Saturday; dimanche — Sunday

    Days are masculine and lowercase.

    Exercise 14

    Match the ordinals.

    AB
    premier5th
    deuxième9th
    cinquième1st
    neuvième2nd
    Answer

    premier — 1st; deuxième — 2nd; cinquième — 5th; neuvième — 9th

    cinq → cinquième (add u), neuf → neuvième (f → v).

    Exercise 15

    How do you say €3.50?

    1. trois virgule cinquante euros
    2. trois euros cinquante
    3. trois cinquante centimes
    4. trois et demi euros
    Answer

    trois euros cinquante

    Euros first, then the cents alone: trois euros cinquante.

    Exercise 16

    'six ans' is pronounced…

    1. [seess ã]
    2. [see-ZÃ]
    3. [see-TÃ]
    4. [sex-ã]
    Answer

    [see-ZÃ]

    Before a vowel, six links with a [z].

    Exercise 17

    Put the words in order: 'It is eight in the evening.'

    Words: Il / du / est / soir / huit / heures

    Answer

    Il est huit heures du soir

    Il est + number + heures + du soir.

    Exercise 18

    Say the price aloud.

    Ça coûte quatre-vingt-dix-neuf euros

    Answer

    Say: Ça coûte quatre-vingt-dix-neuf euros

    quatre-vingt-dix-neuf: 4 × 20 + 19. The t of vingt is silent here; neuf euros keeps [f].

    Exercise 19

    Say your birthday (or this one).

    Mon anniversaire c'est le trois avril

    Answer

    Say: Mon anniversaire c'est le trois avril

    le + number + month: le trois avril. Only the 1st uses premier.
