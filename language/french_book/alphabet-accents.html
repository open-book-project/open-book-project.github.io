    Chapter 2: Alphabet & accents¶

    L’alphabet et les accents

    French uses the same 26 letters as English, but it names most of them differently — and it adds small marks called accents. An accent is not decoration: it can change the sound (e vs é) or the meaning (ou “or” vs où “where”). In this chapter you learn to say the letters, spell your name aloud, and read every accent.

    masculinefeminine

    Accents are part of the spelling

    A word written without its accent is misspelled. Learn été as “é-t-é”, never as “ete”.

    2.1 The 26 letters and their names¶

    You need the letter names to spell words aloud — your name, your email, a street. Listen to each name and repeat it. The vowels are pure and short.

    Letter French name Sounds like
    A a a [ah]
    B b bé [bay]
    C c cé [say]
    D d dé [day]
    E e e [uh]
    F f effe [ehf]
    G g gé [zhay]
    H h ache [ash]
    I i i [ee]
    J j ji [zhee]
    K k ka [kah]
    L l elle [ehl]
    M m emme [ehm]
    N n enne [ehn]
    O o o [oh]
    P p pé [pay]
    Q q qu [kew]
    R r erre [ehr]
    S s esse [ehss]
    T t té [tay]
    U u u [ew]
    V v vé [vay]
    W w double vé [doo-bluh-VAY]
    X x ixe [eeks]
    Y y i grec [ee-GREK]
    Z z zède [zehd]
    • A, B, C, D, E, F, G — The first seven letters in one breath.
    • W, X, Y, Z — The last four: double vé, ixe, i grec, zède.

    2.2 The letters that trick English speakers¶

    Most letter names are easy. A few are dangerous because they sound like a different English letter. These are the ones people get wrong when spelling an email on the phone.

    The swapped pairs

    G = gé [zhay], J = ji [zhee] — the reverse of English. And E = [uh], I = [ee]: French I sounds like English E.

    Careful

    • Wrong: Spelling Gita as “jee, eye, tee, ay”.
    • Right: G-I-T-A = gé, i, té, a [zhay, ee, tay, ah].
    • Why: English letter names confuse French listeners: your “eye” means nothing to them, and your “jee” sounds to them like the letter J (ji).

    Careful

    • Wrong: Saying “aitch” or “haitch” for H.
    • Right: H = ache [ash].
    • Why: And the letter itself is never pronounced in French words: hôtel = [o-TEL].

    Careful

    • Wrong: Saying “why” for Y.
    • Right: Y = i grec [ee-GREK], “the Greek i”.
    • Why: Inside words, y usually sounds like [ee]: un stylo [stee-LO].
    • G, J — gé, ji — Say the swapped pair five times.
    • E, I — e, i — E is [uh], I is [ee].

    2.3 Spelling words aloud¶

    To spell is épeler [ay-puh-LAY]. These are the phrases you will hear at a hotel desk, on the phone, or when someone writes down your name.

    • Comment ça s’écrit ? — How do you spell that? (literally: how is that written?) [ko-mã sa say-KREE]
    • Vous pouvez épeler, s’il vous plaît ? — Can you spell it, please? [voo poo-vay ay-puh-LAY seel voo PLEH]
    • Mon prénom, c’est Priya. Ça s’écrit P, R, I, Y, A. — My first name is Priya. It is spelled P-R-I-Y-A.
    • Mon nom de famille, c’est Sharma : S, H, A, R, M, A. — My surname is Sharma: S-H-A-R-M-A.
    • Ça s’écrit avec deux L. — It is spelled with two Ls (double letters: deux L, “two L”).

    Words for spelling¶

    You say Meaning
    e accent aigu é
    e accent grave è
    e accent circonflexe ê
    c cédille ç
    i tréma ï
    majuscule / minuscule capital / small letter
    arobase @

    tiret / tiret bas - (hyphen) / _ (underscore)
    • priya.sharma@gmail.com — said: “priya point sharma arobase gmail point com”
    • Élodie : E accent aigu, L, O, D, I, E. — Spelling a French name with an accent.

    2.4 The accents at a glance¶

    French has five marks. Only e can take all three “roof” accents; the cedilla goes only under c.

    Mark Letters Main job
    accent aigu ´ é Sound: closed [ay]
    accent grave ` è, à, ù Sound on è: open [eh]. On à, ù: meaning only
    accent circonflexe ^ â, ê, î, ô, û History (lost s); ê = [eh], ô = closed [oh]
    tréma ¨ ë, ï, ü (ÿ in a few names) Say this vowel separately
    cédille ¸ ç c sounds [s] before a, o, u

    2.5 é, è, ê — three e’s, two sounds¶

    Plain e is usually a soft [uh] or silent. The accents fix its sound. Smile slightly for é; open your mouth a little more for è and ê.

    • l’été, le café, le bébé — summer, coffee, baby — é = [ay], short and pure, no “y” glide [lay-TAY, ka-FAY, bay-BAY]
    • la mère, le père, très — mother, father, very — è = [eh], like “e” in “bed” [MEHR, PEHR, TREH]
    • la fête, la tête, être — party, head, to be — ê = [eh] too [FET, TET, ETR]
    • un élève — a pupil — both sounds in one word: é then è [ɛ̃-nay-LEV]

    é vs è/ê

    é = [ay] (closed, smiling) · è = ê = [eh] (open, as in “bed”). Plain e with no accent is usually [uh] or silent.

    The circumflex: a missing s¶

    Centuries ago many French words had an s after the vowel. The s disappeared, and the circumflex took its place. English often kept the s — so the circumflex is a free vocabulary key.

    French English cousin
    la forêt forest
    l’hôpital hospital
    la fête feast (festival)
    l’île isle (island)
    le château castle
    la bête beast
    le coût cost

    On î and û the circumflex changes nothing in sound. On ô it gives a closed [oh]: l’hôtel [lo-TEL]. On â some speakers make a deeper “ah” (pâte, pasta), but most people today say it like a normal a.

    2.6 Accents that change the meaning¶

    Some accents change nothing in the sound — they only tell two words apart in writing. You must write them, or your sentence means something else.

    Without With Example
    a = has à = to, at Il a un billet pour aller à Paris.
    ou = or où = where Où est le thé ou le café ?
    la = the (f) là = there La clé est là.
    sur = on sûr = sure Tu es sûr ? C’est sur la table.
    du = some (m) dû = had to Il a dû acheter du pain.
    des = some (pl) dès = from (time) Dès demain, je vois des amis.
    mur = wall mûr = ripe Un fruit mûr près du mur.
    • Il a un billet pour aller à Paris. — He has a ticket to go to Paris. — a and à sound the same
    • Où est le thé ou le café ? — Where is the tea or the coffee? — ou and où sound the same [oo]
    • La clé est là. — The key is there.
    • Tu es sûr ? C’est sur la table. — Are you sure? It's on the table.

    When the accent changes the sound and the meaning¶

    • je parle / j’ai parlé — I speak / I spoke — the é makes it past [PARL / zhay par-LAY]
    • jeune / le jeûne — young / fasting (like a vrat) — open vs closed eu [ZHÖN / luh ZHÖN]

    Typical accent mistakes

    • Wrong: Je vais a Delhi.
    • Right: Je vais à Delhi.

    • Why: “to” = à with the grave accent. Plain a is the verb “has”.

    • Wrong: Ou habites-tu ?

    • Right: Où habites-tu ?

    • Why: The question word “where” always has the accent: où.

    • Wrong: Il fait chaud en ete.

    • Right: Il fait chaud en été.

    • Why: Without accents the word is unreadable: ete would be pronounced like [et].

    • Wrong: ECOLE (on a sign, accents dropped)

    • Right: ÉCOLE
    • Why: Capital letters keep their accents in careful French: À bientôt, État, Ça va.

    2.7 ç, the tréma, and œ¶

    Hard and soft c and g¶

    The letters c and g change sound depending on the next vowel — exactly like in English cat / city, gate / gem. The cedilla and a silent e or u let French choose the sound it wants.

    Spelling Sound Examples
    c + a, o, u [k] le café, la côte, la culture
    c + e, i, y [s] le centre, la ville de Nice, le cycle
    ç + a, o, u [s] ça, le garçon, j’ai reçu
    g + a, o, u [g] la gare, le goût, aigu
    g + e, i, y [zh] le genou, la girafe, la gym
    ge + a, o [zh] nous mangeons, Georges
    gu + e, i [g] la guerre, la guitare
    • Ça va ? Le garçon est français. — How are you? The boy is French. [sa VA … luh gar-SÕ eh frã-SEH]
    • Nous commençons, nous mangeons. — We begin, we eat. — ç and ge keep the soft sound before o [noo ko-mã-SÕ, noo mã-ZHÕ]
    • la guitare, la guerre, la girafe — guitar, war, giraffe — gu = hard g, gi = soft zh [gee-TAR, GEHR, zhee-RAF]

    Careful

    • Wrong: le garcon [gar-KÕ]
    • Right: le garçon [gar-SÕ]
    • Why: Before o, a plain c is [k]. The cedilla is what makes it [s].

    The tréma: say the vowels apart¶

    Two vowels together usually make one sound (ai = [eh], oi = [wa]). The tréma (two dots) says: stop — pronounce this vowel on its own.

    • mais / le maïs — but / corn (maize) [MEH / ma-EESS]
    • Noël, naïf, égoïste — Christmas, naive, selfish [no-EL, na-EEF, ay-go-EEST]

    The tréma on ü is rare. You will see it in aigüe (the 1990 spelling of aiguë, “sharp”, feminine).

    The joined letters œ and æ¶

    œ (called e dans l’o, “e in the o”) is common and sounds like the [ö] of eu. æ is very rare.

    • le cœur, la sœur, un œuf — heart, sister, an egg [KÖR, SÖR, ɛ̃-NÖF]
    • une œuvre d’art, le bœuf — a work of art, beef [ew-NÖVR DAR, luh BÖF]
    • Ils sont ex æquo. — They are tied (equal score). — æ, from Latin [eel sõ eg-zay-KO]

    Typing accents

    On a phone, press and hold a letter (e → é è ê ë). On Windows, add the US-International keyboard: type ' then e for é, ` + e for è, ^ + e for ê, ' + c for ç.

    2.8 Letter teams: a preview¶

    French often uses two or three letters for one sound. Learn to see them as a single block. Each one is practised in detail in The sounds of French.

    Letters Sound Example
    ou [oo] vous (you)
    oi [wa] moi (me)
    au, eau [oh] beau, l’eau (beautiful, water)
    ai, ei [eh] le lait, la neige (milk, snow)
    eu, œu [ö] deux, la sœur (two, sister)
    an, en [ã] l’enfant (child)
    on [õ] bonbon (sweet)
    in, ain, un [ɛ̃] le vin, le pain, un
    ch [sh] le chat (cat)
    gn [ny] la montagne (mountain)
    qu [k] qui, quatre (who, four)
    ill [y] la fille, la famille
    ph, th [f], [t] la photo, le thé

    Two common exceptions

    ill = [eel] in ville (town), mille (thousand) and tranquille (calm). And th is never the English “th”: le thé = [luh TAY].

    • La famille habite dans une ville tranquille. — The family lives in a quiet town. — famille [y], ville and tranquille [eel] [fa-MEEY … VEEL trã-KEEL]
    • Moi, je bois du thé au lait. — Me, I drink tea with milk. [MWA, zhuh bwa dew tay oh LEH]

    À retenir

    • A word written without its accent is misspelled. Learn été as “é-t-é”, never as “ete”.
    • G = gé [zhay], J = ji [zhee] — the reverse of English. And E = [uh], I = [ee]: French I sounds like English E.
    • é = [ay] (closed, smiling) · è = ê = [eh] (open, as in “bed”). Plain e with no accent is usually [uh] or silent.
    • On a phone, press and hold a letter (e → é è ê ë). On Windows, add the US-International keyboard: type ' then e for é, ` + e for è, ^ + e for ê, ' + c for ç.
    • Spell your full name and your email aloud in French, three times. Then go on to The sounds of French, where every letter team from section 8 gets its own practice.

    2.9 Practice — Alphabet & accents¶

    Exercise 1

    How do you say the letter G in French?

    1. [jee]
    2. [zhay] gé
    3. [gay]
    4. [zhee] ji
    Answer

    [zhay] gé

    G = gé [zhay]. It is J that is called ji [zhee].

    Exercise 2

    The French letter J is called…

    1. jay
    2. ji [zhee]
    3. gé [zhay]
    4. ja
    Answer

    ji [zhee]

    G and J are swapped compared with English: G = gé, J = ji.

    Exercise 3

    Which letter is called 'i grec'?

    1. I
    2. J
    3. Y
    4. X
    Answer

    Y

    Y = i grec, 'the Greek i'.

    Exercise 4

    The French letter I sounds like…

    1. English 'eye'
    2. English 'ee'
    3. English 'ay'
    4. English 'uh'
    Answer

    English 'ee'

    French I = [ee]; French E = [uh].

    Exercise 5

    Comment ça s'___ ? (How is it spelled?)

    Hint: from écrire, to write

    Answer

    écrit / ecrit

    Comment ça s'écrit ? = How do you spell that?

    Exercise 6

    Which letter always sounds [ay], as in café?

    1. e
    2. é
    3. è
    4. ê
    Answer

    é

    é (accent aigu) is the closed [ay]. è and ê are open [eh].

    Exercise 7

    Which word means 'where'?

    1. ou
    2. où
    3. au
    4. eau
    Answer

    où

    où = where; ou = or. Same sound, different meaning.

    Exercise 8

    Tap the word with the missing accent.

    Il va a Paris demain.

    Answer

    Wrong word: a.

    Corrected: Il va à Paris demain.

    'to' = à. Plain a is the verb 'has'.

    Exercise 9

    Tap the wrong word.

    Tu veux du thé où du café ?

    Answer

    Wrong word: où.

    Corrected: Tu veux du thé ou du café ?

    'or' = ou, without an accent. où means 'where'.

    Exercise 10

    The circumflex in 'forêt' often shows…

    1. a lost s
    2. a silent t
    3. a plural
    4. a question
    Answer

    a lost s

    forêt = forest, hôpital = hospital, fête = feast: the s became ^.

    Exercise 11

    Match each mark to its name.

    AB
    éaccent circonflexe
    ècédille
    êtréma
    çaccent aigu
    ïaccent grave
    Answer

    é — accent aigu; è — accent grave; ê — accent circonflexe; ç — cédille; ï — tréma

    Five marks: aigu, grave, circonflexe, tréma, cédille.

    Exercise 12

    le gar___on (the boy) — c or ç?

    Hint: before o, plain c sounds [k]

    Answer

    ç / c cédille

    garçon needs ç to sound [s] before o.

    Exercise 13

    In 'Noël', the two dots on ë mean…

    1. the e is silent
    2. say o and e separately
    3. the word is plural
    4. say [ay]
    Answer

    say o and e separately

    The tréma separates vowels: No-ël [no-EL].

    Exercise 14

    'le maïs' means…

    1. but
    2. corn
    3. the month
    4. my
    Answer

    corn

    maïs [ma-EESS] = corn; mais [meh] = but.

    Exercise 15

    In an email address, '@' is said…

    1. at
    2. arobase
    3. point
    4. tiret
    Answer

    arobase

    @ = arobase, . = point, - = tiret, _ = tiret bas.

    Exercise 16

    Put the words in order: 'How do you spell that?'

    Words: ça / Comment / s'écrit

    Answer

    Comment ça s'écrit

    Comment (how) + ça (that) + s'écrit (is written).

    Exercise 17

    Say it aloud.

    Ça s'écrit avec un accent aigu

    Answer

    Say: Ça s'écrit avec un accent aigu

    sa say-KREE a-vek ɛ̃-nak-SÃ-tay-GEW — notice the links between the words.

    Exercise 18

    Say it aloud.

    Vous pouvez épeler s'il vous plaît

    Answer

    Say: Vous pouvez épeler s'il vous plaît

    ay-puh-LAY: three short, even syllables.
