    Glossary¶

    Glossar

    The first part of this glossary explains the grammar terms used in the book, with a German example of each and the lesson where it is taught. The second part lists words and phrases that often confuse English speakers.

    Grammar terms¶

    Accusative
    The case of the direct object, and the case after es gibt, after durch, für, gegen, ohne, um, and after the two-way prepositions for movement. Only the masculine differs from the nominative: Ich habe einen Bruder. Lessons 8 and 15.
    Adjective
    A word that describes a noun: groß, nett, rot. After sein it has no ending: Der Stuhl ist bequem. Lessons 8 and 9.
    Adverb
    A word that describes a verb, an adjective or a whole sentence: gern, sehr, oft, heute. Adverbs do not change their form. Lessons 6, 9 and 13.
    Article
    The words for the (definite: der, die, das; plural die) and a (indefinite: ein, eine, ein). The negative article is kein. Lessons 4 and 5.
    Case
    The form of an article, pronoun or noun that shows its role in the sentence. German has four cases: nominative, accusative, dative and genitive. Lesson 5.
    Comparative
    The form of an adjective used to compare: älter als, größer als, besser. Lesson 9.
    Compound noun
    A noun made of two or more words; the last part gives the gender and plural: die Haustür (das Haus + die Tür). Lesson 4.
    Conjugation
    The set of forms of a verb for the different persons and tenses: ich komme, du kommst, er kommt … Lesson 2.
    Conjunction
    A word that joins words or clauses: und, aber, oder, denn, sondern (no change of word order); weil, dass, wenn (verb to the end). Lessons 7 and 16.
    Contraction
    A preposition joined to an article: zum (zu dem), zur (zu der), im (in dem), am (an dem), ins (in das). Lessons 11 and 15.
    Dative
    The case of the indirect object, and the case after aus, bei, mit, nach, seit, von, zu, gegenüber, after the two-way prepositions for position, and after verbs such as helfen and gefallen: mit dem Bus, Wien gefällt mir. Lessons 11 and 15.
    Diphthong
    A vowel sound that glides from one quality to another: ei [eye], au [ow], eu [oy]. Lesson 1.
    Direct object
    The person or thing directly affected by the verb, in the accusative: Ich kaufe eine Lampe. Lesson 8.
    Gender
    The grammatical class of a noun: masculine (der), feminine (die) or neuter (das). Lesson 4.
    Genitive
    The fourth case, used mainly in writing for possession: das Auto meines Bruders. In speech, von + dative is usual. Lesson 9.
    Imperative
    The form of the verb for instructions and requests: Komm!, Kommt!, Kommen Sie! Lesson 10.
    Indirect object
    The person to whom something is given, shown or told, in the dative: Ich gebe dem Kellner das Geld. Lesson 15.
    Infinitive
    The basic form of a verb, as listed in a dictionary, ending in -en or -n: kommen, wandern. Lesson 2.
    Inseparable prefix
    An unstressed prefix that never separates from its verb: be-, emp-, ent-, er-, ge-, miss-, ver-, zer-. Its past participle has no ge-: besucht. Lessons 13 and 14.
    Modal verb
    One of the verbs können, müssen, wollen, dürfen, sollen, mögen (and möchten), used with an infinitive at the end: Ich muss arbeiten. Lesson 12.
    Nominative
    The case of the subject, and the case after sein and heißen; the dictionary form of a noun. Lesson 5.
    Noun
    A word for a person, thing, place or idea. In German every noun has a gender and is written with a capital letter: der Tisch, die Freiheit. Lessons 1 and 4.
    Past participle
    The form of the verb used in the perfect: gemacht, gesehen, eingekauft, besucht. Lesson 14.
    Perfect
    The usual spoken past tense, formed with haben or sein and a past participle at the end: Ich habe gegessen. Er ist gekommen. Lesson 14.
    Personal pronoun
    A word that stands for a person or thing: ich, du, er, sie, es, wir, ihr, sie, Sie, with accusative and dative forms (mich, mir …). Lessons 2, 8 and 11.
    Possessive
    A word that shows whose something is: mein, dein, sein, ihr, unser, euer, Ihr. It takes the endings of ein. Lessons 5 and 9.
    Preposition
    A short word that links a noun to the rest of the sentence and decides its case: für (accusative), mit (dative), in (two-way). Lessons 8, 11 and 15.
    Present
    The tense for the present, for habits, for the future with a time word, and with seit for actions still going on: Ich lerne seit einem Jahr Deutsch. Lesson 6.
    Reflexive verb
    A verb whose object refers back to the subject: sich anziehen → Ich ziehe mich an. Lesson 13.
    Sentence bracket
    The pattern in which one verb stands in position 2 and a second verb form (infinitive, participle, separable prefix) at the end: Ich kann morgen nicht kommen. Lessons 12 to 14.
    Separable verb
    A verb with a stressed prefix that goes to the end of the sentence in the present: aufstehen → Ich stehe um sieben auf. Lesson 13.
    Simple past
    The second past tense, used mainly in writing; in speech mostly for war, hatte, es gab and the modal verbs: ich konnte. Lesson 14.
    Stem
    The part of a verb that remains when the infinitive ending is taken off: komm- from kommen. Lesson 2.
    Stem-changing verb
    A verb whose stem vowel changes in the du and er forms of the present: fahren, du fährst; sprechen, er spricht; lesen, er liest. Lesson 6.
    Subject
    The person or thing that does the action or that the sentence is about, in the nominative: Mein Bruder ist Koch. Lesson 5.
    Subordinate clause
    A clause that cannot stand alone, introduced by a word such as weil, dass, wenn, with the verb at the end: … weil ich müde bin. Lesson 16.
    Superlative
    The form that says something is the most: am größten, am besten. Lesson 9.
    Two-way preposition
    One of the prepositions in, an, auf, neben, vor, hinter, über, unter, zwischen, which take the accusative for movement (wohin?) and the dative for position (wo?). Lessons 11 and 15.
    Umlaut
    The two dots over a, o or u, which change the vowel: ä, ö, ü. Written ae, oe, ue when the letters are not available. Lesson 1.
    Verb second
    The rule that the conjugated verb is the second element of a statement: Heute arbeite ich. Lesson 7.

    Words that cause trouble¶

    also
    "So, therefore", not "also" (auch): Also, los geht's! Lesson 16.
    bekommen
    "To get, receive", not "to become" (werden): Ich bekomme einen Brief. Lessons 8 and 16.
    bitte
    "Please", "here you are" and "you're welcome"; Wie bitte? means "Sorry? Pardon?" Lesson 1.
    doch
    "Yes" in answer to a negative question or statement: Kommst du nicht? – Doch! Also a softening particle: Komm doch mit! Lessons 7 and 10.
    du, ihr, Sie
    The three words for "you": one friend, several friends, and anyone you address politely. Lesson 2.
    gern
    With a verb, "to like doing": Ich koche gern. Compare lieber (prefer) and am liebsten (like best). Lesson 6.
    halb
    In the time, half to the next hour: halb drei is 2.30. Lesson 3.
    Handy
    A mobile phone (das Handy), not the adjective "handy" (praktisch). Lesson 4.
    kein and nicht
    kein negates nouns with ein or no article (Ich habe kein Auto); nicht negates everything else (Ich bin nicht müde). Lessons 5 and 7.
    kennen and wissen
    kennen: to know a person or place (Ich kenne Wien); wissen: to know a fact (Ich weiß es nicht). Lesson 6.
    lernen and studieren
    lernen: to learn, to study for a lesson or a language; studieren: to study at university. Lesson 6.
    Mir ist kalt
    "I'm cold": never Ich bin kalt, which describes a cold person. Lesson 5.
    morgen and der Morgen
    morgen: tomorrow; der Morgen: the morning; morgen früh: tomorrow morning. Lesson 3.
    nach Hause and zu Hause
    nach Hause: (going) home; zu Hause: at home. Lesson 11.
    sie and Sie
    sie is "she" (verb in -t) or "they" (verb in -en); Sie with a capital is the polite "you". Lessons 2 and 5.
    sympathisch
    "Likeable, pleasant", not "sympathetic" (mitfühlend). Lesson 9.
    Uhr and Stunde
    Uhr: the time on the clock, o'clock (drei Uhr); Stunde: an hour as a length of time (drei Stunden). Lesson 3.
    wer and wo
    wer: who; wo: where. Lesson 2.
    will
    "Want(s)", from wollen, not the English future "will" (werde). Lessons 12 and 16.
