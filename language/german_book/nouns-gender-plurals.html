    Lesson 4: Nouns, Gender and Plurals¶

    Nomen, Genus und Plural

    Every German noun belongs to one of three genders: masculine, feminine or neuter. A table is masculine, a lamp is feminine, a bed is neuter, and a girl, surprisingly, is neuter too. The gender is a grammatical class, not a statement about the thing itself, and it decides the word for "the": der, die or das. German plurals are just as varied: there are several ways of making a plural, and you will learn which nouns take which. This sounds daunting, but there are good rules of thumb, and the habit that solves both problems is simple: learn every noun with its article and its plural, as this lesson shows you.

    Learning outcomes

    By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

    • use the definite articles der, die, das and the plural die;
    • predict the gender of many nouns from their meaning and their ending, and remember the common exceptions;
    • make the plural of nouns with the main patterns: -e, -er, -(e)n, -s, no ending, and umlaut;
    • replace nouns with the pronouns er, sie, es and sie;
    • understand and build compound nouns such as die Haustür;
    • name the rooms and furniture of a flat and the things you use in class.

    4.1 Dialogue: the new flat¶

    Emma has just moved into a shared flat with Jonas. He shows her round.

    • Jonas: So, Emma, das ist die Wohnung. Hier ist die Küche. — So, Emma, this is the flat. Here is the kitchen.
    • Emma: Oh, die Küche ist groß! Und der Kühlschrank ist neu. — Oh, the kitchen is big! And the fridge is new.
    • Jonas: Ja, er ist ganz neu. Und das ist das Bad. — Yes, it's brand new. And this is the bathroom.
    • Emma: Und wo ist das Wohnzimmer? — And where is the living room?
    • Jonas: Hier links. Da sind das Sofa, der Fernseher und die Bücher. — Here on the left. There are the sofa, the television and the books.
    • Emma: Und mein Zimmer? — And my room?
    • Jonas: Hier. Das Zimmer ist klein, aber das Fenster ist groß. — Here. The room is small, but the window is big.
    • Emma: Das Bett ist schön. Und die Lampe? — The bed is nice. And the lamp?
    • Jonas: Die Lampe ist alt, aber sie funktioniert. — The lamp is old, but it works.
    • Emma: Wo sind die Schlüssel? — Where are the keys?
    • Jonas: Hier: der Schlüssel für die Haustür und der Schlüssel für die Wohnungstür. — Here: the key for the front door of the building and the key for the door of the flat.
    • Emma: Danke, Jonas! Die Wohnung ist toll. — Thanks, Jonas! The flat is great.

    Notice:

    1. There are three words for "the": der Kühlschrank, die Küche, das Bad. In the plural, all three become die: die Bücher, die Schlüssel.
    2. A thing is "he", "she" or "it" according to its gender: the fridge is er ("he"), the lamp is sie ("she").
    3. Haustür is one word made of two: Haus (house) + Tür (door).
    4. "Is" is ist and "are" is sind: Das Zimmer ist klein. Wo sind die Schlüssel? Lesson 5 gives the whole verb sein.

    4.3 Grammar: der, die, das¶

    Three genders¶

    German has three definite articles in the singular, one for each gender, and one in the plural:

    Masculine Feminine Neuter Plural
    "the" der Tisch die Lampe das Bett die Tische
    pronoun er sie es sie

    Table 4.4. The definite article and the matching pronoun. Switch on Practice and fill them in.

    For people, the grammatical gender usually follows the person: der Mann, der Vater, die Frau, die Mutter. For things and ideas there is no logic in the meaning: der Löffel (spoon), die Gabel (fork) and das Messer (knife) are one of each. But the form of a noun, and especially its ending, often shows the gender. The rules below cover a large part of everyday vocabulary.

    Masculine: der¶

    • Male people and animals: der Mann, der Vater, der Sohn, der Onkel.
    • Days, months, seasons: der Montag, der Mai, der Sommer (Lesson 3).
    • Weather and points of the compass: der Regen, der Schnee, der Wind, der Norden, der Süden.
    • Nouns in -er made from verbs, for people and machines: der Lehrer (from lehren, to teach), der Fernseher, der Computer.
    • The endings -ling, -ismus, -ist: der Frühling, der Tourismus, der Tourist.

    Feminine: die¶

    • Female people and animals: die Frau, die Mutter, die Tochter, die Tante, and the feminine forms in -in: die Lehrerin, die Studentin, die Ärztin.
    • Most nouns ending in -e: die Lampe, die Küche, die Tasche, die Straße, die Woche. This is a strong tendency, not a law (see the exceptions below), but it is right far more often than not.
    • The endings -ung, -heit, -keit, -schaft: die Wohnung, die Zeitung, die Freiheit, die Möglichkeit, die Freundschaft. These are completely reliable.
    • The endings -ion, -tät, -ik, -ie, -ei, -ur: die Information, die Universität, die Musik, die Familie, die Bäckerei, die Kultur. Most of them correspond to English words, which makes them easy to spot.

    Neuter: das¶

    • The endings -chen and -lein, which make "little" words, always neuter: das Mädchen (girl, originally "little maid"), das Brötchen (bread roll, "little bread"), das Häuschen (little house).
    • Young people and animals: das Kind, das Baby.
    • Infinitives used as nouns: das Essen (food, eating), das Leben (life), das Schwimmen (swimming).
    • The endings -um and -ment, in words from Latin: das Museum, das Zentrum, das Datum, das Dokument, das Instrument.
    • Many short words borrowed from other languages: das Hotel, das Kino, das Café, das Restaurant, das Auto, das Büro, das Handy, das Sofa.
    • Colours and languages used as nouns: das Rot, das Deutsch.

    The exceptions to learn now¶

    Word Why it is surprising
    das Mädchen a girl, but -chen is always neuter
    der Junge ends in -e, but a male person
    der Name ends in -e, but masculine
    der Käse ends in -e, but masculine
    das Ende ends in -e, but neuter
    das Auge ends in -e, but neuter (the eye)
    das Bier most alcoholic drinks are masculine (der Wein), but beer is neuter

    Table 4.5. Common exceptions.

    Das Mädchen: es or sie?

    Strictly, a pronoun follows the grammatical gender, so das Mädchen is es: Das Mädchen heißt Lea. Es ist sieben. In everyday speech, though, people often switch to sie when they go on talking about the girl, especially in a longer text. Both are heard; at this stage, use es straight after the noun.

    The pronouns er, sie, es for things¶

    Because every noun has a gender, "it" has three forms in German. A thing is referred to with the pronoun of its gender:

    • Wo ist der Schlüssel? – Er ist hier. — Where is the key? – It is here.
    • Wie ist die Küche? – Sie ist groß. — What is the kitchen like? – It is big.
    • Ist das Zimmer klein? – Ja, es ist klein. — Is the room small? – Yes, it is small.
    • Wo sind die Bücher? – Sie sind im Regal. — Where are the books? – They are on the shelf.

    The trick is that er looks like der, sie like die, and es like das: the last letter of the article is the pronoun's clue (der → er, das → es).

    Common mistake: es for everything

    English speakers tend to say es for every thing, because English says it. Der Tisch ist neu. Es ist schön. is wrong: the table is masculine, so Er ist schön. Always look back at the article.

    4.4 Grammar: plurals and compound nouns¶

    The plural article¶

    In the plural there is only one article for all three genders, die, and one pronoun, sie (they). The plural is easy to recognise but harder to form, because the noun itself changes in one of several ways.

    The plural patterns¶

    Pattern Singular Plural Typical for
    -e der Tisch, das Heft die Tische, die Hefte many short masculine and neuter nouns
    umlaut + -e der Stuhl, die Stadt die Stühle, die Städte many short masculine nouns, some feminine
    (umlaut +) -er das Kind, das Buch die Kinder, die Bücher many short neuter nouns; umlaut where possible
    -n / -en die Lampe, die Frau die Lampen, die Frauen almost all feminine nouns
    -nen die Lehrerin die Lehrerinnen feminine nouns in -in
    no ending der Lehrer, das Zimmer die Lehrer, die Zimmer masculine and neuter nouns in -er, -el, -en, -chen, -lein
    umlaut only der Vater, die Mutter die Väter, die Mütter a few common nouns, many of them family words
    -s das Auto, das Handy die Autos, die Handys words ending in a vowel other than -e; many borrowed words

    Table 4.6. The plural patterns. The bold letters show what changes.

    The column "Typical for" gives you rules of thumb that work most of the time:

    1. Feminine nouns almost all add -n or -en: die Lampen, die Wohnungen, die Türen, die Zeitungen. Learn the few exceptions as you meet them: die Städte, die Hände, die Nächte, die Mütter, die Töchter.
    2. Masculine and neuter nouns ending in -er, -el, -en, -chen, -lein add nothing, though some take an umlaut: die Fenster, die Schlüssel, die Mädchen; die Väter, die Äpfel, die Gärten.
    3. Short masculine nouns usually add -e, often with an umlaut: die Tage, die Freunde, die Stühle, die Schränke.
    4. Short neuter nouns add -e or -er: die Hefte, die Jahre; die Kinder, die Bilder, die Bücher, die Häuser.
    5. Words ending in -a, -i, -o, -u, -y and many recent borrowings add -s: die Sofas, die Kulis, die Kinos, die Handys, die Hotels.

    Even native speakers learnt the plurals one by one, as children. Take the same path: learn the plural with the noun, and these rules will make most of them feel natural.

    Common mistake: adding -s to everything

    English makes almost every plural with -s, so English speakers are tempted to do the same in German. Only a minority of German nouns take -s. die Tischs and die Lampes are wrong: die Tische, die Lampen.

    Nouns with no singular or no plural¶

    Some nouns are plural in German where English uses a singular, and the other way round:

    • die Brille is one pair of glasses, singular: Die Brille ist neu. So is die Hose, a pair of trousers.
    • die Eltern (the parents) and die Leute (the people) exist only in the plural.
    • die Information has a plural, die Informationen, used where English says information without a plural.

    Compound nouns¶

    German freely joins nouns together into one long word, the compound noun (das Kompositum). English does the same with bedroom or toothbrush, but usually writes the parts apart (front door, washing machine). Two rules make compounds easy:

    1. The last part says what the thing is, and gives the gender and the plural of the whole word. The parts before it describe it.
    2. The main stress falls on the first part.
    Parts Compound English
    das Haus + die Tür die Haustür the front door
    kühl + der Schrank der Kühlschrank the fridge
    schlafen + das Zimmer das Schlafzimmer the bedroom
    das Telefon + die Nummer die Telefonnummer the phone number
    die Wohnung + die Tür die Wohnungstür the door of the flat
    der Geburtstag + die Party die Geburtstagsparty the birthday party

    Table 4.7. Compound nouns. The article of the compound is the article of its last part.

    Sometimes a linking -s- or -n- joins the parts: Wohnung-s-tür, Geburtstag-s-party, Straße-n-bahn (tram). After -ung, -heit, -keit, -schaft and -ion there is always an -s-.

    Worked example: an unknown compound

    You see the word Kinderzimmerlampe. What is it, and what is its article and plural?

    1. Split it from the end: Kinder + Zimmer + Lampe.
    2. The last part says what it is: a lamp. Kinderzimmer (children's room) describes which lamp.
    3. The gender and plural come from die Lampe, plural die Lampen.

    So: die Kinderzimmerlampe, the lamp in the children's room; plural die Kinderzimmerlampen. The stress is on the first part: KIN-der-tsim-muh-lahm-puh.

    4.6 Culture: living in Germany, Austria and Switzerland¶

    Renting is normal. In the German-speaking countries, and in Switzerland and Germany above all, far more people rent their homes than in Britain or the United States. Renting a flat for many years, even for life, is common, and tenants are well protected by law. Flats are described by the number of rooms, not bedrooms: a Dreizimmerwohnung (three-room flat) has three rooms plus kitchen and bathroom, and its size is always given in square metres. The rent is quoted as Kaltmiete ("cold rent", the flat alone) or Warmmiete ("warm rent", including heating and other running costs).

    The kitchen may not be included. A surprise for many newcomers to Germany is that an unfurnished flat may come without a fitted kitchen: no cupboards, no cooker, sometimes not even a sink. Tenants often buy a kitchen and take it with them, or sell it to the next tenant, when they move.

    The WG. Students and young people often share a flat in a Wohngemeinschaft, usually shortened to WG [vay-GAY]. Each person has a room of their own and shares the kitchen and bathroom, as Emma and Jonas do. Advertisements for rooms ask for a Mitbewohner or Mitbewohnerin (flatmate), and new flatmates are often chosen at a casual interview over coffee, the WG-Casting.

    Rules of the house. Blocks of flats usually have a set of house rules (die Hausordnung), which may set quiet hours at night and on Sundays and say who cleans the stairs. Rubbish is sorted carefully into several bins: paper, packaging, glass by colour, organic waste and the rest. The colours of the bins and the exact rules vary from town to town, so newcomers ask their neighbours or read the local guide.

    4.7 Summary¶

    Summary

    • Three genders: der (masculine), die (feminine), das (neuter); plural die for all. Pronouns: er, sie, es; plural sie.
    • Masculine: male people, days, months, seasons, weather, -er from verbs, -ling, -ismus.
    • Feminine: female people, -in, most nouns in -e, -ung, -heit, -keit, -schaft, -ion, -tät, -ik, -ie.
    • Neuter: -chen, -lein, young beings, infinitives as nouns, -um, -ment, many borrowed words.
    • Exceptions: das Mädchen, der Junge, der Name, der Käse, das Auge, das Ende, das Bier.
    • Plurals: -e (Tische), ¨-e (Stühle), ¨-er (Bücher), -(e)n (Lampen, almost all feminines), -nen (Lehrerinnen), no ending (Zimmer), umlaut (Väter), -s (Autos).
    • Compounds: the last part gives the gender and plural; the first part takes the stress: die Haustür.
    • Learn every noun as article + noun + plural.
