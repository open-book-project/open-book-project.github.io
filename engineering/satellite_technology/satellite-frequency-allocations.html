    Satellite Frequency Allocations¶

    Radio-frequency spectrum is a finite, internationally shared natural resource governed by the International Telecommunication Union (ITU) Radio Regulations (RR). This appendix tabulates the primary frequency allocations used in satellite communications, navigation, and remote sensing, together with propagation characteristics, multiple-access efficiency metrics, and geostationary orbital slot assignments.

    Satellite Service Frequency Allocations¶

    The ITU RR allocates spectrum to over 40 radiocommunication services. For satellites the most important are the Fixed Satellite Service (FSS), the Broadcasting Satellite Service (BSS), the Mobile Satellite Service (MSS), the Radio Navigation Satellite Service (RNSS), and the Space Research Service (SRS).

    ITU Administrative Regions¶

    The ITU divides the world into three Regions for the purposes of differing spectrum allocations (ITU RR No. 5.1):

    Table 7. ITU Radio Regulation administrative regions
    Region Geographic coverage Key spectrum distinction
    Region 1 Europe, Africa, Middle East, Russia, BSS Ku: 11.7–12.5 GHz;
    Central Asia, Mongolia C-band to 6.725 GHz UL
    Region 2 North, Central and South America, BSS Ku: 12.2–12.7 GHz;
    Greenland, Pacific islands Widest C-band allocation
    Region 3 Asia-Pacific, Iran, Afghanistan, BSS Ku: 11.7–12.2 GHz;
    Australasia Shared C/Ku allocations

    A given GEO satellite serving multiple regions must comply with the most restrictive of the applicable regional constraints on EIRP flux density, off-axis emission masks, and coordination trigger levels.

    Atmospheric Windows¶

    Satellite-to-ground communication exploits transparent “windows” in the atmosphere. Major absorption features to avoid include the \(22.235\) GHz water-vapour line and the \(60\) GHz oxygen complex. The primary transparent windows for satellite service are:

    • \(1\)–\(10\) GHz (L to X): near-transparent; rain effects minor.

    • \(10\)–\(20\) GHz (Ku/K): moderate cloud and rain attenuation.

    • \(17\)–\(25\) GHz (K/Ka): increasing rain; water-vapour wing effects.

    • \(27\)–\(40\) GHz (Ka): significant rain fade; site diversity required.

    • \(75\)–\(110\) GHz (W): usable for short paths and space-only ISLs.

    Spectral Efficiency Formula¶

    The theoretical maximum spectral efficiency (Shannon capacity) for a single carrier on a satellite link is:

    \[\eta = \frac{C}{B} = \log_2\!\left(1 + \frac{C}{N}\right) \quad\text{(bit/s/Hz)}\tag{1}\]

    DVB-S2 reaches about 4.45 bit/s/Hz (32APSK, FEC 9/10) on additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) channels with a quasi-error-free \(E_s/N_0\) threshold of about \(+16\) dB, and DVB-S2X extends the range down to 0.43 bit/s/Hz (QPSK, FEC 11/45, \(E_s/N_0\) threshold about \(-2.85\) dB) and up to higher spectral efficiencies with the 64- to 256APSK modes.

    GEO Orbital Slot Assignments¶

    The geostationary arc is divided into orbital positions (slots). Satellites using the same frequencies are typically spaced about \(2^\circ\) apart (closer spacings are possible with coordination), and each satellite is held within a station-keeping box of typically \(\pm 0.05^\circ\). Positions are recorded by the ITU in its Master International Frequency Register (MIFR) and through inter-administration coordination under Radio Regulation Articles 9, 11, and Appendices 30, 30A, and 30B.

    ITU Frequency Coordination Process¶

    The ITU coordination process for GEO satellite networks follows these main stages (simplified):

    1. Advance Publication Information (API): Submitted 2–7 years before launch; published in ITU Special Section BR IFIC. Provides notice to other administrations.

    2. Coordination Request (CR): Submitted \(\ge\)2 years before the intended date of bringing into use; triggers bilateral coordination with all potentially affected administrations exceeding the coordination arc.

    3. Bilateral Coordination: Affected administrations exchange detailed technical data; interference analysis per the relevant ITU-R S-series recommendations. Resolved by agreement, frequency/orbit adjustment, or ITU arbitration.

    4. Notification: When coordination is complete, the frequency assignment is notified to the ITU BR for recording in the MIFR; must occur before the satellite begins operation.

    5. Bringing Into Use (BIU): The satellite must begin operation within 7 years of the API, with verifiable evidence of actual use submitted to the ITU BR within 30 days of first operation.

    6. Administrative Due Diligence (ITU RR Resolution 49): The notifying administration must submit information identifying the satellite manufacturer and launch arrangements for the network.

    Failure to bring a network into use within the regulatory period results in cancellation of its ITU frequency assignments; the detailed deadlines and milestones depend on the current edition of the Radio Regulations.
