    Preface¶

    The dawn of the Space Age in 1957 with the launch of Sputnik 1 marked the beginning of humanity’s most ambitious technological enterprise. Today, satellites form the invisible backbone of modern civilization—enabling global communications, precise navigation, continuous Earth observation, and scientific exploration that has fundamentally transformed our understanding of the cosmos and our own planet.

    This comprehensive monograph, Satellite Technology: From Fundamentals to Future Frontiers, represents a systematic compilation of satellite technology knowledge spanning from classical orbital mechanics to the cutting-edge developments of the 2020s. The explosive growth of commercial space—with mega-constellations comprising tens of thousands of satellites, miniaturized CubeSats enabling university-level access to space, and reusable launch vehicles slashing the cost of reaching orbit—demands a unified reference that bridges the gap between classical aerospace textbooks and the rapidly evolving landscape of modern space systems.

    Scope and Organization

    This book is organized into six major parts:

    • Part I: Fundamentals lays the mathematical and physical foundations: orbital mechanics, the space environment, celestial mechanics, and astrodynamics.

    • Part II: Satellite Systems and Subsystems provides a systematic treatment of all spacecraft subsystems, from power generation to attitude control, propulsion, thermal management, and on-board computing.

    • Part III: Satellite Communications covers the complete communications chain—from RF link budgets and antenna design to optical laser communications and digital signal processing.

    • Part IV: Satellite Applications examines the major application domains: Earth observation, navigation (GNSS), communication services, scientific missions, and military systems.

    • Part V: Ground Systems addresses the often-underappreciated ground segment, including station design, mission operations, and data processing pipelines.

    • Part VI: New Developments and Future Trends explores the frontier: SmallSats, mega-constellations, artificial intelligence, on-orbit servicing, space debris, quantum communications, 6G/NTN integration, and visionary future concepts.

    Audience

    This book is intended for graduate students in aerospace, electrical, and systems engineering; practicing satellite engineers seeking a comprehensive reference; researchers working on space systems; and technology managers and policy professionals who need to understand the technical foundations of satellite systems.

    Mathematical Notation

    Throughout this book, vectors are denoted by boldface lowercase letters (e.g., \(\mathbf{r}\), \(\mathbf{v}\)), matrices by boldface uppercase letters (e.g., \(\mathbf{A}\), \(\mathbf{R}\)), and scalars by italic letters. Unit vectors are indicated with a hat notation (e.g., \(\hat{\mathbf{r}}\)). The International System of Units (SI) is used throughout, with customary aerospace units noted where appropriate.

    The text is a synthesis of the public literature listed in the references. Statements about missions, constellations, companies and standards describe the situation as of 2025 and may be out of date.
