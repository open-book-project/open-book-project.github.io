    Glossary¶

    Terms used in this book, with the chapter where each is explained

    A–B¶

    Absolute majority
    More than half of the total membership of a House, counting vacant seats. Part of the special majority under Article 368. (Chapter 6)
    Adjournment
    Suspension of a sitting of a House for a stated time, by the presiding officer. Adjournment sine die ends a sitting without fixing a date for the next. (Chapter 6)
    Adjournment motion
    A motion in the Lok Sabha to set aside normal business and discuss a definite matter of urgent public importance; needs the support of 50 members. (Chapter 6)
    Advisory jurisdiction
    The power of the Supreme Court under Article 143 to give its opinion on a question referred by the President. (Chapter 7)
    All-India Services
    Services common to the Union and the States (the IAS, IPS and Indian Forest Service), recruited by the Union and serving in the States. (Chapter 8)
    Amendment
    A change in the text of the Constitution made under Article 368 by addition, variation or repeal. (Chapter 11)
    Appropriation Bill
    The bill that authorises withdrawal from the Consolidated Fund of the sums voted by the Lok Sabha and charged on the Fund (Article 114). (Chapter 6)
    Basic structure
    The essential features of the Constitution that Parliament cannot destroy by amendment, according to Kesavananda Bharati (1973). (Chapter 11)
    Bicameral
    Having two Houses. Parliament is bicameral; some State legislatures are bicameral. (Chapters 6, 9)

    C¶

    Cabinet
    The inner group of senior ministers (Cabinet Ministers) within the Council of Ministers, headed by the Prime Minister. (Chapter 5)
    Casting vote
    The vote of a presiding officer used only to break a tie. (Chapter 6)
    Certiorari
    A writ by which a higher court quashes an order of a lower court or tribunal made without or in excess of jurisdiction, or with an error of law. (Chapter 3)
    Charged expenditure
    Expenditure charged on the Consolidated Fund of India by the Constitution or by law, which is not put to the vote of Parliament (Article 112(3)). (Chapter 6)
    Citizenship
    Full membership of the State, with civil and political rights. India has a single citizenship. (Chapter 2)
    Collective responsibility
    The principle that the Council of Ministers stands or falls together and is answerable as a body to the Lok Sabha (Article 75(3)). (Chapter 5)
    Collegium
    The body of the Chief Justice of India and the senior-most judges of the Supreme Court that recommends appointments of judges. (Chapter 7)
    Comptroller and Auditor-General (CAG)
    The constitutional authority that audits the accounts of the Union and the States (Articles 148 to 151). (Chapter 12)
    Concurrent List
    List III of the Seventh Schedule; subjects on which both Parliament and the State legislatures may make laws. (Chapter 8)
    Consolidated Fund of India
    The fund into which all revenues, loans raised and loan repayments received by the Union are credited, and from which money is spent only under appropriation by law (Article 266). (Chapter 6)
    Constituent Assembly
    The body that framed the Constitution between December 1946 and November 1949. (Chapter 1)
    Constitutional morality
    A habit of respect for the forms and limits of the Constitution; in later usage, action according to constitutional values. (Chapter 14)
    Contingency Fund of India
    A fund at the disposal of the President for unforeseen expenditure pending authorisation by Parliament (Article 267). (Chapter 6)
    Convention
    An unwritten rule of political practice regarded as binding by those concerned. (Chapter 14)
    Cut motion
    A motion to reduce a demand for grant during the budget: a policy cut, an economy cut or a token cut. (Chapter 6)

    D–E¶

    Delimitation
    The drawing of constituency boundaries, carried out by a Delimitation Commission whose orders cannot be challenged in court. (Chapter 13)
    Directive Principles of State Policy
    The goals for the State in Part IV (Articles 36 to 51), not enforceable by courts but fundamental in governance. (Chapter 4)
    Dissolution
    The ending of the life of the Lok Sabha or a State Legislative Assembly, followed by a general election. (Chapter 6)
    Doctrine of eclipse
    The rule that a pre-Constitution law inconsistent with a Fundamental Right is overshadowed rather than dead, and revives if the conflict is removed. (Chapter 3)
    Effective majority
    More than half of the then members of a House (total membership minus vacancies). (Chapter 6)
    Election petition
    The only means of challenging an election to Parliament or a State legislature, filed in the High Court (Article 329). (Chapter 13)
    Electoral college
    The body of electors that elects the President (Article 54) or the Vice-President (Article 66). (Chapter 5)
    Emergency
    A situation in which the President may exercise extraordinary powers under Part XVIII: national (Article 352), in a State (Article 356) or financial (Article 360). (Chapter 10)

    F–G¶

    Federalism
    A system in which power is divided between a central government and regional governments by a written constitution. (Chapter 8)
    Finance Commission
    The body constituted every five years under Article 280 to recommend the sharing of Union taxes with the States and grants-in-aid. (Chapters 8, 12)
    Financial bill
    A bill dealing with fiscal matters that does not meet the strict definition of a money bill (Article 117). (Chapter 6)
    First past the post
    An electoral system in which the candidate with the most votes in a single-member constituency wins. (Chapter 13)
    Fundamental Duties
    The duties of citizens listed in Article 51A (Part IVA), added in 1976 and 2002. (Chapter 4)
    Fundamental Rights
    The rights guaranteed in Part III (Articles 12 to 35), enforceable in the courts. (Chapter 3)
    Gram Sabha
    The body of all registered voters of a village (Article 243A). (Chapter 9)
    GST Council
    The body of the Union and State finance ministers that recommends rates and rules for the Goods and Services Tax (Article 279A). (Chapter 8)

    H–L¶

    Habeas corpus
    A writ ordering a person who has detained another to produce the detained person before the court, so that the lawfulness of the detention can be examined. (Chapter 3)
    Impeachment
    The quasi-judicial procedure for removing the President for violation of the Constitution (Article 61). (Chapter 5)
    Joint sitting
    A sitting of both Houses of Parliament together, called by the President to resolve a deadlock on an ordinary bill (Article 108). (Chapter 6)
    Judicial review
    The power of the courts to examine whether laws, executive actions and amendments are consistent with the Constitution, and to declare them void if not. (Chapter 7)
    Locus standi
    The right to bring a case to court; relaxed for public interest litigation. (Chapter 3)
    Lok Adalat
    A forum for the amicable settlement of disputes under the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987, whose awards are final. (Chapter 7)

    M–O¶

    Mandamus
    A writ commanding a public authority to perform a public duty it has failed to perform. (Chapter 3)
    Model Code of Conduct
    Guidelines for parties, candidates and governments during elections, enforced by the Election Commission. (Chapter 13)
    Money bill
    A bill containing only the matters listed in Article 110, such as taxation and appropriation; it originates in the Lok Sabha, and the Rajya Sabha can only make recommendations. (Chapter 6)
    No-confidence motion
    A motion in the Lok Sabha expressing lack of confidence in the Council of Ministers; if adopted, the Council must resign. (Chapters 5, 6)
    NOTA
    "None of the Above", an option on voting machines that lets a voter reject all candidates. (Chapter 13)
    Ordinance
    A temporary law made by the President (Article 123) or a Governor (Article 213) when the legislature is not in session. (Chapters 5, 9)
    Overseas Citizen of India (OCI)
    A status for foreign citizens of Indian origin, giving a lifelong visa and certain rights; not citizenship. (Chapter 2)

    P–Q¶

    Panchayati Raj
    The system of rural local self-government given constitutional status by the 73rd Amendment (1992). (Chapter 9)
    Pith and substance
    The doctrine by which the courts decide which list a law falls under by looking at its true nature, not its incidental effects. (Chapter 8)
    Pocket veto
    The President's power to keep a bill pending indefinitely, since Article 111 fixes no time limit. (Chapter 5)
    Preamble
    The introduction to the Constitution, stating its source of authority, the nature of the State, its objectives and the date of adoption. (Chapter 2)
    Preventive detention
    Detention without trial to prevent a person from acting against security or public order, permitted by Article 22 with safeguards. (Chapter 3)
    Prohibition
    A writ by which a higher court forbids a lower court or tribunal from exceeding its jurisdiction in a pending case. (Chapter 3)
    Prorogation
    The ending of a session of a House by the President; pending bills do not lapse. (Chapter 6)
    Public interest litigation (PIL)
    Litigation brought in the public interest, often on behalf of those unable to approach the court themselves. (Chapters 3, 7)
    Quo warranto
    A writ by which a court inquires into the authority of a person holding a public office. (Chapter 3)
    Quorum
    The minimum number of members needed for a sitting; one-tenth of the total membership for each House of Parliament. (Chapter 6)

    R–S¶

    Reasonable classification
    A classification permitted under Article 14, based on an intelligible differentia with a rational nexus to the object of the law. (Chapter 3)
    Repugnancy
    Conflict between a Union law and a State law on a Concurrent List subject; the Union law prevails unless Article 254(2) applies. (Chapter 8)
    Republic
    A State whose head is elected rather than hereditary. (Chapter 2)
    Residuary powers
    The power to legislate on subjects not in any of the three lists; it belongs to Parliament (Article 248). (Chapter 8)
    Rule of law
    The principle that no one is above the law and that government acts only according to law. (Chapter 3)
    Schedule
    A part at the end of the Constitution containing lists and details; there are twelve. (Chapter 1)
    Secularism
    In the Indian sense, a State with no religion of its own that treats all religions equally and guarantees freedom of religion. (Chapter 2)
    Separation of powers
    The division of legislative, executive and judicial functions among separate organs; applied in India in a modified form. (Chapter 14)
    Simple majority
    More than half of the members present and voting. (Chapter 6)
    Single transferable vote
    A preferential voting system used for indirect elections in India, in which votes are transferred according to voters' rankings. (Chapters 5, 13)
    Sovereign
    Free from any external authority in internal and external affairs. (Chapter 2)
    Speaker
    The presiding officer of the Lok Sabha (or of a State Legislative Assembly), elected by its members. (Chapter 6)
    Special majority
    Under Article 368, a majority of the total membership of a House and two-thirds of the members present and voting. Under Article 249, two-thirds of members present and voting. (Chapters 6, 11)
    Suspensive veto
    The President's power to return a bill (other than a money bill) for reconsideration once. (Chapter 5)

    T–Z¶

    Tenth Schedule
    The anti-defection law, added by the 52nd Amendment (1985). (Chapter 6)
    Union Territory
    A territory administered by the President through an administrator (Article 239); some have legislatures. (Chapter 9)
    Universal adult franchise
    The right of every adult citizen to vote, without qualification of property, education, sex, religion or caste (Article 326). (Chapters 1, 13)
    Vote on account
    An advance grant by the Lok Sabha for part of the financial year, pending the passing of the full budget (Article 116). (Chapter 6)
    Writ
    A formal order of a court; the Supreme Court and High Courts issue writs of habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, certiorari and quo warranto. (Chapter 3)
    Zero Hour
    An informal period after Question Hour in which members raise urgent matters without notice. (Chapter 6)
