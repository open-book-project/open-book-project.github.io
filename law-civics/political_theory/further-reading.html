    Further Reading¶

    Primary texts, textbooks and standard works for going further

    Classic texts¶

    Most of these are available in many editions and translations; the older ones are out of copyright and can be read free of charge.

    • Plato, Republic. The founding work of Western political philosophy, on justice and the ideal city.
    • Aristotle, Politics. On the nature of the political community, citizenship and the classification of constitutions.
    • Kauṭilya, Arthaśāstra. Translations by R. Shamasastry (1915) and, more recently, by Patrick Olivelle, King, Governance, and Law in Ancient India (Oxford University Press, 2013).
    • Niccolò Machiavelli, The Prince.
    • Thomas Hobbes, Leviathan (1651).
    • John Locke, Second Treatise of Government (1689)* and A Letter Concerning Toleration (1689).*
    • Jean-Jacques Rousseau, The Social Contract (1762).
    • Edmund Burke, Reflections on the Revolution in France (1790).
    • Mary Wollstonecraft, A Vindication of the Rights of Woman (1792).
    • Alexis de Tocqueville, Democracy in America (1835, 1840).
    • Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels, The Communist Manifesto (1848).
    • John Stuart Mill, On Liberty (1859), Considerations on Representative Government (1861)* and The Subjection of Women (1869).*
    • M. K. Gandhi, Hind Swaraj (1909). A good edition is Hind Swaraj and Other Writings, edited by Anthony J. Parel (Cambridge University Press).
    • Rabindranath Tagore, Nationalism (1917).
    • B. R. Ambedkar, Annihilation of Caste (1936), and his speeches in the Constituent Assembly Debates (especially 4 November 1948 and 25 November 1949), published by the Lok Sabha Secretariat.
    • Jawaharlal Nehru, The Discovery of India (1946).

    Twentieth-century works discussed in this book¶

    • Isaiah Berlin, "Two Concepts of Liberty" (1958), reprinted in Liberty, edited by Henry Hardy (Oxford University Press).
    • John Rawls, A Theory of Justice (Harvard University Press, 1971; revised edition 1999); Political Liberalism (1993); The Law of Peoples (1999); Justice as Fairness: A Restatement (2001).
    • Robert Nozick, Anarchy, State, and Utopia (Basic Books, 1974).
    • Amartya Sen, Development as Freedom (Oxford University Press, 1999)* and The Idea of Justice (Allen Lane / Harvard University Press, 2009).*
    • Martha C. Nussbaum, Creating Capabilities: The Human Development Approach (Harvard University Press, 2011).
    • Steven Lukes, Power: A Radical View (Macmillan, 1974; 2nd edition 2005).
    • Robert A. Dahl, On Democracy (Yale University Press, 1998).
    • Will Kymlicka, Multicultural Citizenship (Oxford University Press, 1995).
    • Benedict Anderson, Imagined Communities (Verso, 1983).
    • Susan Moller Okin, Justice, Gender, and the Family (Basic Books, 1989).
    • Rajeev Bhargava (ed.), Secularism and Its Critics (Oxford University Press, 1998).

    Textbooks and introductions¶

    • Andrew Heywood, Political Theory: An Introduction and Political Ideologies: An Introduction (Bloomsbury / Palgrave, several editions). Clear, widely used introductions.
    • Will Kymlicka, Contemporary Political Philosophy: An Introduction (Oxford University Press, 2nd edition 2002). The standard guide to utilitarianism, liberal equality, libertarianism, Marxism, communitarianism, citizenship theory, multiculturalism and feminism.
    • Jonathan Wolff, An Introduction to Political Philosophy (Oxford University Press, several editions). A short, lively introduction organised by questions.
    • Adam Swift, Political Philosophy: A Beginners' Guide for Students and Politicians (Polity, several editions).
    • David Miller, Political Philosophy: A Very Short Introduction (Oxford University Press, 2003).
    • Rajeev Bhargava and Ashok Acharya (eds), Political Theory: An Introduction (Pearson Longman, 2008). Essays on the main concepts, written with Indian readers in mind.
    • NCERT, Political Theory (textbook in political science for Class XI). An accessible introduction to the core concepts.
    • David Held, Models of Democracy (Polity, several editions).

    Indian political thought¶

    • V. R. Mehta, Foundations of Indian Political Thought (Manohar, 1992).
    • Thomas Pantham and Kenneth L. Deutsch (eds), Political Thought in Modern India (Sage, 1986).
    • Bhikhu Parekh, Gandhi: A Very Short Introduction (Oxford University Press, 2001).
    • Valerian Rodrigues (ed.), The Essential Writings of B. R. Ambedkar (Oxford University Press, 2002).

    Global justice and human rights¶

    • The Universal Declaration of Human Rights and the two International Covenants, published by the United Nations Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights.
    • Peter Singer, "Famine, Affluence, and Morality", Philosophy and Public Affairs 1 (1972).
    • Thomas Pogge, World Poverty and Human Rights (Polity, 2002; 2nd edition 2008).
    • Gillian Brock, Global Justice: A Cosmopolitan Account (Oxford University Press, 2009).
    • Jack Donnelly, Universal Human Rights in Theory and Practice (Cornell University Press, several editions).

    Reference works¶

    • The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, a free online encyclopedia with authoritative articles on most of the concepts and thinkers in this book (liberty, equality, justice, rights, democracy, citizenship, nationalism, secularism, feminism, global justice, and many more).
    • Robert E. Goodin, Philip Pettit and Thomas Pogge (eds), A Companion to Contemporary Political Philosophy (Blackwell, 2nd edition 2007).
